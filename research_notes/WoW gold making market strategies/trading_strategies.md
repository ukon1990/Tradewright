# Auction-house trading strategies (buy and resell) in World of Warcraft, as of November 2026

Reader notes (apply to every section):

- Source tiers are tagged after each link: [peer-reviewed], [working paper], [official], [primary data], [reference wiki], [tool docs], [community].
- "Convention" means a rule of thumb that goldmakers recommend; none of the numeric buy/sell thresholds in these notes is backed by a peer-reviewed or official source. Claims resting only on [community] sources are marked "unverified community claim".
- Every link listed under "Cited Findings" was opened and checked during this research unless it is explicitly marked "NOT OPENED". Sources I could not open are listed in the final Gaps section and are not used as sole support for anything.
- Game flavors: "Retail" = Midnight 12.x (modern AH, region-wide commodities). "Classic-style" = Classic Era / Anniversary / progression Classic (bid + buyout, stacks, per realm and faction). "Forever" = WoW Forever (launched November 2026, megaserver, hybrid AH); Forever mechanics below rest on community reporting only, because Blizzard has not published the fee formula.
- Shared fee arithmetic used throughout (derived, not quoted): with a 5% cut, break-even resale price = buy price / 0.95 (about 1.053 x buy); with a 15% neutral cut, buy price / 0.85 (about 1.176 x buy).

## 1. Flipping: how goldmakers pick items (price vs market value, sale rate, minimum profit after the 5% cut and deposit)

### Takeaway
Flipping is buying a listing below a reference "market value" and reposting at about market value; the only verified hard numbers are the fee mechanics (5% cut, deposit as a share of vendor price) and the definitions of the TSM reference prices. The buy thresholds (80% of dbmarket for materials, 60% for BoE gear, 15-30% target margin) are conventions from a small number of community authors, not measured optima.

### Cited Findings

Fee and reference-price mechanics (verified)
- Retail: the house takes 5% of the winning bid; the seller receives bid x 0.95 plus the returned deposit; the deposit is 15% / 30% / 60% of the item's vendor sell price for 12 / 24 / 48 hours, with a 1 silver minimum; the deposit is lost if the auction fails or is cancelled. — [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki] (page carries an "outdated" notice and describes retail only)
- TSM `DBMarket` is "a weighted 14-day average of the auctions listed" on the realm, favoring the most recent 3 days; `DBMinBuyout` is the lowest-priced auction as of the last pricing update (about every 30 minutes); `DBHistorical` is a 60-day average of DBMarket; `DBRecent` uses only the latest snapshot; `DBRegionMarketAvg` is the average of DBMarket across the region; `DBRegionSaleAvg` is the average sale and purchase price in the region (from TSM Accounting data uploaded via the desktop app). — [TSM: Which Price Sources can I use](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean) [tool docs]
- TSM `DBRegionSaleRate` is a decimal: 0.4 means the item sells about 40% of the times it is posted, averaged per auction house across the region. `DBRegionSoldPerDay` is the average volume sold per auction house per day in the region. `SaleRate` and `NumExpires` are the player's own history (share of own posts that sold; expirations/cancels since last sale). — [TSM: Which Value Sources can I use](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/custom-strings/which-value-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean) [tool docs]
- TSM's default Shopping operation has a maximum auction price of `dbmarket` (auctions at or below market value are returned) and no restock limit. — [TSM: How do the Default Operations work](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/addon/how-do-the-default-operations-work) [tool docs]
- TSM Shopping operations expose "Maximum auction price" (accepts custom strings), "Show auctions above max price" (on by default) and "Maximum restock quantity" with selectable inventory sources. — [TSM Addon: Shopping Operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/tsm-addon-shopping-operations) [tool docs]
- Saddlebag Exchange computes "Current Market Value" = sales per day x current minimum price and "Historic Market Value" = sales per day x TSM average price, and classes an item by percent change of current price against the TSM average: Spiking above +60%, Increasing +15% to +60%, Stable -15% to +15%, Decreasing -15% to -60%, Crashing below -60%. — [Saddlebag Exchange: How to trade using our WoW market overview](https://saddlebagexchange.com/blog/wow/how-to-trade-using-our-wow-market-overview) [tool docs]
- Saddlebag's item-picking advice for flips: filter for a higher average sale rate and a higher average sale price; for realm-only (non-commodity) items lower the sale-rate filter and raise the minimum price, because they sell slowly but have far less competition. No numeric cutoffs are given. — [Saddlebag Exchange: market overview guide](https://saddlebagexchange.com/blog/wow/how-to-trade-using-our-wow-market-overview) [tool docs]

Thresholds (conventions; unverified community claims)
- Convention (The Lazy Goldmaker, Wrath Classic, Feb 2023): buy trade materials at 80% of dbmarket or lower, sell at about 100% of market value ("go wide" over many materials), and skip any material whose market value is under 5x its vendor sell price because deposits eat the margin. Expect to reinvest most sales for a week to a month before gold grows. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping is still the king of entry level goldmaking in classic](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-the-king-of-entry-level-goldmaking-in-classic) [community]
- Convention (The Lazy Goldmaker, Classic, Dec 2024): same 80%-of-market buy rule and "5x vendor sell price materials only"; the mantra is "Buy low, sell normal"; favorites are herbs and enchanting materials (low vendor price means low deposit). — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping is still an absolute blast on the classic AH](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-an-absolute-blast-on-the-classic-ah-the-easiest-gold-you-will-ever-make) [community]
- Convention (The Lazy Goldmaker, Wrath Classic, Aug 2023): for non-crafted BoE gear buy at 60% of market value, start strict, and exclude raid BoEs because informed sellers rarely underprice them. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Low effort gold flipping BoEs in Wrath Classic](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/low-effort-gold-flipping-boes-in-wrath-classic) [community]
- Convention (TimeSaver, Classic Era, 2026): target a 15-30% margin; break-even is "buy price / (1 - cut) + expected deposit loss from re-lists"; a 50g buy at a 5% cut breaks even at about 52.6g and should be listed at about 58-65g. — [TimeSaver: WoW Classic Era auction house flipping](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-classic-era-auction-house-flipping-gold) [community]
- Unverified community claim: battle-pet flipping across 11 realms returned about 16 million gold for about 134 hours of reposting since Shadowlands launch ("at least" about 120k gold/hour), logging in every 3-14 days. Self-reported by one author; settings are paywalled. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Make 120k+ gold per hour flipping battle pets](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/make-120k-gold-per-hour-flipping-battle-pets) [community]

Academic grounding
- Morrison and Fontenla (2013), "Price convergence in an online virtual world", Empirical Economics 44(3):1053-1064, DOI 10.1007/s00181-012-0567-3: using WoW data from eight structurally identical servers, the Phillips-Sul nonlinear test and a band-of-inaction test both find price convergence in all eight worlds. — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:spr:empeco:v:44:y:2013:i:3:p:1053-1064) [peer-reviewed] (abstract only read)
- Kosminsky (2009), "World of Warcraft: The Viability of Massively Multiplayer Online Role-Playing Games as Platforms for Modeling and Evaluating Perfect Competition", Journal of Virtual Worlds Research 2(4), DOI 10.4101/jvwr.v2i4.869: 30 days of auction prices across servers; predictions tested include "larger populations yield greater price stability" and only minor cross-server arbitrage; concludes the economy "in most respects behaves like a highly competitive real world market". — [JVWR article page](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/index.php/jvwr/article/view/869) [peer-reviewed] (abstract only read)
- Einav, Kuchler, Levin and Sundaresan (2015), "Assessing Sale Strategies in Online Markets Using Matched Listings", American Economic Journal: Microeconomics 7(2):215-247, DOI 10.1257/mic.20130046: uses hundreds of thousands of matched eBay listings to "measure the dispersion in auction prices for identical goods sold by the same seller". The abstract page does not give the dispersion magnitude. — [AEA article page](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/mic.20130046) [peer-reviewed] (abstract only read)

### Inferences
- A detection rule consistent with the sources: candidate if `minBuyout <= k x marketValue` (k = 0.8 materials, 0.6 gear, by convention) AND `marketValue >= 5 x vendorSell` AND liquidity is acceptable (sale rate or sold-per-day above a user threshold) AND `marketValue x 0.95 - buyPrice - expectedDepositLoss >= minProfit`.
- Arithmetic on the conventions: buying at 80% and selling at 100% of market value nets 0.95 - 0.80 = 0.15 of market value, an 18.75% return on cost before deposit losses; buying at 60% nets 0.35 of market value, a 58% return on cost. The 80% rule therefore leaves only a 15-point cushion for market-value error.
- Expected deposit loss can be modelled as `deposit x (1/saleRate - 1)` (expected failed listings before a sale if each posting sells with probability saleRate). This is my modelling assumption, not from a source.
- The academic results (price convergence, competitive behavior, larger servers more stable) imply flipping margins should be thinner and shorter-lived on large or region-wide markets and wider on thin, realm-only markets, which matches Saddlebag's realm-only advice.
- `DBMarket` is a listing-price average, not a sale-price average; an item nobody buys can still have a high DBMarket. Rules should prefer sale-based data (`DBRegionSaleAvg`, sold per day) or the tool's own observed sales where available.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed, official or tool-documentation source gives a recommended buy threshold or a minimum region sale rate for flipping. Thresholds such as "70% of DBRegionMarketAvg / 65% of DBMarket" (r/woweconomy beginner's flipping guide) and "10% minimum profit if region sale rate >= 0.2, otherwise 20%" (a pastebin TSM string) appeared only in search-result summaries; both pages failed to open (HTTP 502 / connection refused), so they are NOT verified and are reported here only as leads.
- I found no measured distribution of realized flipping margins from a dataset; all margin figures are self-reported.
- `DBRegionSaleRate` is defined per posting, so its value depends on how often sellers cancel and repost; TSM's docs do not give the formula.

## 2. Sniping: what it is, how sniper tools work, what gets sniped

### Takeaway
Sniping is buying a newly posted, badly underpriced listing before anyone else; the tools simply re-run a filtered search and alert, and every purchase still needs a click. In Classic-style houses the sniper watches the last page (newest listings), while in retail since patch 8.3 it re-runs a shopping search with a 30-second delay, which is why goldmakers narrow it to small item groups.

### Cited Findings
- TSM Sniper "allows for 'sniping' items from the Auction House as quickly as possible". In retail a Sniper operation is a Shopping operation whose search "is repeated until stopped", with a 30-second delay between searches; in Classic it repeatedly searches only the last page of the auction house, where newly posted items appear. The only pricing setting is a maximum price custom string. The docs say Sniper does not run on the Base Group in retail but does in Classic. — [TSM Addon: Sniper Operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/tsm-addon-sniper-operations) [tool docs]
- Patch 8.3 removed auction house "pages" and any "most recent" sort, so the only way to replicate sniping in retail is to repeatedly search for the items; every search returns all matching listings, which makes it slower. TSM recommends a smaller, focused group and removing the default Sniper operation from the Base Group. — [TSM news: Sniping with TSM in 8.3 (24 Apr 2020)](https://tradeskillmaster.com/news/sniping-with-tsm-in-8-3-628) [tool docs]
- TSM4 shipped two modes: Buyout Sniper (scans the most recently posted auctions and compares buyout with the configured max price) and Bid Sniper (looks for auctions about to expire that have a bid price below the max price). The single operation setting is "Below custom price". — [TSM news: TSM4 Deep Dive: Sniper](https://tradeskillmaster.com/news/tsm4-deep-dive-sniper-136) [tool docs]
- Placing a bid or buyout from an addon requires a hardware event (key or mouse input): `C_AuctionHouse.PlaceBid(auctionID, bidAmount)` is tagged `#hwevent` and `#noscript`, and patch 9.1.5 made it protected when called from a macro script. — [Warcraft Wiki: API C_AuctionHouse.PlaceBid](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.PlaceBid) [reference wiki]
- Saddlebag Exchange offers API-driven snipers and alerts outside the game (Azeroth Auction Assassin, a sniper addon, a Discord price-alert bot) that find cross-realm deals shortly after Blizzard's AH API data updates; buying still happens in game. — [Saddlebag Exchange: TL;DR guide](https://saddlebagexchange.com/blog/wow/tldr) [tool docs]; [Saddlebag Exchange: cross-realm announcement](https://saddlebagexchange.com/blog/wow/crossrealm1) [tool docs]
- Convention (The Lazy Goldmaker, TSM4 guide, 2018): a tiered sniper max price as a share of a conservative "minprice" (the minimum of several historical and region averages, floored at vendor sell): 80% of minprice for items valued above 250k, 70% above 100k, 60% above 50k, 50% above 10k, 30% above 5k, 10% above 1k, nothing below (value tiers as summarized from the guide's string; unit presumably gold, 2018 retail price levels); materials use a flat 80% of dbmarket. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: TSM4 Guide Part 10: Finding the best deals with Sniper](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/tsm4-guide-part-10-finding-the-best-deals-with-sniper) [community]
- Unverified community claim: sniper "traps" exist; some sellers raise an item's apparent value over time and then post it "cheaper but still expensive"; on high-population realms other snipers win the race, and results are inconsistent. The author advises checking the tooltip of any unfamiliar item before buying. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: TSM4 Guide Part 10](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/tsm4-guide-part-10-finding-the-best-deals-with-sniper) [community]
- Unverified community claim: a classic snipe target is a listing with a pricing mistake such as a forgotten zero, bought before the seller cancels. — [Warcraft Tavern: WoW Classic Gold and Trading Guide](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/wow-classic/guides/gold-and-trading-guide/) [community]
- WoW Forever (community reporting): an "Item was not found" error means another buyer won the auction first; Auctionator has a Forever build, and posting, buying and cancelling still require manual clicks. — [ConquestCapped: WoW Forever auction house guide](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community] (publisher sells gold and boosting services; treat as low reliability)
- Academic grounding: Roth and Ockenfels (2002), "Last-Minute Bidding and the Rules for Ending Second-Price Auctions: Evidence from eBay and Amazon Auctions on the Internet", American Economic Review 92(4):1093-1103 (NBER Working Paper 7729, DOI 10.3386/w7729): much more late bidding ("sniping") on eBay, which has a fixed end time, than on Amazon, whose auctions extend until ten minutes pass without a bid; the ending rule drives the behavior. — [NBER w7729](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7729) [working paper; published version peer-reviewed]

### Inferences
- A scan-data rule for sniping is the flipping rule with a much stricter k (for example 10-80% of a conservative reference, by price tier) and without a liquidity requirement on cheap items; the reference should be the minimum of several averages so that one manipulated average cannot trigger a buy.
- Missing-zero detection can be expressed as "buyout is roughly 1/10 or 1/100 of the next-cheapest listing and of market value"; this is my formulation, not a sourced rule.
- Because an addon scan in retail is at best 30 seconds stale and API-based data is far older, a rule should present snipes as time-critical and expect a high miss rate.
- The Roth-Ockenfels result explains why bid sniping matters only where the auction has a hard close; see section 8 for WoW's extension rule.

### Gaps
- The retirement of TSM's Bid Sniper in retail 8.3 ("no longer possible to bid on commodities or sort them by duration") comes from a TSM blog post that I could not open (the blog now redirects to a news index); it appeared only in a search summary. NOT verified.
- TSM's default Sniper max-price string is not documented ("coming soon" on the support page).
- A community reply claims Sniper can run on the Base Group since TSM 4.14, contradicting the support page; not verified.
- No data on snipe frequency or realized profit was found.

## 3. Resetting a market: buying out a thin order book to relist higher

### Takeaway
A reset buys every listing (full reset) or everything below a price gap (partial reset) and relists higher; experienced goldmakers treat it as high risk and judge it on cost to clear, how fast new supply can appear, and the real sale volume at the target price. The best documented case, a 1.3 million gold reset of a region-wide commodity, ended in a 300k loss because volume was overestimated.

### Cited Findings
- Definitions: full reset = buy out all auctions of an item and repost higher; partial reset = buy everything up to a price threshold (a visible gap in the listing ladder) and repost above it. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Advanced gold making tactics: Resetting markets (Dec 2017)](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/advanced-gold-making-tactics-resetting-markets) [community]
- Convention (same author) for candidate selection: limited supply, cannot be farmed effectively, you control the input materials for crafted items, high demand; smaller realms are better because fewer buyouts are needed and fewer sellers can undercut. For full resets look for few items currently posted; for partial resets look for a marked gap in pricing. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Resetting markets](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/advanced-gold-making-tactics-resetting-markets) [community]
- Convention (same author): have "at least 1 million gold" and stable income before attempting resets; for farmable (Legion-era) materials do not expect to sell much above 110% of dbmarket; an example scan showing 85k potential profit was judged not worth it because the total cost was well above that. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Resetting markets](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/advanced-gold-making-tactics-resetting-markets) [community]
- TSM has (or had, as of 2017) a reset scan under the Auctioning operation with these limits: maximum quantity to buy, maximum inventory quantity, maximum total cost, maximum cost per item, minimum profit per item; results show total cost and target price per buyout depth. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Resetting markets](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/advanced-gold-making-tactics-resetting-markets) [community] (not confirmed in current TSM docs)
- Case study (retail, region-wide EU commodity, Mar 2023): about 1.3 million gold spent on about 1,600 quality-3 Chronocloth bolts to raise the price about 50% to a 1,200 gold target; the author held the cheapest listings but 3 hours of active reposting sold only about 200-300 bolts; the price of the key input fell, making crafting viable again; 500 remaining bolts were mis-posted at 5 gold; final result a net loss of 300,000 gold. Stated lesson: estimate the realistic sale rate first and check substitutes. The stake was about 2% of the author's roughly 60 million gold. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: I spent 1.3 million gold resetting the Chronocloth market](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/i-spent-1-3-million-gold-resetting-the-chronocloth-market) [community]
- Unverified community claim (Classic Era): a 5,000g+ bankroll "can dominate a consumable market but risks a crash if a rival undercuts". — [TimeSaver: Classic Era flipping](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-classic-era-auction-house-flipping-gold) [community]
- Saddlebag Exchange notes that low-quantity commodities "may spike when stock runs out" and points to its Shortage Predictor / Commodity Shortage tools to see whether quantity is still falling; it tracks current quantity against average quantity ("Quantity Percent Remaining"). — [Saddlebag Exchange: market overview guide](https://saddlebagexchange.com/blog/wow/how-to-trade-using-our-wow-market-overview) [tool docs]
- Academic grounding: Shleifer and Vishny (1997), "The Limits of Arbitrage", Journal of Finance 52(1):35-55 (NBER Working Paper 5167): arbitrage is done by a few specialized, capital-constrained traders and becomes ineffective when prices move far from fundamentals. — [NBER w5167](https://www.nber.org/papers/w5167) [working paper; published version peer-reviewed]
- Academic grounding (NOT OPENED; citation from search metadata only): Allen, Litov and Mei (2006), "Large Investors, Price Manipulation, and Limits to Arbitrage: An Anatomy of Market Corners", Review of Finance 10(4):645-693, DOI 10.1007/s10679-006-9008-5, studies stock and commodity corners 1863-1980. — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:revfin:v:10:y:2006:i:4:p:645-693) [peer-reviewed] (flag: page not opened; do not rely on specific findings)

### Inferences
- A scoring rule that follows the sources: for each candidate target price P, compute `costToClear(P)` = sum of price x quantity of all listings below P; `units` bought; `daysToSell = units / soldPerDay`; `profit = units x P x 0.95 - costToClear - expectedDepositLoss`. Reject if daysToSell is long relative to how fast supply can respond, or if costToClear exceeds a small share of the bankroll.
- Partial-reset detection from one scan: sort listings by unit price and find the largest relative gap between consecutive price levels; the quantity below the gap is the cost to clear and the price above the gap is the target.
- Supply-response risk can be proxied without history: crafted items (cost-to-craft below target price means crafters re-enter), farmable materials, vendor-sold items (hard price ceiling at vendor buy price), and region-wide commodities (any seller in the region can refill) are poor reset targets.
- The Chronocloth case shows a 50% price lift cost about 1.3M and the binding constraint was volume, not price; a rule should weight sold-per-day at least as heavily as depth.

### Gaps
- No quantitative reset criteria from tool documentation or academic work; all practice rules come from one author.
- I could not confirm that the TSM reset scan still exists in current TSM.
- No WoW-specific academic study of cornering or price manipulation was found; the finance papers are analogies.

## 4. Vendor flips, limited-supply vendor items, and vendor-value sniping

### Takeaway
Two rules are fully mechanical: buy any listing priced below its vendor sell value (risk-free), and resell NPC-vendor items, especially limited-stock recipes, at a markup over the vendor's price. The markup rule of thumb (30% minimum profit) is a single-author convention.

### Cited Findings
- A limited quantity recipe is "a recipe sold by a vendor in limited quantities (usually 1, 2, 3 or 4)"; restocking "can take as long as several hours"; the page lists 99 such recipes across 8 professions (Engineering 24, Leatherworking 17, Tailoring 15, Alchemy 13, Blacksmithing 12, Enchanting 10, Jewelcrafting 7, Cooking 1); one listed restock time is 5-22 minutes (Herbalist's Gloves pattern). The page does not discuss reselling. — [Warcraft Wiki: Limited quantity recipes](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Limited_quantity_recipes) [reference wiki]
- TSM `VendorSell` is the value when selling an item to a vendor; `VendorBuy` is the cost to buy from a vendor and "is only populated for a particular item when you visit a vendor that can sell it". — [TSM: Which Price Sources can I use](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean) [tool docs]
- TSM's default Vendoring operation sells to a vendor any item that has expired from the auction house at least 20 times. — [TSM: How do the Default Operations work](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/addon/how-do-the-default-operations-work) [tool docs]
- Convention (The Lazy Goldmaker, Classic, Nov 2019): flip vendor-sold recipes chosen from profession leveling guides and end-game usefulness; limited-supply ones are the most valuable; price with a 30% minimum profit over the vendor cost (the author uses 5x vendor sell price as a stand-in for the vendor's purchase price because `vendorbuy` data goes missing); post one of each. Example: the Runecloth Bag pattern costs 1g 20s at the vendor and sold for up to 30 gold on the author's realm. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Classic goldmaking: Make easy gold flipping vendor recipes](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/classic-goldmaking-make-easy-gold-flipping-vendor-recipes) [community]
- Convention (commenter on The Lazy Goldmaker's TSM4 sniper guide): a vendor-only sniper operation with max price `vendorsell-1c`, i.e. buy anything listed below its vendor sell price. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: TSM4 Guide Part 10](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/tsm4-guide-part-10-finding-the-best-deals-with-sniper) [community]
- Unverified community claim (Classic): early in a realm's life undercutting can push prices "below vendor level", and sellers are told never to vendor an item that would fetch more on the auction house. — [Warcraft Tavern: WoW Classic Gold and Trading Guide](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/wow-classic/guides/gold-and-trading-guide/) [community]
- WoW Forever (community reporting): use vendor price as a floor; disenchanting materials such as Strange Dust vendor for 1 copper so their value depends on the auction house; a Black Market vendor sells Black Lotus and Arcane Crystal at fixed prices, so stockpiling those is not profitable. — [ConquestCapped: WoW Forever auction house guide](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community] (gold-seller site; low reliability)
- WoW Forever: Blizzard's 10 Nov 2026 hotfix notes reduced vendor prices of some crafted items (for example "Minor Arcane Elixir sell value reduced to 5 copper (was 25 copper)", Iron Grenades to 1s 25c from 5s). — [Warcraft Tavern: WoW Forever hotfixes for November 10](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/forever/news/class-economy-changes-wow-forever-hotfixes-for-november-10/) [community reproducing official Blizzard notes]

### Inferences
- Vendor-value snipe rule: flag any listing with `buyoutPerUnit < vendorSellPerUnit`; profit per unit is the difference, with no cut or deposit, so the only risk is stale vendor data (see the Forever hotfix that lowered vendor values) and bag space.
- Vendor-ceiling rule: for an item with known `vendorBuy` and unlimited stock, the auction price cannot sustainably exceed vendorBuy plus a convenience premium; flag listings above vendorBuy as flip targets only for limited-stock items or for vendors that are remote or faction-restricted.
- Vendor-flip rule: candidate if `minBuyout x 0.95 >= vendorBuy x 1.3` and current auction quantity is small; an addon can only know `vendorBuy` after the player visits the vendor, so a static table of limited-supply items is needed.

### Gaps
- No verified data on how often listings appear below vendor value or on sell-through of vendor recipes.
- The wiki list of limited-quantity recipes is Classic-era content; I found no equivalent verified list for Midnight or for WoW Forever vendors.
- Vendor-sold battle pets as a flip were mentioned only in old (2011) articles seen in search summaries; not opened, not used.

## 5. Cross-faction, neutral auction house, and cross-realm arbitrage

### Takeaway
In retail, commodities have been region-wide since 9.2.7 and factions merged since 6.0.2, so the only location arbitrage left is realm-to-realm for non-commodity items, moved through the Warband bank. In Classic-style houses the faction markets are separate and connect only through the neutral house, whose higher cut (15% by community sources) must be exceeded by the price gap.

### Cited Findings
- Retail: "Auction Houses on all realms in a region now share commodity listings" (gems, herbs, flasks, consumables); "Items that aren't commodities - like armor and weapons - will continue to be realm-specific." — [Blizzard: Shadowlands 9.2.7 Update Notes](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23833174) [official]
- Retail: patch 6.0.2 (14 Oct 2014) combined Alliance, Horde and neutral listings per realm; patch 9.2.7 (16 Aug 2022) made commodity listings region-wide. — [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki]
- Retail: a Warband spans every realm and faction within a region; "any non-soulbound item" can be placed in the Warband bank and gold can be deposited and withdrawn across characters. — [Blizzard: Get the Band Together for Warbands](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24115313/) [official]
- Saddlebag Exchange's workflow: "Simply buy low in one realm, deposit an item in your warband bank, move it to another realm and sell high!"; its Export Search ranks realms to sell on by price and population, its Import Search finds items to buy on other realms and sell at home. Commodities are excluded because they are region-wide. — [Saddlebag Exchange: TL;DR guide](https://saddlebagexchange.com/blog/wow/tldr) [tool docs]; [Saddlebag Exchange: cross-realm announcement](https://saddlebagexchange.com/blog/wow/crossrealm1) [tool docs]
- Undermine Exchange (covers Midnight and Forever) offers an "Arbitrage Mode" for searching a whole region, region median prices, and per-item listings, charts and heat maps (some features paid). — [Undermine Exchange](https://undermine.exchange/) [primary data / tool docs] (site was under maintenance when opened; data source and update frequency not stated on the page)
- Retail wiki note: characters on the same account cannot bid on items posted by another character on that account; Blizzard does not prohibit cross-faction "gifting" by listing an item at a 1 copper buyout, and anyone may buy it at that price. — [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House (via Neutral Auction House redirect)](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Neutral_Auction_House) [reference wiki]
- Classic-style fees (unverified community claim; no official or wiki confirmation found): faction house cut 5%, neutral house cut 15%; deposits 5% / 20% / 60% of vendor sell value for 2 / 8 / 24 hours at faction houses and 25% / 100% / 300% at neutral houses. — [Ten Ton Hammer: Auctioneer guide (2007, updated 2016)](https://www.tentonhammer.com/guides/auctioneer-guide-page-2) [community]; cut figures repeated by [TimeSaver](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-classic-era-auction-house-flipping-gold) [community]
- Convention (TimeSaver, Classic Era): flip through the neutral house only when the cross-faction price gap is bigger than the 10-percentage-point cut penalty. — [TimeSaver: Classic Era flipping](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-classic-era-auction-house-flipping-gold) [community]
- Economist's analysis (blog, 2009): the neutral house moves goods cheaply between factions but not money, so faction gold behaves like two currencies and only relative price differences are exploitable; with a 15% neutral commission, a 40% price-level gap was barely worth arbitraging in the author's worked example. — [David Friedman: Arbitrage, Comparative Advantage, and World of Warcraft](https://daviddfriedman.blogspot.com/2009/11/arbitrage-comparative-advantage-and.html) [community] (academic economist's personal blog)
- WoW Forever (community reporting, tested in game on 16 Nov 2026 by the publisher): one auction house per region, ruleset and faction; a faction's three capitals share one market; faction houses are not cross-faction; the factions trade only through neutral goblin houses (Gadgetzan, Booty Bay, Everlook, plus a neutral auctioneer in Valanaar); cut 5% faction and 15% neutral. — [ConquestCapped: WoW Forever auction house guide](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community] (gold-seller site; low reliability; the page says other sites disagree on cross-faction status)
- WoW Forever: Blizzard "has published neither a deposit formula nor a commission" for the Forever auction house. — [Kami Labs: WoW Forever auction house: deposit drops to 1 copper](https://kami-labs.fr/en/wow-forever/hotel-des-ventes-wow-forever-depot-1-cuivre/) [community]
- Academic grounding: cross-server prices in WoW converge (Morrison and Fontenla 2013) and cross-server arbitrage was predicted and found to be minor in 2009 data (Kosminsky 2009). — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:spr:empeco:v:44:y:2013:i:3:p:1053-1064) [peer-reviewed]; [JVWR](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/index.php/jvwr/article/view/869) [peer-reviewed]

### Inferences
- Retail cross-realm rule: for a non-commodity item, candidate if `priceOnTargetRealm x 0.95 - priceOnSourceRealm >= minProfit` and the target realm has demand (population, sale rate). An in-game addon only sees the realm it is on, so it needs imported region data or per-character scans stored in SavedVariables.
- Classic-style cross-faction rule: candidate if `otherFactionPrice x 0.85 - ownFactionPrice >= minProfit` (15% neutral cut), plus the neutral deposit (five times higher by community figures) and travel time; a single addon cannot see the other faction's house without a second character scanning it.
- Friedman's point implies the profitable trade is a round trip in goods (carry item A across, carry item B back), so the rule should compare price ratios of two items between factions, not absolute prices.
- On megaserver or region-wide markets (retail commodities, Forever) there is no realm arbitrage at all; only faction (Forever) or realm-only item (retail) gaps remain.

### Gaps
- No official or wiki source confirmed the Classic-era neutral cut (15%) or the Classic deposit table; a Blizzard support article on fee calculation (article 180541) is login-gated and could not be read.
- Whether MoP Classic or other progression Classic versions have merged-faction or region-wide auction houses in late 2026 was not established; the MoP Classic 5.5.0 development notes I opened do not mention the auction house.
- Whether player-to-player cross-realm trading is currently enabled in retail was not verified.
- Forever fee and structure claims rest on community reporting from a commercially interested site.

## 6. Time arbitrage: weekday cycles, patch transitions, stockpiling ahead of demand

### Takeaway
Goldmakers buy when supply piles up and sell into raid-night and reset-day demand, and stockpile ahead of known content releases, but the evidence is anecdotal: I found no dataset or study that quantifies a weekly cycle. The one piece of semi-quantified evidence is a 2012 column showing supply peaking after the Tuesday reset and a consumable price rising nearly 50% in a low-supply week.

### Cited Findings
- Unverified community claim (Engadget "Gold Capped" column, Feb 2012, charts from one US realm): "Tuesdays are easily the most volatile of all the days to either buy or sell"; supply peaks right after the Tuesday reset then drops over the following days; listings rise on weekends; Volcanic Potion prices rose "nearly 50%" during one low-supply week while Inferno Ruby stayed in a 210-230 range most of the year; "there is no perfect pattern". — [Engadget: What days should you buy or sell on the Auction House?](https://www.engadget.com/2012/02/07/what-days-should-you-buy-or-sell-on-the-auction-house/) [community]
- Unverified community claim (The Lazy Goldmaker, Classic): material prices shift with raid days, weekly resets and crafter session sizes; consumables often sell on popular raid nights; Classic's 24-hour maximum auction duration speeds up daily and weekly swings. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping (Wrath Classic)](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-the-king-of-entry-level-goldmaking-in-classic) [community]; [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping (Classic, Dec 2024)](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-an-absolute-blast-on-the-classic-ah-the-easiest-gold-you-will-ever-make) [community]
- Unverified community claim (TimeSaver, Classic Era): buy raid consumables midweek and sell into the Tuesday/Wednesday raid rush. — [TimeSaver: Classic Era flipping](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-classic-era-auction-house-flipping-gold) [community]
- Content-driven demand shifts cut both ways: holding fire protection potion materials when the next raid tier (Ahn'Qiraj) arrives is given as an example of a stockpile losing value. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping (Classic, Dec 2024)](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-an-absolute-blast-on-the-classic-ah-the-easiest-gold-you-will-ever-make) [community]
- WoW Forever (community reporting, first two weeks after the 4 Nov 2026 launch): prices reportedly lowest in the morning and highest in the evening; first raids open 9 Dec and raid consumables are "expected next"; observed moves include Giant Egg falling from about 1 gold (7 Nov) to about 18 silver (13 Nov) with over 1,300 listed, and Copper Ore rising from about 20 copper at launch to about 1.25 silver five days later. — [ConquestCapped: WoW Forever auction house guide](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community] (gold-seller site; anonymous player reports)
- Tool support: Saddlebag Exchange flags items whose quantity is falling toward a shortage, and TSM's DBMarket (14-day weighted) versus DBHistorical (60-day) gives a ready-made short-versus-long price comparison. — [Saddlebag Exchange: market overview guide](https://saddlebagexchange.com/blog/wow/how-to-trade-using-our-wow-market-overview) [tool docs]; [TSM: Price Sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean) [tool docs]
- Academic grounding on interventions and price shocks in a virtual economy: Hogan-Hennessy, Xenopoulos and Silva (2022), "Market Interventions in a Large-Scale Virtual Economy", arXiv:2210.07970: in Old School RuneScape a transaction tax did not meaningfully affect trading volume at the tax boundaries, and an item sink contributed to luxury-good price inflation "without reducing trade volume". — [arXiv:2210.07970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07970) [working paper] (abstract only read)
- Academic grounding on monitoring: Scholten, Cowling, Hawick and Walker (2019), "Unconventional Exchange: Methods for Statistical Analysis of Virtual Goods", IEEE Conference on Games 2019, arXiv:1905.06721: applies financial volatility and inflation methods to 3,467 Old School RuneScape price series over 180 trading days. — [arXiv:1905.06721](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06721) [peer-reviewed conference paper] (abstract only read)

### Inferences
- A weekly-cycle rule needs the addon's own scan history: per item, compute the median price by weekday (and by hour bucket) over several weeks; flag "buy" when the current price is in the item's low-weekday band and the high-weekday median x 0.95 exceeds it by the minimum margin. Without stored history this strategy cannot be detected from a single scan.
- A simpler single-scan proxy: current minBuyout well below DBMarket (14-day) while DBMarket is near or above DBHistorical (60-day) suggests a temporary dip, whereas minBuyout below DBMarket and DBMarket below DBHistorical suggests a falling market (Saddlebag's "Decreasing/Crashing" classes).
- Event stockpiling is a calendar rule, not a scan rule: tag items by the content they serve (raid consumables before a raid opens, old-tier resistance materials before the tier is replaced) and warn when a held position's demand event has passed.
- The reset-day supply peak implies buying materials just after reset and selling finished consumables just before raid times; the direction is consistent across the community sources but its size is not measured.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or primary-data analysis of day-of-week or hour-of-day price cycles in WoW was found; Undermine Exchange exposes charts and heat maps that could be used to test it, but I did not obtain data.
- No verified source on end-of-patch material buying or pre-expansion stockpiling returns; the guidance is anecdotal.
- The Engadget evidence is 14 years old and from one realm.

## 7. Posting strategy: undercut rules, duration, stacks, cancel-scanning, walls, deposit loss, quantity versus daily volume

### Takeaway
In retail (and, by community reporting, in WoW Forever for commodities) matching the lowest price is enough because the newest listing at a given price sells first, so TSM defaults to a 0 copper undercut; in Classic the default is a 1 copper undercut and same-size stacks matter. Deposits scale with vendor price and duration and are lost on expiry or cancel, which is the main cost of cancel-scanning and of posting more than the market absorbs.

### Cited Findings
- TSM Auctioning: undercut amount defaults to 0c in retail and 1c in Classic; the docs say 0c is generally fine in retail "because of the Last-In-First-Out system". — [TSM Addon: Auctioning Operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/1072146-tsm-addon-auctioning-operations) [tool docs]
- TSM Auctioning settings relevant to posting rules: auction duration (12, 24 or 48 hours); post cap (0 disables); amount kept in bags; "don't post after this many expires"; bid as % of buyout; minimum, normal and maximum price; behavior when the market is below minimum (don't post, post at minimum / normal / maximum, or ignore auctions below minimum) and above maximum; ignore competing auctions with short time left (under 30 minutes, 30 minutes-2 hours, 2-12 hours); blacklisted players (undercut even below minimum). — [TSM Addon: Auctioning Operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/1072146-tsm-addon-auctioning-operations) [tool docs]
- TSM cancel scanning: "Cancel undercut auctions" defaults to yes and triggers when you are undercut or when someone posts the same item at the same price after you; "Cancel to repost higher" offers to cancel and repost when competition is gone, subject to a repost threshold (minimum price difference). — [TSM Addon: Auctioning Operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/1072146-tsm-addon-auctioning-operations) [tool docs]
- Retail fees on failure: cancelling an auction with no bids forfeits the deposit; cancelling with a bid forfeits the deposit plus a fee equal to the house cut of the current bid; an unsold auction returns the item without the deposit. Deposit is 15% / 30% / 60% of vendor sell price for 12 / 24 / 48 hours, minimum 1 silver. — [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki]
- Retail commodities since patch 8.3: stackable items no longer sell in stacks; the buyer enters a quantity and the system takes the lowest-priced listings ("the cheapest auctions are always sorted to the top"). — [Warcraft Wiki: Patch 8.3.0](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Patch_8.3.0) [reference wiki] (first 100,000 characters of the page read)
- Historical official context: in August 2018 Blizzard added a deposit equal to 20% of the asking price per auction for certain high-volume trade goods to discourage single-item stack spam, refunded if the item sells ("Successful auctions aren't affected by this change"). — [Blizzard forums: Auction House Update (Ythisens, 22 Aug 2018)](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/auction-house-update/10385) [official] (pre-8.3 measure; whether it still applies to commodities in 12.x was not verified)
- Sale proceeds arrive by mail with a delay: patch 2.2.0 delayed sale emails by one hour (retail history); Forever reportedly intends a 60-minute delay. — [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki]; [ConquestCapped: WoW Forever guide](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community]
- Classic stacks (unverified community claim): TSM's Classic posting operation "only considers stacks of the same size" when pricing; turn-in items should be posted in the stack size buyers need (for example E'ko in stacks of 3 or multiples of 3). — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping (Classic, Dec 2024)](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-an-absolute-blast-on-the-classic-ah-the-easiest-gold-you-will-ever-make) [community]; [The Lazy Goldmaker: Consumable flipping](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/consumable-flipping-is-a-great-supplement-to-your-flipping-routine) [community]
- Convention (Warcraft Tavern, Classic): do not undercut "by more than a single copper"; deep undercuts lower the whole market. — [Warcraft Tavern: WoW Classic Gold and Trading Guide](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/wow-classic/guides/gold-and-trading-guide/) [community]
- Unverified community claim (Engadget, 2012): in small undercut-driven markets (glyphs) it pays to list when rivals go offline, for example late evening. — [Engadget: What days should you buy or sell](https://www.engadget.com/2012/02/07/what-days-should-you-buy-or-sell-on-the-auction-house/) [community]
- WoW Forever (community reporting): commodities are sold by quantity and fill from the cheapest units first across all sellers; "at equal prices, the newest listing sells first", so matching the lowest commodity price puts your stack first and a 1 copper undercut only loses money; gear and most recipes are one auction per item; durations are 2, 8 or 24 hours with larger deposits for longer auctions; deposit refunded only on sale. Example given: with 95 silk bags listed at 40 silver, a post at 40 silver sold about 50 bags within minutes. — [ConquestCapped: WoW Forever auction house guide](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community] (gold-seller site; low reliability)
- WoW Forever minimum deposit: Blizzard's 10 Nov 2026 hotfix notes state "Adjusted the minimum deposit amount to 1 copper (was 1 silver)". — [Warcraft Tavern: WoW Forever hotfixes for November 10](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/forever/news/class-economy-changes-wow-forever-hotfixes-for-november-10/) [community reproducing official Blizzard notes]. The section was then removed and rescheduled for the 17 Nov maintenance; under the 1 silver minimum a fish worth about 60 copper cost 100 copper to list. — [Kami Labs: deposit drops to 1 copper](https://kami-labs.fr/en/wow-forever/hotel-des-ventes-wow-forever-depot-1-cuivre/) [community]
- TSM's default Vendoring operation treats 20 expirations as the point to stop relisting and vendor the item. — [TSM: How do the Default Operations work](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/addon/how-do-the-default-operations-work) [tool docs]

### Inferences
- Posting-price rule by flavor: retail and (per community reports) Forever commodities, match the lowest price at or above your minimum; Classic-style, undercut the lowest same-size stack by 1 copper if still above your minimum; in all flavors, if the lowest price is below your minimum, hold or post at normal price for a later recovery.
- Because same-price priority goes to the newest listing, every competing post at the same price pushes yours back; an addon can estimate queue position as "quantity listed at or below my price that was posted after me", which a scan cannot see directly; the practical proxy is total quantity at or below your price.
- Quantity rule: post no more than what sells within the chosen duration (`postQty <= soldPerDay x durationDays x expectedShare`); the remainder only adds deposit exposure and signals depth to competitors. No source gives a number, so this is a derived rule.
- Deposit-bleed rule: skip or shorten duration when `deposit / expectedProfit` is high; the 5x vendor-price convention in section 1 is the community's shorthand for this.
- A "price wall" (a large quantity at one price that undercutters must get below) is detectable as a single price level holding a large share of total listed quantity; I found no source that defines or evaluates walls, so this term is unsourced here.
- Cancel-scanning costs one forfeited deposit per cancel; it is rational only when the deposit is small relative to the margin (low vendor-price items) and is pointless for retail commodities where reposting at the same price regains priority at the cost of the deposit.

### Gaps
- Blizzard's own statement of the 8.3 "last in, first out" rule was not opened (the wiki patch page section I read does not include it and the Icy Veins copy returned HTTP 403); the rule is supported here by TSM's documentation only.
- Current retail commodity deposit rules in 12.x (whether the 2018 20% surcharge persists in some form) were not verified.
- Classic-style durations (2/8/24 hours) and deposit rates rest on community sources; TSM's doc lists 12/24/48 without distinguishing Classic.
- No source quantifies how many units to post relative to daily sold volume, or evaluates price walls.

## 8. Bid-versus-buyout opportunities in Classic-style auction houses

### Takeaway
Classic-style listings carry a minimum bid that is often far below the buyout, so an auction near expiry with a low current bid can be won below market; TSM's Bid Sniper automates the search for exactly that. WoW extends an auction's time when it is bid on late, which, following Roth and Ockenfels, weakens last-second sniping compared with eBay's hard close.

### Cited Findings
- Bidding rules (retail-era wiki page describing the classic bid system): minimum starting bid is 1 copper; the default bid is the seller's starting bid or the next increment above the current bid; bid money is held in escrow and returned if outbid; bids cannot be cancelled; buyout exists only if the seller set one; sorting by price per unit uses buyout, so auctions without a buyout are listed last; the selling UI suggests a starting bid of 150% of vendor sell price. — [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki]
- Time-left is shown only in bands (Short under 30 minutes, Medium 30 minutes-2 hours, Long 2-12 hours, Very Long 12-48 hours), and heavy bidding can extend a listing (Short becomes Medium, Medium becomes Long); patch 1.1.0 added "time added per bid". — [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki]
- TSM's Bid Sniper "looks for auctions that are about to expire" and have a bid price below the max price in the Sniper operation; it uses the same operations as the Buyout Sniper. — [TSM news: TSM4 Deep Dive: Sniper](https://tradeskillmaster.com/news/tsm4-deep-dive-sniper-136) [tool docs]
- TSM Auctioning lets sellers set the bid as a percentage of buyout (0-100%, never below the minimum price) and lets posters ignore competing auctions with little time left. — [TSM Addon: Auctioning Operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/1072146-tsm-addon-auctioning-operations) [tool docs]
- Retail: commodities cannot be bid on at all since they are bought by quantity from the cheapest listings. — [Warcraft Wiki: Patch 8.3.0](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Patch_8.3.0) [reference wiki]
- API detail: `C_AuctionHouse.PlaceBid` needs a hardware event and, per the wiki, accepts only whole-gold bid amounts in retail (non-zero copper values silently fail). — [Warcraft Wiki: API C_AuctionHouse.PlaceBid](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.PlaceBid) [reference wiki]
- Academic: on eBay (fixed end time) there is far more last-minute bidding than on Amazon (auction extends until ten minutes pass with no bid); the ending rule explains the difference. — Roth and Ockenfels (2002), American Economic Review 92(4):1093-1103; [NBER w7729](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7729) [working paper; published version peer-reviewed]
- Academic: an economics honors thesis on WoW auctions concludes that the WoW mechanism differs enough from eBay's that techniques used to analyze eBay auctions do not carry over. — Marks, E. L. (2009), "Price Dynamics in Virtual World Auctions", Stanford University Department of Economics honors thesis; [Stanford Digital Repository](https://purl.stanford.edu/pd000kr9463) [working paper / thesis] (abstract only read)
- Unverified community claim: bait bidding (bidding up your own auction) needs a second account or a friend because you cannot buy your own auctions. — [TimeSaver: Classic Era flipping](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-classic-era-auction-house-flipping-gold) [community] (borderline tactic: shill bidding to mislead buyers; not recommended, listed so a tool can warn about it)

### Inferences
- Bid-opportunity rule: candidate if `max(minBid, currentBid + increment) <= k x marketValue` AND time-left band is Short or Medium AND the item passes the flipping liquidity test. Expected value must be discounted by the chance of being outbid; funds are locked in escrow until then and bids cannot be withdrawn.
- Longer-dated low bids are weaker signals: a 1 copper bid on a Very Long auction will almost always be outbid, so rank by time-left band first.
- Because bids can extend the timer, WoW behaves more like Amazon's soft close than eBay's hard close; the edge comes from other buyers not looking at bid-only or about-to-expire auctions (they sort by buyout), not from last-second timing.
- Auctions with a bid but no buyout sort last in price-per-unit order, so they are systematically under-seen; a scan rule that surfaces "no-buyout, low-bid, short time" listings targets that blind spot.

### Gaps
- The minimum bid increment in Classic-style houses was not found in any opened source.
- Whether WoW Forever listings support bids at all is not stated by the Forever source I opened.
- Whether TSM's Bid Sniper still works in current Classic clients is not confirmed by current TSM docs.
- No data on win rates or margins for bid strategies.

## 9. Disenchant / destroy-value sniping (gear below disenchant value, ore below prospecting value, herbs below milling value)

### Takeaway
The signal is a listing priced below the expected market value of what it yields when destroyed; TSM exposes this as the `Destroy` price source, which makes the rule directly computable. I found no verified thresholds, so the safety margin is left to the user.

### Cited Findings
- TSM `Destroy` is "the value of the items you would receive by destroying it, on average", computed by default from DBMarket values of the resulting materials. — [TSM: Which Price Sources can I use](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean) [tool docs]
- TSM's Vendoring operation has "max market value" and "max destroy value" limits (unset by default), i.e. TSM itself treats destroy value as a floor alongside vendor value when deciding what to dump. — [TSM: How do the Default Operations work](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/addon/how-do-the-default-operations-work) [tool docs]
- Since patch 1.5.0, disenchanted items cannot be auctioned (the destroy step is one-way: only the resulting materials can be sold). — [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki]
- WoW Forever (community reporting): one Greater Magic Essence sold for about 2.2 silver while each Lesser sold for 1 silver, and the page says splitting one Greater into three Lessers "paid about 80 copper per essence after the cut" (the page's own arithmetic is not shown); an example of a conversion arbitrage inside the enchanting-material market. Crafted Lesser and Greater Magic Wands were reported as saturated. — [ConquestCapped: WoW Forever auction house guide](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community] (gold-seller site; low reliability)
- Unverified community claim: enchanting materials are among the preferred flip targets in Classic because of low vendor prices (low deposits) and steady demand. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping (Classic, Dec 2024)](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-an-absolute-blast-on-the-classic-ah-the-easiest-gold-you-will-ever-make) [community]

### Inferences
- Rule: candidate if `buyout <= k x destroyValue x 0.95 - depositOnMaterials`, where `destroyValue = sum(probability_i x expectedQuantity_i x marketValue_i)` over the outputs for the item's class, quality and item level, and k below 1 is a safety margin for drop-rate variance and for the materials' price falling as you sell into them.
- The same structure covers prospecting (ore to gems, usually 5 ore per attempt) and milling (herbs to pigments, usually 5 herbs per attempt) and material conversions (greater to lesser essences); the addon needs yield tables per flavor, which TSM ships internally but which I did not find documented at a citable URL.
- Liquidity check belongs on the output materials (sold per day), not on the gear piece; the buy side is a one-off.
- The player must have the profession (or an alt that does); a rule should be gated on known professions.
- Using DBMarket for the outputs overstates value when the material price is falling; a conservative rule uses `min(dbmarket, dbminbuyout)` of the outputs.

### Gaps
- No opened source gives disenchant, prospecting or milling yield tables or recommended safety margins; TSM's "disenchant search" appeared only in old changelog summaries (not opened).
- Which destroy professions exist and yield what in WoW Forever and in Midnight 12.x was not researched here.
- No data on how often gear is listed below disenchant value.

## 10. Position sizing and risk management

### Takeaway
I found no formal position-sizing rule (no "at most N% of gold per market") in any tier of source; practice is expressed as diversification ("go wide"), minimum bankrolls for risky tactics, and using sale rate and sold-per-day as the liquidity gauge. The one concrete ratio is a goldmaker risking about 2% of his gold on a reset and still losing a quarter of the stake.

### Cited Findings
- Liquidity measures available to a rule: `DBRegionSaleRate` (share of postings that sell, region average), `DBRegionSoldPerDay` (average units sold per auction house per day), and the player's own `SaleRate` and `NumExpires`. — [TSM: Which Value Sources can I use](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/custom-strings/which-value-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean) [tool docs]
- TSM Shopping's "Maximum restock quantity" caps how many of an item to hold, counting chosen inventory sources; this is the built-in per-item exposure limit. — [TSM Addon: Shopping Operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/tsm-addon-shopping-operations) [tool docs]
- Convention (The Lazy Goldmaker): spread flipping across many materials ("go wide") and expect to reinvest for a week to a month; on average the approach is "extremely consistent" with occasional losses. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping (Wrath Classic)](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-the-king-of-entry-level-goldmaking-in-classic) [community]
- Convention (The Lazy Goldmaker): have at least 1 million gold and stable income before attempting resets; the Chronocloth reset risked 1.3M of about 60M (about 2%) and lost 300k. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Resetting markets](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/advanced-gold-making-tactics-resetting-markets) [community]; [The Lazy Goldmaker: Chronocloth reset](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/i-spent-1-3-million-gold-resetting-the-chronocloth-market) [community]
- Convention (Warcraft Tavern, Classic): trade several item types so one price collapse does not hurt much; compute margin after fees before any flip. — [Warcraft Tavern: WoW Classic Gold and Trading Guide](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/wow-classic/guides/gold-and-trading-guide/) [community]
- Unverified community claim (TimeSaver, Classic Era): flipping with 50-100g is "gold-gated"; 500-1,000g is where it becomes worthwhile; rough returns 20-60g/hour on 100-300g and 50-150g/hour on 1,000g+. — [TimeSaver: Classic Era flipping](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-classic-era-auction-house-flipping-gold) [community]
- Supply-depth warning (convention): if thousands of an item are listed, prices may be trending down; consider switching materials. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping (Wrath Classic)](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-the-king-of-entry-level-goldmaking-in-classic) [community]
- Academic grounding: arbitrage capital is limited and concentrated, so mispricings can persist or widen before they close (Shleifer and Vishny 1997); larger populations give more stable prices in WoW (Kosminsky 2009). — [NBER w5167](https://www.nber.org/papers/w5167) [working paper; published version peer-reviewed]; [JVWR](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/index.php/jvwr/article/view/869) [peer-reviewed]

### Inferences
- Exposure rules a tool can offer as user-set parameters (defaults are my suggestions, not sourced): cap per-item holdings at a number of days of volume (`heldQty <= d x soldPerDay`), cap per-item capital at a percentage of liquid gold, and cap high-variance tactics (resets, speculation) at a low single-digit percentage, consistent with the 2% example.
- "Days to exit" = `heldQty / (soldPerDay x myExpectedShare)` is the most decision-relevant liquidity figure and can be computed from TSM-style data or from the addon's own sales ledger.
- For thin, realm-only items the sale rate is a per-posting probability; expected holding cost is `deposit x (1/saleRate - 1)`, so low-sale-rate items need both a larger discount and a low vendor price.
- Track realized results per item (own SaleRate, NumExpires, average buy versus average sell) and stop buying an item after repeated expirations; TSM's default of 20 expirations is very permissive.

### Gaps
- No source, at any tier, states a maximum share of gold per market; the "N%" framing in the research question has no citable answer.
- No WoW-specific academic work on trader risk or inventory management was found.

## 11. Mistakes that lose gold

### Takeaway
The recurring losses are mechanical: ignoring the cut and deposits, buying on a reference price that does not reflect real sales, holding illiquid stock, and fat-finger posting. Deliberate price-manipulation baits exist but are documented only anecdotally.

### Cited Findings
- Deposit bleed: deposits are lost on every expiry or cancel and scale with vendor price and duration (retail: 15% / 30% / 60% of vendor sell price for 12 / 24 / 48 hours). — [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki]
- Convention: avoid flipping items whose market value is under 5x vendor sell price (metal bars and similar high vendor-value goods) because the deposit is large relative to the margin. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping (Wrath Classic)](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-the-king-of-entry-level-goldmaking-in-classic) [community]
- Forever example of deposit bleed: with a 1 silver minimum deposit, an item worth about 60 copper cost 100 copper to list; European players reported green crafting materials losing money versus vendoring. — [Kami Labs: deposit drops to 1 copper](https://kami-labs.fr/en/wow-forever/hotel-des-ventes-wow-forever-depot-1-cuivre/) [community]
- Stale or misleading reference prices: `DBMinBuyout` is as of the last pricing update, not live; `DBMarket` is an average of listed auctions, not of sales. — [TSM: Which Price Sources can I use](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean) [tool docs]
- Unverified community claim (manipulation bait): some sellers push an item's apparent value up over time and then list it "cheaper but still expensive" to catch snipers; unfamiliar items that trigger a sniper should be checked by tooltip before buying. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: TSM4 Guide Part 10](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/tsm4-guide-part-10-finding-the-best-deals-with-sniper) [community]
- Overestimating volume: the Chronocloth reset failed because the realistic sale rate at the target price was far below expectation and a substitute path (crafting) became viable. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Chronocloth reset](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/i-spent-1-3-million-gold-resetting-the-chronocloth-market) [community]
- Fat-finger posting: the same author mis-posted 500 bolts at 5 gold each; in Forever, entering a stack total in the unit-price field multiplies the price. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Chronocloth reset](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/i-spent-1-3-million-gold-resetting-the-chronocloth-market) [community]; [ConquestCapped: WoW Forever guide](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community]
- Unverified community claims (TimeSaver, Classic Era) on common mistakes: forgetting the cut and selling at a loss; ignoring failed re-list deposits; holding dead stock of low-volume items; flipping random green gear as a beginner because its prices are noisy. — [TimeSaver: Classic Era flipping](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-classic-era-auction-house-flipping-gold) [community]
- Holding stock through a content change: resistance-potion materials lose value when the raid tier that needs them is superseded. — [The Lazy Goldmaker: Material flipping (Classic, Dec 2024)](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-an-absolute-blast-on-the-classic-ah-the-easiest-gold-you-will-ever-make) [community]
- Markets with a fixed-price vendor source cannot be cornered or stockpiled profitably (Forever: Black Lotus and Arcane Crystal sold by an NPC at fixed prices). — [ConquestCapped: WoW Forever guide](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community]
- Developer-side changes can move floors overnight: Blizzard lowered vendor values of several crafted items in a Forever hotfix. — [Warcraft Tavern: WoW Forever hotfixes for November 10](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/forever/news/class-economy-changes-wow-forever-hotfixes-for-november-10/) [community reproducing official Blizzard notes]
- Terms-of-service boundary: bids and buyouts require a hardware event per action, so any tool that buys or posts without a click is outside what the client allows; Forever can place a "Temporary Economy Hold" on accounts suspected of gold buying or selling. — [Warcraft Wiki: API C_AuctionHouse.PlaceBid](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.PlaceBid) [reference wiki]; [ConquestCapped: WoW Forever guide](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community]

### Inferences
- Guard rules a tool should apply before flagging any buy: (1) the reference price must be corroborated by at least two independent measures (for example realm average, region average, and the tool's own observed sales) and the lowest is used; (2) if the item has no recorded sales or a very low sale rate, never flag on price alone; (3) if the current listing ladder has a single seller holding most of the supply at a high price with one "cheap" outlier, mark as possible bait; (4) compute net profit after cut and expected deposit loss and show it, not the gross spread.
- Outlier-inflated averages are the mechanism behind "fake high market value": a few absurd listings on an item with no trades raise a listing-based average; using medians, trimming, or sale-based prices removes most of it.
- A posting sanity check (price entered is less than 20% or more than 500% of market value; unit versus stack confusion) would have prevented the two fat-finger losses cited.
- Borderline or excluded tactics for a tool to avoid promoting: shill or bait bidding with a second account, coordinated cross-faction gold transfers through 1 copper listings, and anything buying or posting without a hardware event. Real-money trading is excluded entirely.
