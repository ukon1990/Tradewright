# Price dynamics in World of Warcraft auction house economies (retail, Classic-style, WoW Forever) — notes as of 2026-11-20

Source tiers used below: **[peer-reviewed]**, **[working paper]** (incl. theses, arXiv preprints), **[official]** (Blizzard staff posts / news / API announcements), **[primary data]**, **[reference wiki]** (warcraft.wiki.gg), **[tool docs]**, **[community]** (guides, blogs, news sites, player forum posts).
Claims resting only on [community] sources are marked **UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM**. Every link listed as a source was opened and checked unless it is explicitly flagged "NOT OPENED / could not verify". Flavor labels: **Retail** (Midnight, 12.x), **Classic** (Era / Anniversary / progression), **Forever** (WoW Forever, launched Nov 2026, Classic-style AH), **Other MMO** (evidence from a different game, used as an analogue only).

Headline caveat for the report writer: I found **no peer-reviewed or primary-data study that measures weekday or hour-of-day price cycles in WoW**. All "Tuesday is expensive" style statements are community lore, and the community sources contradict each other. The academic literature on WoW prices covers cross-server convergence, price stability vs population, and expansion-driven inflation, not weekly seasonality.

## 1. What drives supply and demand for in-game goods?

### Takeaway
The best-supported drivers are structural ones set by the game operator: content releases (expansions/patches) that raise gold supply and create new demand, the share of players in the level band that uses a good, and deliberate cost/recipe changes in patch notes. Calendar drivers (raid reset, weekends, Darkmoon Faire, holidays, PvP seasons) are plausible and their schedules are documented, but their price effects are documented only by community sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Player composition drives price (Retail-era data, TBC 2009).** Across six US servers, the server in each pair with a higher share of level 58-70 characters had the higher median Netherweave Cloth price; a quadratic fit of price on that share gave R² = 0.993 (six data points, 30 days of data from wowecon.com). — [Kosminsky 2009, *Journal of Virtual Worlds Research* 2(4), DOI 10.4101/jvwr.v2i4.869](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/view/869) [peer-reviewed]; full text [PDF](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/download/869/634) (read in full). Dated: TBC era, tiny sample.
- **Expansion launches increase money supply and devalue gold.** The authors conclude the Legion release (30 Aug 2016) led to "a vast increase in money supply"; they also note goods from older expansions lose value as newer substitutes appear while newer, scarcer goods gain value. — [Holm & Mäkinen 2018, *Journal of Internet Social Networking & Virtual Communities*, Article ID 672253, DOI 10.5171/2018.672253](https://ibimapublishing.com/articles/JISNVC/2018/672253/672253.pdf) [peer-reviewed; minor open-access journal, purely descriptive, no econometrics]. Dated: Legion.
- **Operator patches are a long-run inflation driver.** A 2009 Charles University thesis using self-collected data concludes long-run inflation of WoW gold stems from the EUR/CNY exchange rate (via real-money trade) and from patches issued by the game operator. — [Skuhrovec 2009, "Inflation of virtual currencies", Charles University, record at NUSL](https://invenio.nusl.cz/record/277053?ln=en) [working paper/thesis; abstract only was readable, full PDF returned 403]. Dated: 2009.
- **Raid/weekly reset schedule (Retail).** NA resets Tuesdays 7 am PDT, EU Wednesdays 3 am CET; reset refreshes weekly quests, Great Vault, raid lockouts, and content/balance updates often go live at reset. — [Siliconera, 29 Oct 2024](https://www.siliconera.com/when-is-wow-weekly-reset-time-na-and-eu-retail/) [community]. I could not open an official or wiki page for reset times (warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Weekly_reset returned 404), so this is community-only for the exact hours.
- **Reset day concentrates raid-consumable demand.** A 2012 gold column describes Tuesday (US reset) as the most volatile day: more players, more raiding, more demand for materials, with supply peaking right after reset and thinning over the following days. — [Engadget / WoW Insider "Gold Capped", 7 Feb 2012](https://www.engadget.com/2012/02/07/what-days-should-you-buy-or-sell-on-the-auction-house/) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM; Cataclysm-era, single-realm anecdotes.
- **Patch-driven demand categories (Retail-era, Cataclysm).** A 2011 column lists recurring per-patch demand surges: enchanting materials (new gear needs enchants), gems (red cuts first), item enhancements such as belt buckles (smaller bump), new craftable gear (sells out for weeks), and glyphs after class changes. Datamining gave "a month or two" of advance notice of new craftables. — [Engadget "Gold Capped", 12 May 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011/05/12/gold-capped-how-to-profit-from-every-single-new-patch/) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM; mechanism is still valid in principle, examples are Cataclysm 4.1/4.2.
- **Darkmoon Faire schedule and material demand (Retail).** Starts Sunday of the first full week of each month at 3 AM realm time and lasts one week. One profession quest per profession per Faire gives +5 skill (since 8.0 only to the Classic-tier profession) and several require imported materials (e.g. Simple Flour, Light Parchment). Completed Darkmoon card decks are turned in for trinkets/reputation. — [warcraft.wiki.gg, Darkmoon Faire](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Darkmoon_Faire) [reference wiki]. The page covers retail; the pre-4.3 (Classic-style) Faire is on a separate article I did not open.
- **Recipe-cost changes in patch notes (Retail, Midnight 12.1.5, 14 Oct 2026).** Crafting costs of 200+ housing-decor recipes were reduced, material costs by up to 80%, lumber most of all (e.g. Apothecary Table: Ironwood Boards 40 → 8, Arcanite Bars 12 → 2, Black Lotus 8 → 1). Blizzard's stated goal: more decor crafting and use of old-expansion professions. — [PC Games (DE), 13 Oct 2026](https://www.pcgames.de/World-of-Warcraft-Spiel-42971/News/Dekorationen-guenstiger-Housing-1556580/) [community/news reporting Blizzard + Wowhead PTR data]. The article does not report AH price effects.
- **Hotfixes change faucets and vendor floors (Forever, Nov 2026).** Blizzard's 10 Nov 2026 hotfix notes: most quests level 48+ now additionally reward gold; vendor value of Iron Grenades cut 5s → 1s25c, Minor Arcane Elixir 25c → 5c, and vendor prices of some crafted leather/cloth/plate armor reduced; two ammo items made non-BoP (tradeable). — [Blizzard forums, Kaivax, "WoW: Forever Hotfixes", post 6, 11 Nov 2026](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-hotfixes-november-11-2026/2403040/6) [official].
- **Operator-added item sinks raise prices of the targeted goods (Other MMO: Old School RuneScape).** Causal-inference study: an item sink contributed to inflation of luxury-good prices without reducing trade volume; a transaction tax did not meaningfully change trading volume at the tax boundaries. — [Hogan-Hennessy, Xenopoulos & Silva 2022, arXiv:2210.07970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07970) [working paper].

### Inferences
- For the addon, the reliably machine-detectable drivers are (a) the calendar (region-specific reset day, first-full-week-of-month Darkmoon Faire) and (b) discontinuities at maintenance windows (patch/hotfix days). Tagging each hourly snapshot with "hours since last weekly reset" and "is Darkmoon Faire week" is cheap and lets the addon test the community lore on the user's own data instead of assuming it.
- Vendor-price changes in hotfixes move both the "vendor floor" (minimum rational AH price) and, on Classic-style clients, the deposit, because the deposit is a function of vendor price (see section 3). A stored vendor price can therefore go stale after a hotfix.
- Kosminsky's level-share result implies demand for a tier of materials tracks how many characters sit in the level band that consumes it. On a fresh ruleset like Forever this band moves upward week by week, so low-tier material demand should fade and high-tier demand rise as the population levels (consistent with the community week-1/week-2 data in section 2, but not proven by it).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed, official, or primary-data evidence was found for price effects of holidays/world events, PvP season starts, Darkmoon Faire weeks, or season starts/ends. Only the schedules are documented.
- Wowhead "Economy Weekly Wrap-Up" articles (e.g. [#304, 26 Nov 2023, "Soaring Crafting Reagents in 10.2"](https://www.wowhead.com/news/soaring-crafting-reagents-in-10-2-wowhead-economy-weekly-wrap-up-304-336364) and the [10.1 speculation issue, 9 Apr 2023](https://www.wowhead.com/news/10-1-speculation-tsm-groups-and-money-from-engineering-wowhead-economy-weekly-332386)) would be the natural community source for patch-by-patch drivers, but only their headline, author (SamadanPlaysWoW) and date were retrievable; the article bodies could not be read, so nothing is cited from them.
- r/woweconomy and The Lazy Goldmaker blog were not reachable/returned no usable result in search; nothing is cited from them.

## 2. Documented time patterns: weekly cycle, daily cycle, expansion/patch lifecycle

### Takeaway
The lifecycle pattern (launch scarcity, then rapid price decay as supply ramps; gold devaluation in the months after an expansion) has peer-reviewed and data-backed support. Weekly and daily price cycles do not: the only empirical checks found are an Other-MMO study that saw "no obvious seasonal effects" in daily indexes and a study showing strong daily periodicity in player counts (activity, not prices).

### Cited Findings
- **Weekly cycle, community lore (Retail-era).** Claimed pattern: reset day (Tue US / Wed EU) is the highest-demand, most volatile day for consumables; "weekends are for manufacturing" because more players are online and more auctions get listed; for stable, well-stocked raw-material markets the weekday matters little; in small markets (glyphs) timing relative to competitors' log-off matters more than the weekday. — [Engadget "Gold Capped", 7 Feb 2012](https://www.engadget.com/2012/02/07/what-days-should-you-buy-or-sell-on-the-auction-house/) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM, dated 2012.
- **The community does not agree on the weekly low.** Search-result extracts of current guide sites give conflicting lows (one says Sunday-Monday, another says mid-week after supply catches up; an older guide says sell Friday/Saturday). These pages were NOT OPENED individually and are listed only to document the disagreement: [auricdb.com](https://auricdb.com/guides/wow-classic-auction-house), [thewowdb.com](https://thewowdb.com/item/), [almarsguides.com](https://almarsguides.com/WoW/Farming/Classic/AuctionHouse/) [community, not opened]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM. A widely repeated figure that Tuesday-evening flask prices are "30-50% above Thursday" appeared only as an unsourced search snippet and should not be used.
- **What data shows about weekly seasonality (Other MMO).** In 3,358 Old School RuneScape Grand Exchange daily price series over 180 days (to 9 Dec 2018), the authors report "no obvious seasonal effects" in quartile price indexes or the top-100 index, by visual inspection only (no formal weekday test). — [Scholten, Cowling, Hawick & Walker 2019, "Unconventional Exchange: Methods for Statistical Analysis of Virtual Goods", IEEE (conference not named on the page), arXiv:1905.06721](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.06721) [peer-reviewed conference paper / arXiv]. Caveat: index-level, daily data, different game with no weekly raid lockout.
- **Daily cycle of player activity (Other MMO).** Per-second server logs of a Chinese MMORPG show the number of online avatars has one-day periodic behaviour and a clear intraday pattern. — [Jiang, Ren, Gu, Tan & Zhou 2010, *Physica A* 389, 807-814; arXiv:0904.4827](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.4827) [peer-reviewed]. This supports a daily cycle in the number of buyers/sellers online, not directly in prices.
- **Expansion lifecycle: gold devalues after launch (Retail, Legion).** WoW Token gold price was "quite steady" before summer 2016, dipped briefly, then started rising around end of September 2016, about one month after Legion's 30 Aug 2016 launch. NA: 37,971 g (16 Aug 2016) → 101,042 g (25 Apr 2017), ×2.66. EU: 71,316 g → 194,164 g, ×2.72. China ×2.91, Korea ×2.61, Taiwan ×1.54. Real-world FX moves explained only −0.06 to +0.11 of those multipliers. — [Holm & Mäkinen 2018, DOI 10.5171/2018.672253](https://ibimapublishing.com/articles/JISNVC/2018/672253/672253.pdf) [peer-reviewed; token data from wowtoken.info].
- **Expansion as an aggregate shock: realm price gaps open, then close (Retail-era, Cataclysm).** Across eight parallel WoW realms, price levels stay within a computed "band of inaction" and are highly correlated; the data span the Cataclysm launch, and when price levels diverge, arbitrage opportunities arise and the differentials quickly become mean-reverting. — [Morrison & Fontenla 2017, "Purchasing power parity across eight worlds", *Economics Letters* 158, 62-66, DOI 10.1016/j.econlet.2017.06.031](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v158y2017icp62-66.html) [peer-reviewed; abstract only].
- **Launch decay curve, fresh economy (Forever, Nov 2026).** Between scans on 7 Nov and 12 Nov 2026 (launch was 4 Nov), 34 of 46 tracked materials got cheaper, the median item fell 41%, and listed units rose 63% (1.21M → 1.97M). Largest drops: Goldthorn −73%, Runecloth −72%, Liferoot −67%, Kingsblood −66%, Mithril Bar −54%, Iron Ore −53%. Risers were the lowest-tier materials: Copper Bar +93%, Peacebloom +80%, Earthroot +73%, Copper Ore +41%. RP / RP-PvP ruleset premiums over Normal shrank from 1.63× / 1.88× to 1.11× / 1.22×. Data: per-item medians from AH Ledger (player addon scans, 16 markets). — [timesaver.gg, published 7 Nov, updated 12 Nov 2026](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-auction-house-prices-week-1) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM: the publisher sells gold and boosting (conflict of interest), early listing counts were thin for some items, and the underlying AH Ledger page could not be opened.
- **Patch-day spikes are short-lived (Retail-era, Cataclysm).** Gem prices spiked sharply at patch 4.1 from a temporary supply shortage and the rise was not permanent; in 4.2, tailors who stockpiled Dreamcloth for a new bag pattern oversupplied it and first-wave bags sold below material cost. — [Engadget "Gold Capped", 26 Sep 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011/09/26/gold-capped-preparing-for-and-making-money-off-of-patch-4-3/) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM, dated 2011.

### Inferences
- The addon should not hard-code "Tuesday is expensive". Because the evidence is contradictory, a per-item weekday/hour index computed from the user's own hourly history (e.g. median price by hour-of-week divided by the trailing 2-4 week median, shown only when it is stable across at least 3-4 weeks) is the defensible design. Bucketing must be relative to the region's reset (US Tuesday, EU Wednesday), not to calendar weekday.
- The Forever week-1 → week-2 numbers (median −41% in five days, +63% listed units) and the Legion token series (flat, then ×2.7 in ~7 months) describe two different lifecycle effects that both matter for stored history: goods prices fall fast as supply ramps on a fresh economy, while the general gold price level rises for months after an expansion. Long-window averages (14-day+ "market value" style) will lag badly in both regimes; a "regime break" reset of the baseline at launch/patch dates is advisable.
- The risers in the Forever data are bottom-tier materials while mid/high-tier materials fell. That is consistent with gatherers moving up tiers as they level (supply of low-tier goods dries up while profession-levelling demand persists). This is an inference from one community dataset.
- Daily player-count periodicity plus hourly AH snapshots mean the addon can measure, per item, whether listing counts and minimum price co-move with hour of day; there is no published benchmark to compare against.

### Gaps
- No published dataset analysis of WoW weekday or hour-of-day price effects was found (searched academic and data-site sources). Undermine Exchange exposes ~14 days of hourly history and long daily history per item (see section 3), so the test is feasible, but nobody appears to have published it.
- No sourced decay-curve parameters (half-life of launch prices) for Midnight, The War Within, Classic Anniversary phases, or end-of-expansion lulls/pre-patch periods. Pre-patch and "end-of-expansion lull" behaviour is community lore for which I found no openable source with numbers.
- Castronova et al. 2009 ("As real as real? Macroeconomic behavior in a large-scale virtual world", *New Media & Society*) reportedly documents >50% inflation in five months on EverQuest II and a new server converging to the aggregates of existing ones; I saw this only in secondary coverage and did NOT open the paper, so it is a lead, not a cited finding.

## 3. Fixed market frictions: cut, deposits, durations, undercut rules, market scope

### Takeaway
Retail: 5% cut, deposit = 15/30/60% of vendor sell price for 12/24/48 h (1 silver minimum), prices in whole silver, commodities are one region-wide order book bought cheapest-first by quantity, everything else is per connected realm. Classic-style clients (Era, Forever) use 2/8/24 h listings with a deposit based on vendor price and a 5% faction / 15% neutral cut according to community sources; the exact Classic and Forever numbers have conflicts and should be read from the client rather than hard-coded.

### Cited Findings
**Cut and payout**
- (Retail) The house takes 5% of the winning bid: amount received = winning bid × 0.95 + deposit refund. Cancelling an auction that has a bid costs the deposit plus the cut of the current bid. — [warcraft.wiki.gg, Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki; page is flagged outdated by the wiki itself].
- (Retail) Since patch 6.0.2 (14 Oct 2014) Alliance, Horde and neutral listings are combined per realm, so there is no separate neutral-AH cut on retail. — [warcraft.wiki.gg, Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki].
- (Classic / Forever) Faction AH cut 5%, neutral AH (Gadgetzan, Booty Bay, Everlook) 15%. — [ConquestCapped WoW Forever AH guide, updated 16 Nov 2026](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community; says fee tables were checked in the Forever client on 13 Nov 2026]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM. Conflict: a player in a Blizzard bug thread computes Forever proceeds as "AH price × 0.9 − 1 silver" (i.e. a 10% tax) — [Blizzard forums, user Swiftkill, 13 Nov 2026](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/bug-auction-house-deposit-price/2411025) [community]. The Blizzard support article that should settle this ([article 180541, "How is the auction house fee calculated?"](https://eu.support.blizzard.com/en/article/180541)) redirects to a login wall and could NOT be opened.
- (All) The WoW Token is exempt: no cut and no deposit. — [warcraft.wiki.gg, WoW Token](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/WoW_Token) [reference wiki].

**Deposits**
- (Retail) Deposit is a percentage of vendor sell price (VSP) by duration: 12 h = 15%, 24 h = 30%, 48 h = 60%; minimum 1 silver (also when the item has no VSP). Refunded on sale, lost on expiry or cancel. — [warcraft.wiki.gg, Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki; outdated flag].
- (Retail) The client exposes the deposit directly: `C_AuctionHouse.CalculateCommodityDeposit(itemID, duration, quantity)` and `CalculateItemDeposit(item, duration, quantity)`, duration 1/2/3 = 12/24/48 h, added in 8.3.0; the wiki does not document the underlying formula. — [warcraft.wiki.gg API page](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.CalculateCommodityDeposit) [reference wiki].
- (Retail, history) Blizzard stated in Aug 2018 that deposits are based on vendor price, that trade goods therefore have very small deposits, and that it would raise the deposit on some profession materials on a per-stack basis to discourage hundreds of single-item listings; deposits stay refunded on sale. — [Blizzard forums, Ythisens, "Auction House Update", 22 Aug 2018](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/auction-house-update/10385) [official]. Dated: pre-8.3; the 8.3 commodity system later removed stack listings.
- (Classic / Forever) Durations are 2, 8 or 24 hours; faction-AH deposit is 5% / 20% / 60% of vendor price respectively (i.e. 2.5% of vendor price per hour), neutral AH "five times that". — [ConquestCapped, 16 Nov 2026](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community; the guide itself says the formula is not confirmed by Blizzard]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM. A search extract of the Wowhead Classic guide by TheLazyGoldmaker (updated 14 Jul 2021) gives the same 5/20/60% faction schedule but 75/150/300% for neutral, which is not 5× of 5/20/60 (it is 5× of 15/30/60) — the two sources conflict on neutral deposits, and the guide body itself could NOT be opened: [Wowhead Classic AH guide](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/guide/classic-auction-house-economy) [community, body not readable].
- (Forever) Minimum deposit is 1 silver per auction at launch. Blizzard listed a reduction to 1 copper in its 10 Nov 2026 hotfix notes, then removed the AH changes from that update. — reported by [ConquestCapped](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) and [Bind on Forever](https://bindonforever.com/articles/wow-forever-auction-house-changes-november-17) [community]; players confirmed 1 silver was still charged on 11-13 Nov on client 1.60.1.70938 — [Blizzard forums bug thread](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/bug-auction-house-deposit-price/2411025) [community]. Official confirmation of the delay: "Auction House changes" were temporarily removed and "will go live with scheduled weekly maintenance on November 17" — [Blizzard forums, Kaivax, 12 Nov 2026](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-hotfixes-november-11-2026/2403040/7) [official]. Whether the 1-copper minimum is actually live after 17/18 Nov was not confirmed by any source I could open.
- (Forever) Worked example of why the minimum matters: a Shadowgem listed at 2.68 s nets about 1.41 s after fees vs 2.5 s from a vendor. — [Blizzard forums, user Swiftkill, 13 Nov 2026](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/bug-auction-house-deposit-price/2411025) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM.

**Durations, time-left bands, mail**
- (Retail) Listing durations are 12, 24 or 48 hours (the duration set changed in patch 2.3.0, 13 Nov 2007). Time-left bands shown to buyers: Short < 30 min, Medium 30 min-2 h, Long 2-12 h, Very Long 12-48 h. Late bids extend an auction to the minimum of the next band. — [warcraft.wiki.gg, Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki; outdated flag].
- (Retail) Sale proceeds arrive by mail with a one-hour delay (since patch 2.2.0). — [warcraft.wiki.gg, Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House) [reference wiki]. (Forever) Intended delay is also 60 minutes; some mail arrived in ~2 minutes at launch as a bug. — [Bind on Forever](https://bindonforever.com/articles/wow-forever-auction-house-changes-november-17) [community, paraphrasing Blizzard's 10 Nov notes]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM for Forever.

**Listing, pricing and undercut rules**
- (Retail, since 8.3, Jan 2020) Commodities (stackable items) are no longer sold in stacks: the seller sets a unit price, the buyer picks a quantity, and the AH automatically fills it from the lowest-priced auctions. Prices can only be set in silver and gold, not copper. Non-commodity items keep bid, buyout and duration. — [Blizzard news, "Visions of N'Zoth: Auction House Update Preview", 9 Jan (2020)](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23236723) [official].
- (Retail) Among commodity listings at the same unit price, the most recently posted sells first ("first in, last out"), which makes copper/silver undercutting pointless. — player comment in [Blizzard forums 8.3 preview thread](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/news-visions-of-n%E2%80%99zoth-auction-house-update-preview/405423) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM: the official preview text does not state the tie-break rule.
- (Forever) Stackables are described as sold per unit with cheapest units filled first; gear is listed per item. — [ConquestCapped](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM.

**Market scope**
- (Retail, since 9.2.7, 16 Aug 2022) Commodity listings (gems, herbs, flasks, consumables, etc.) are shared by all realms in a region; non-commodities such as armor and weapons remain realm-specific. — [Blizzard news, 9.2.7 notes](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23833174) [official].
- (Retail) All realms in a connected-realm group share a single auction house (connections began Sep/Oct 2013). — [warcraft.wiki.gg, Connected Realms](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Connected_Realms) [reference wiki].
- (Forever) There are no realms: one AH per region, ruleset (Normal, PvP, RP, RP-PvP) and faction; capital-city AHs were tested in-game as not cross-faction; Horde/Alliance trade only via neutral AHs; Oceanic shares with US. — [ConquestCapped, in-game test 16 Nov 2026](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM.

**Data access constraints (relevant to snapshot design)**
- (Retail) Blizzard's web API publishes auction data as snapshots that update hourly: `/data/wow/connected-realm/{id}/auctions` (non-commodities per connected realm) and `/data/wow/auctions/commodities` (whole region). — [Blizzard API forum, Korakk, 18 Aug 2022](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/blizzard/t/immediate-change-to-auction-apis-for-commodities-with-927/31522) [official].
- (Retail) Snapshots are "roughly hourly, but it's not guaranteed", especially around weekly downtime; the data contain listings, quantities and prices only — no sales. Undermine Exchange keeps ~14 days of hourly history and long-run daily history, and covers retail only. — [Undermine Exchange API docs](https://undermine.exchange/api.html) [tool docs / primary data site].
- (Retail, in-game) `C_AuctionHouse.ReplicateItems()` (full AH dump) has a 15-minute account-wide throttle between successful calls; added in 8.3.0 replacing the old "getall" `QueryAuctionItems()`. — [warcraft.wiki.gg API page](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.ReplicateItems) [reference wiki].
- (Forever) Blizzard publishes no price API for Forever; third-party data come from player addon scans. — [timesaver.gg](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-auction-house-prices-week-1) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM. TradeSkillMaster's public data files list Retail, Classic, Mists of Pandaria and Forever as covered game versions (realm/commodity files change every few hours, region files daily; prices in copper). — [TradeSkillMaster public data](https://tradeskillmaster.com/public-data) [tool docs].

### Inferences
- Break-even for a flip or craft at a faction/retail AH with cut c and deposit D: sell price must exceed (cost + D × expected number of failed postings) / (1 − c). With c = 5% a flip needs > 5.3% gross margin before deposits. On Classic-style clients the deposit scales at 2.5% of vendor price per hour (5% per 2 h) vs 1.25% per hour on retail (15% per 12 h), so on Classic/Forever items with a high vendor-price-to-market-price ratio (crafted armor, weapons, some trade goods) can lose most of their margin to one or two failed 24 h posts. A "deal" score should subtract cut and expected deposit loss, not just compare to market value.
- Because Classic/Forever deposits and the 1 silver/1 copper minimum are in flux and community sources conflict (5% vs an alleged 10%; neutral 5× vs other schedules), the addon should treat cut and deposit as data: read the deposit shown by the client when the AH is open where the API allows, and keep the cut percentage a per-flavor constant that is easy to change.
- Retail commodity prices are quantized to 1 silver and same-price ordering reportedly favours the newest listing, so "undercut by 1 copper" logic from Classic does not transfer; on retail the meaningful signal is the depth of the cheapest price levels (how many units sit within x% of the minimum), which hourly snapshots capture.
- Snapshot data never contain sales. Any "sold" or "sale rate" statistic must be inferred from listings that disappear before their time-left band would expire, which is exactly the limitation the data sites document; time-left bands (Short/Medium/Long/Very Long) are the only expiry signal available.
- Retail has two market scopes that need separate handling in history storage: region-wide commodities (one price for everyone, heavy competition) and per-connected-realm items (thin, realm-specific). Forever's single AH per region/ruleset/faction behaves like one very large Classic realm.

### Gaps
- No openable official statement of the Classic-era or Forever deposit formula or of the Forever cut. Blizzard's support article on fee calculation is behind a login redirect.
- The retail commodity deposit formula (whether quantity scales it linearly, any cap) is not documented on the wiki API page; the 2018 per-stack surcharge predates the 8.3 redesign and its current status is unknown.
- Listing durations and deposit schedule for Classic Anniversary / TBC Anniversary / MoP Classic clients were not confirmed from any source I could open (the 12/24/48 h set dates from the original patch 2.3.0, so TBC-and-later Classic clients presumably use it — unconfirmed).
- The LIFO tie-break for same-price retail commodities rests on a single player comment.

## 4. Inflation, gold sinks, the WoW Token, bots and bans

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed and thesis evidence agrees that the gold price level is driven mainly by operator decisions (expansions and patches that raise gold income) and that the token's gold price is a usable inflation gauge; the token price is set by Blizzard's supply/demand algorithm, not by players. Blizzard documents large ban waves and a 2023 token purchase restriction, but I found no rigorous measurement of what bans do to item prices in WoW.

### Cited Findings
- **Token mechanics (Retail).** Launched 7 Apr 2015 (Americas, starting at 30,000 g) and 21 Apr 2015 (Europe, 35,000 g). The gold value is "determined dynamically based on supply and demand"; players cannot set prices; the quoted amount is locked in at listing; no AH cut or deposit. Real-money price $20 / €20. Convertible to Battle.net Balance since Feb 2017 ($15 / €13). — [warcraft.wiki.gg, WoW Token](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/WoW_Token) [reference wiki].
- **Token availability by flavor.** Added to Wrath of the Lich King Classic on 23 May 2023 (game time only). Not available in Classic Era, Hardcore, Season of Discovery or the Classic Anniversary branch (exception: Chinese Anniversary realms from 13 Feb 2025). — [warcraft.wiki.gg, WoW Token](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/WoW_Token) [reference wiki; the page has an internal date inconsistency in its patch-changes list]. Token status in WoW Forever was not found in any source.
- **Token as an inflation gauge; expansion effect (Retail, Legion).** Token gold price ×2.66 (NA) and ×2.72 (EU) between 16 Aug 2016 and 25 Apr 2017; USD 1 bought ~1,899 g (NA) before and ~5,052 g after. The authors attribute the rise to increased money supply after Legion plus the token's new Battle.net Balance use. — [Holm & Mäkinen 2018, DOI 10.5171/2018.672253](https://ibimapublishing.com/articles/JISNVC/2018/672253/672253.pdf) [peer-reviewed; descriptive].
- **Gold faucets and sinks.** Gold enters via quests, vendor sales, dungeon/raid rewards and loot; it leaves via payments to NPC services such as repairs, and when players quit. Each expansion raised quest and dungeon gold rewards ("expansionist monetary policy"). — [Holm & Mäkinen 2018](https://ibimapublishing.com/articles/JISNVC/2018/672253/672253.pdf) [peer-reviewed].
- **Token price all-time high (Retail, US).** Wowhead reported the NA token breaking 400k gold on 9 Oct 2023. — [Wowhead news headline, 9 Oct 2023](https://www.wowhead.com/news/wow-token-price-breaks-400k-all-time-high-in-north-america-335416) [community; only headline/date were readable]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM for the exact level.
- **Token purchase restriction (Retail, effective 21 Nov 2023).** Accounts that have not paid real money for at least 30 days of game time since 2017 can no longer buy a token with gold; token-granted game time does not count toward qualifying. — [Blizzard forums, Kaivax, "Restriction on Purchase of WoW Token – November 2023", 17 Nov 2023](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/1714609) [official]. This removes gold-funded bot accounts as token buyers (the motive is my reading; the post states the rule).
- **Ban-wave scale (Classic, 2020).** Over 74,000 WoW accounts in the Americas, Oceania and Europe were closed or suspended in one month for EULA violations, most for gameplay automation used to farm resources; Blizzard says real-money trading motivates the activity and bans are "simply a cost of doing business" for those operators. — [Blizzard forums, Kaivax, "Actions Taken to Address Exploitative Gameplay", 17 Jun 2020](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/actions-taken-to-address-exploitative-gameplay/558339) [official].
- **RMT links the gold price level to real-world factors.** Real-money trade strongly influences the WoW price level; long-run inflation is tied to the EUR/CNY exchange rate (gold-farming cost base) and operator patches. — [Skuhrovec 2009 thesis, abstract](https://invenio.nusl.cz/record/277053?ln=en) [working paper/thesis]. Dated 2009.
- **Virtual-economy inflation is uneven across price tiers (Other MMO).** Old School RuneScape six-month inflation by price quartile: lower −0.18%, lower-mid +4.91%, upper-mid +11.97%, upper −5.30%; volume-weighted top-100 index +9.81%. The top 20 items made up 69.7% of top-100 traded volume. — [Scholten et al. 2019, arXiv:1905.06721](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.06721) [peer-reviewed conference paper / arXiv]. The paper's stationarity table is internally inconsistent; use only the descriptive numbers.
- **Taxes and sinks (Other MMO).** A transaction tax did not meaningfully reduce trading volume; an item sink raised luxury-good prices; the illicit gold market was relatively unaffected by either. — [Hogan-Hennessy et al. 2022, arXiv:2210.07970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07970) [working paper].
- **Faucet increase by hotfix (Forever).** Most quests level 48+ now additionally reward gold (10 Nov 2026 hotfix). — [Blizzard forums, Kaivax, 11 Nov 2026](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-hotfixes-november-11-2026/2403040/6) [official].

### Inferences
- Stored price history should be deflatable. On retail the token gold price (readable in-game) is the only continuous, operator-computed index of gold's value and has peer-reviewed precedent as an inflation proxy; "market movers" computed in nominal gold will otherwise flag the whole market during post-launch inflation. Where no token exists (Classic Era, Anniversary, probably Forever), a basket index of high-volume commodities is the substitute; the RuneScape result (top 20 items ≈ 70% of volume) suggests a small basket suffices.
- Bans remove supply of botted raw materials and RMT gold at the same time; the direction of the net price effect on any item is therefore not obvious in advance, and no WoW measurement exists. The addon should treat sudden supply drops in heavily-farmed raw materials (listing count falls sharply while price jumps, outside patch days) as a detectable event without claiming a cause.
- The 5% cut is itself a gold sink proportional to traded value; the RuneScape tax study suggests such a tax does not suppress volume much, so volume changes around fee changes (e.g. Forever's deposit minimum) are more likely to appear in low-value items, where the fixed minimum deposit dominated, than across the board.

### Gaps
- No rigorous (peer-reviewed or primary-data) study of bot-ban effects on WoW item prices or token price. Community reports that the Taiwan token fell from ~1.25M to ~592K gold after Sept 2023 anti-bot measures and that the US token range fell after the Nov 2023 restriction come from Icy Veins forum posts that returned HTTP 403 ([link](https://www.icy-veins.com/forums/topic/76144-wow-token-prices-are-down-after-blizzards-anti-bot-measures/)) — NOT OPENED, not usable as cited fact.
- No verified current (Nov 2026) token price or Midnight-era token history; a search snippet mentioned ~220k gold on US in April 2026 from an unidentified site (not opened).
- Midnight gold sinks: Wowhead ran "Repair Bills are Becoming Increasingly Expensive in Midnight" on 20 May 2026 ([link](https://www.wowhead.com/news/repair-bills-are-becoming-increasingly-expensive-in-midnight-381679)), but only the headline was readable; no figures.
- Later ban figures (e.g. ~120,000 Wrath Classic accounts in 2023; Forever gold-buyer bans in Oct 2026) appeared only in news search results that I did not open.
- Other-MMO news reports of botted-item prices rising 40-90% after mass bans (Old School RuneScape, 2026) were seen only as search snippets; not opened.

## 5. Speculation on patch notes / datamining and "investing" before patches

### Takeaway
The mechanism is well described by community writers (buy or craft ahead of a known demand or supply change, sell into the patch) and two things are consistently reported: the spike is short, and crowded speculation erases or inverts the profit. The best-evidenced concrete cases are announced operator changes whose price effect shows up in data (token Feb 2017, Legion launch) and documented recipe/cost changes (Midnight 12.1.5).

### Cited Findings
- **Announced function change → immediate price jump (Retail, Feb 2017).** When tokens became convertible to Battle.net Balance, token gold prices on Western servers showed a sudden upward shift on Tuesday 7 Feb 2017 (Korea dropped the same day; Taiwan stayed flat). — [Holm & Mäkinen 2018](https://ibimapublishing.com/articles/JISNVC/2018/672253/672253.pdf) [peer-reviewed].
- **Expansion launch → delayed devaluation of gold (Retail, 2016).** Token price began rising about a month after Legion's launch and reached ×2.7 within ~8 months of the pre-patch event. — [Holm & Mäkinen 2018](https://ibimapublishing.com/articles/JISNVC/2018/672253/672253.pdf) [peer-reviewed].
- **Speculation is self-limiting.** Buying before a patch raises pre-patch prices and selling after it lowers post-patch prices, so the gap shrinks as more players speculate. — [Engadget "Gold Capped", 12 May 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011/05/12/gold-capped-how-to-profit-from-every-single-new-patch/) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM.
- **Worked historical examples (Retail-era, Cataclysm; all UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIMS).** Patch 4.1: gem prices spiked sharply on a temporary supply shortage and fell back; belt buckles sold out for days; the author's intellect gems sold about every 15 minutes. Patch 4.2: datamined craftables gave weeks of notice, but tailors who stockpiled Dreamcloth for the Luxurious Silk Gem Bag oversupplied the market and bags sold below material cost. Patch 4.3 (forecasts at the time, not outcomes): Heavenly Shards expected up (no new source), Maelstrom Crystals expected down (more disenchantable epics). — [Engadget, 12 May 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011/05/12/gold-capped-how-to-profit-from-every-single-new-patch/); [Engadget, 26 Sep 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011/09/26/gold-capped-preparing-for-and-making-money-off-of-patch-4-3/) [community].
- **Datamined/PTR recipe-cost change (Retail, Midnight 12.1.5, live 14 Oct 2026).** Decor recipe material costs cut by up to 80% (lumber 40 → 8 on several recipes), known from PTR before release. — [PC Games (DE), 13 Oct 2026](https://www.pcgames.de/World-of-Warcraft-Spiel-42971/News/Dekorationen-guenstiger-Housing-1556580/) [community/news]. No price series was found for the affected materials.
- **Pre-patch collector demand (Retail, Midnight pre-patch, Feb 2026).** Grey "Frayed" gear pieces dropping in the pre-launch event sold for up to 25,000 gold, with one hood reported at up to 50,000 gold, because they belong to a new appearance set; prices varied by realm. — [PC Games (DE), 4 Feb 2026](https://www.pcgames.de/World-of-Warcraft-Spiel-42971/Tipps/WoW-Reich-durch-Pre-Patch-Gear-diese-grauen-Items-sind-viel-Gold-wert-1494751/) [community/news; the 50k figure is sourced to a social media post]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM.
- **Structural rule changes as "patch events" (Retail).** 8.3 (Jan 2020): unsold commodity listings were force-returned before maintenance and their deposits not refunded. 9.2.7 (Aug 2022): commodity markets merged region-wide. — [Blizzard 8.3 preview](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23236723); [Blizzard 9.2.7 notes](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23833174) [official].
- **Cross-market divergences close quickly.** When realm price levels diverge after a shock, arbitrage opportunities arise and differentials mean-revert quickly. — [Morrison & Fontenla 2017, DOI 10.1016/j.econlet.2017.06.031](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v158y2017icp62-66.html) [peer-reviewed; abstract only].

### Inferences
- A snapshot addon can detect speculation footprints without reading patch notes: (1) rising price together with falling listed quantity in the weeks before a maintenance day (accumulation), (2) a jump in listed quantity plus a price drop in the first hours to days after it (distribution / oversupply, as in the Dreamcloth case). Flagging "listed quantity up > X% and price down > Y% since last reset" is a direct oversupply warning for crafters.
- Since spikes are short and crowded trades invert, a "market movers" view should show time since the move started and current listed depth relative to the pre-move baseline, not only the percentage change.
- For region-wide retail commodities there is no cross-realm arbitrage left after 9.2.7; the convergence literature now applies only to realm-specific items (gear, pets) and to Classic-style realms.

### Gaps
- No price-series evidence (data site or study) was retrieved for any Midnight, The War Within or Dragonflight speculation case; modern examples in Wowhead economy posts and creator content (Samadan, Studen Albatroz, Manthieus) could not be read.
- No source quantifies typical pre-patch run-up or post-patch decay magnitudes or durations.
- No openable source on Classic phase-release speculation (e.g. stockpiling for a new raid phase or profession recipes on Anniversary realms).

## 6. Which categories behave differently?

### Takeaway
The hard, sourced distinction is mechanical: retail commodities trade in one region-wide, quantity-filled order book, while gear, pets and other non-stackables are thin per-realm markets with bids and buyouts. Evidence on behavioural differences is limited to: high-volume consumable materials are more price-stable on larger markets, mode prices of convertible goods respect conversion ratios, and volume is concentrated in few items; everything else is community lore.

### Cited Findings
- **Commodities vs items (Retail).** Stackables: unit price, quantity purchase, cheapest-first fill, region-wide since 9.2.7. Non-commodities (armor, weapons): bid/buyout per listing, realm-specific. — [Blizzard 8.3 preview](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23236723); [Blizzard 9.2.7 notes](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23833174) [official].
- **Battle pets and gear variants need their own keys (Retail).** Data sites track pets by species (and breed), and gear by item level and suffix, separately from plain item IDs; realm-level "now/hourly/daily" series exist per variant. — [Undermine Exchange API docs](https://undermine.exchange/api.html) [tool docs]. TSM's pet files list base species only. — [TradeSkillMaster public data](https://tradeskillmaster.com/public-data) [tool docs].
- **Market size and price stability (Retail-era, 2009).** For Netherweave Cloth over 30 days, the standard deviation of price averaged 0.895 on high-population servers vs 1.62 on low-population servers; medium-population servers were, unexpectedly, the least stable (2.35). Servers within a size pair differed by 12.9% on average. — [Kosminsky 2009, JVWR 2(4)](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/download/869/634) [peer-reviewed; six servers].
- **Convertible goods hold their conversion ratio at the mode, not the mean (Retail-era, 2009).** Lesser vs Greater Planar Essence (3:1): mode prices were exactly 5.00 g and 15.00 g; mean prices implied a roughly 5% profit from splitting Greater into Lesser (6.02 g vs 17.12 g), explained by level-70 players not bothering to convert. — [Kosminsky 2009](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/download/869/634) [peer-reviewed].
- **Prices converge across parallel realms.** Price levels converged in all eight WoW realms studied (Phillips-Sul test and a band-of-inaction test). — [Morrison & Fontenla 2013, *Empirical Economics* 44(3), 1053-1064, DOI 10.1007/s00181-012-0567-3](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/empeco/v44y2013i3p1053-1064.html) [peer-reviewed; abstract via RePEc — the Springer page itself redirected to a login].
- **Volume concentration (Other MMO).** Top 20 items = 69.7% of top-100 traded volume; most items are not rising or falling at alarming rates, and rising and falling items are roughly balanced. — [Scholten et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.06721) [peer-reviewed conference paper / arXiv].
- **Luxury goods respond to sinks (Other MMO).** An operator item sink raised luxury-good prices. — [Hogan-Hennessy et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07970) [working paper].
- **Stable vs volatile vs small markets (Retail-era, 2012).** Well-stocked raw materials/gems (Inferno Ruby) barely react to weekly supply swings; raid consumables with thin supply (Volcanic Potion on one realm) swing around reset and can hit zero stock; small markets like glyphs are driven by competitor timing. — [Engadget, 7 Feb 2012](https://www.engadget.com/2012/02/07/what-days-should-you-buy-or-sell-on-the-auction-house/) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM.
- **Per-patch category response (Retail-era, 2011).** Enchanting materials: demand up, post-patch price drops unlikely; gems: fast spike; item enhancements: small bump because materials are shared; new craftable gear: sells out for weeks; glyphs: move with class changes. — [Engadget, 12 May 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011/05/12/gold-capped-how-to-profit-from-every-single-new-patch/) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM.
- **Transmog/collector items (Retail, 2026).** Grey appearance pieces from the Midnight pre-patch event reached 25k-50k gold with realm-to-realm variation. — [PC Games (DE), 4 Feb 2026](https://www.pcgames.de/World-of-Warcraft-Spiel-42971/Tipps/WoW-Reich-durch-Pre-Patch-Gear-diese-grauen-Items-sind-viel-Gold-wert-1494751/) [community]. UNVERIFIED COMMUNITY CLAIM.
- **WoW Token is a category of its own.** Operator-set price, no cut, no deposit, not resellable after a gold purchase. — [warcraft.wiki.gg, WoW Token](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/WoW_Token) [reference wiki].
- **Buyers and sellers price consistently in gold.** A Stanford honors thesis on WoW auctions concludes both sides value the currency and price items consistently, and that the auction mechanism differs enough from eBay that eBay techniques do not carry over. — [Marks 2009, "Price Dynamics in Virtual World Auctions", Stanford Economics honors thesis](https://purl.stanford.edu/pd000kr9463) [working paper/thesis; abstract only].

### Inferences
- Statistic choice should differ by category. For deep markets (retail commodities, Classic cloth/herbs/ore) the mode or a low-quantile price is the robust "market price" (Kosminsky's mode result: the typical trade obeys the 3:1 ratio while the mean is distorted by outliers). For thin per-realm markets (BoE gear, transmog, pets) the minimum buyout is often a single seller's opinion; a "deal" needs a longer history window and a minimum number of distinct observations before it is trusted.
- Conversion relationships (essences 3:1, bars from ore, bolts from cloth, and in general any vendor- or craft-linked pair) give the addon cheap, always-valid arbitrage checks on Classic/Forever: compare mode/low-quantile prices of both sides net of the 5% cut and deposit.
- Larger markets are more stable for staple commodities, so volatility thresholds for "market mover" alerts should scale with market depth (listing count / quantity), not be a fixed percentage; Forever's single huge AH per faction should look more like the high-population case.
- The "medium population is least stable" result is from six servers and is explained by the author only via speculation about self-selection; do not build on it.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence (any tier) was found on price behaviour of profession-cooldown items (e.g. Classic Arcanite/Mooncloth-style cooldown goods), vendor-recipe flipping, limited-supply vendor items, or mounts. These remain unsupported.
- No data-backed comparison of BoE gear price decay over a raid tier or season, or of pet/transmog price persistence, was found.
- Kosminsky 2009 and Morrison & Fontenla 2013/2017 use TBC/Wrath/Cataclysm-era realm data from before connected realms, cross-faction AHs and region-wide commodities; their mechanisms are plausible today but the magnitudes are dated.
