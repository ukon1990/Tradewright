# Profession- and crafting-based gold-making strategies in World of Warcraft (as of November 2026)

Scope: how players earn gold by transforming items (crafting, converting, destroying), written so that an addon that already knows recipes, reagent lists and auction prices can rank opportunities. Covers retail (Midnight 12.x), Classic-style clients and the "WoW Forever" client where sources exist.

Source tiers used below: **[peer-reviewed]**, **[working paper]**, **[official]** (Blizzard), **[primary data]** (game-data extracts / database sites built from client data), **[reference wiki]** (warcraft.wiki.gg), **[tool docs]**, **[tool source]** (addon source code, read directly), **[community]** (guides, columns, commercial blogs). Claims resting only on [community] sources are marked *unverified community claim*. Every link below was opened and checked unless it is listed under "Gaps" as not opened.

Version labels: **Retail** = Dragonflight/The War Within/Midnight profession system (10.0+); **Classic** = Classic Era / progression Classic rules; **Forever** = WoW Forever client; **Historical** = no longer true on live retail.

Note on TSM source: current TradeSkillMaster (4.x) source was not found in a public repository. The TSM code cited here is a public fork of legacy TSM 2.8.3 (3.3.5-era code base). It shows how TSM has computed these values historically and matches the current official docs where both exist, but current TSM may differ in detail.

## 1. How is craft profit computed correctly, and what do TSM, CraftSim and Auctionator compute?

### Takeaway
All three tools use the same skeleton: profit = (expected output quantity x sale price x 0.95) - sum(reagent quantity x cheapest reagent price) - optional deposit. CraftSim adds expected-value terms for multicraft, resourcefulness, quality and concentration (retail only); TSM adds recursive "cheapest of buy / craft / vendor / convert" material pricing and restock gating; the Auctionator-ecosystem code only sums reagent cost with vendor price taking precedence.

### Cited Findings

**Auction house fees (all clients unless noted)**
- The auction house keeps 5% of the winning bid; the wiki page lists no separate neutral rate after Patch 6.0.2 merged the houses per realm. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House)
- Deposit = a percentage of the item's vendor sell price by duration: 12 h = 15%, 24 h = 30%, 48 h = 60%; minimum deposit 1 silver (also for items with vendor price 0). — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House)
- On a successful sale the deposit is returned: "Amount Received = Winning Bid - Cut + Deposit". If the item expires or is cancelled the deposit is lost. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House)
- Since Patch 9.2.7 (2022-08-16) retail commodities (gems, herbs, flasks, consumables) are region-wide; armor and weapons stay realm-specific. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House)
- The deposit can be read from the client rather than re-derived: `C_AuctionHouse.CalculateItemDeposit(item, duration, quantity)` (duration 1 = 12 h, 2 = 24 h, 3 = 48 h) is listed for the retail (12.1.5), Forever (1.60.1) and Mists (4.4.2) builds. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: API C_AuctionHouse.CalculateItemDeposit](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.CalculateItemDeposit)

**TradeSkillMaster**
- Official definitions: Crafting Cost is "the sum of the material price of each reagent in a given recipe"; "Crafted Item Value minus Crafting Cost is the profit." The Crafted Item Value can be overridden per operation. — [tool docs] [TSM: Crafting Operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/1236068-tsm-addon-crafting-operations)
- `Crafting` price source = total of `matPrice` of each material; cannot be referenced without knowing the recipe. `matPrice` is set by a default in Crafting options and can be overridden per item. — [tool docs] [TSM: Which Price Sources can I use](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)
- Legacy TSM defaults: `defaultMatCostMethod = "min(dbmarket, crafting, vendorbuy, convert(dbmarket))"` (cheapest of market, craft-it-yourself, vendor, or convert from another item); `defaultCraftPriceMethod = "dbminbuyout"`; `profitPercent = 0`; `ignoreCDCraftCost = true`. — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [TradeSkillMaster_Crafting.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting.lua)
- Legacy TSM cost algorithm: for each recipe that makes the item, cost = sum(matQuantity x matCost) / numResult; the lowest-cost recipe wins; a recipe is invalid if any material has no price; recipes with a cooldown are skipped as a cost basis when another recipe exists; material cost lookups are recursive with a visited-set guard against cycles. Profit = `buyout - buyout * profitPercent - cost` (so the 5% cut is only deducted if the user sets profitPercent). — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [Cost.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/Modules/Cost.lua)
- `convert()` cost of a target item = min over all source items of price(source) / rate. — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [Conversions.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster/Data/Conversions.lua)
- Price inputs: `DBMarket` = "a weighted 14-day average of the auctions listed" favouring the last 3 days, and reflects listing prices, not sale prices; `DBMinBuyout` = lowest current listing (data may be about 30 minutes old); `DBHistorical` = 60-day average of DBMarket; `DBRegionSaleAvg` = average actual sale/purchase price in the region; `vendorBuy` is only known for items seen at a vendor; `vendorSell` = vendor sale value. — [tool docs] [TSM: Which Price Sources can I use](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)
- Sale-rate inputs: `DBRegionSaleRate` is a decimal (0.4 = sells about 40% of the times it is posted, averaged across the region's auction houses); `DBRegionSoldPerDay` = average units sold per auction house per day in the region; `SaleRate` = the player's own sale rate; `NumExpires` = expirations/cancels since last sale; `NumInventory` = quantity owned. — [tool docs] [TSM: Which Value Sources can I use](https://tradeskillmaster.helpjuice.com/custom-strings/which-value-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)

**CraftSim (retail; source read directly)**
- Feature set: average profit, stat weights (profit per stat point), profit per concentration point, best reagent-quality combination, best profession gear, recipe scan, craft queue, patron-order queueing, customer history, price overrides; price sources TSM, Auctionator, RECrystallize, Oribos Exchange. — [tool docs] [CraftSim on CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/craftsim)
- AH cut constant: `CraftSim.CONST.AUCTION_HOUSE_CUT = 0.95`. — [tool source] [CraftSim Util/Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua)
- Recipes without crafting stats: `profit = price * baseItemAmount * 0.95 - craftingCosts - expectedDeposit`; if the result is a grey (junk) item it is valued at vendor sell price with no AH cut. — [tool source] [CraftSim Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua)
- Recipes with stats: CraftSim enumerates the four outcomes {multicraft yes/no} x {resourcefulness yes/no}, with probability p_MC x p_Res each, and averages. Per outcome: value = price(expected quality) x (items) x 0.95 - deposit; cost = craftingCosts - (saved cost if resourcefulness procs). — [tool source] [CraftSim Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua)
- Rating-to-chance conversion: chance = rating / 1100 for multicraft, rating / 900 for resourcefulness, rating / 1000 for ingenuity; identical tables for Dragonflight, The War Within and Midnight. — [tool source] [CraftSim Util/Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua), [Classes/ProfessionStat.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/ProfessionStat.lua)
- Multicraft model: on a proc, maxExtraItems = mcConstant x baseYield x (1 + specialization bonus); expectedExtraItems = (1 + maxExtraItems) / 2; mcConstant = 2.1 for base yield 1, 1.83 for base yield 2, 1.875 for base yield 5, 2.5 otherwise; labelled in the source as "Approximation based on recipe base yield". Expected yield per craft = baseYield + expectedExtraItems x p_MC. — [tool source] [CraftSim Util/Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua), [Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua), [Classes/ResultData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/ResultData.lua)
- Resourcefulness model: on a proc, saved cost = required-reagent cost x 0.30 x (1 + specialization bonus). The source comments: "this average comes from 20-40% resources saved on proc with a minimum of 1; currently this is just an approximation". Average saved cost per craft = that x p_Res. For work orders only the crafter-supplied reagents count. — [tool source] [CraftSim Util/Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua), [Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua)
- Expected cost per produced item = (craftingCosts - average resourcefulness saving) / expected yield per craft. This is the "craft it yourself" price used for sub-recipes. — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/PriceData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/PriceData.lua)
- Reagent price selection order: manual override, then (if sub-recipe optimisation is on) the crafter's own expected cost per item when it is lower than the AH price or no AH price exists, then AH price. By default sub-recipes on a cooldown and sub-recipes needing concentration are excluded from this substitution. — [tool source] [CraftSim DataSource/PriceSource.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceSource.lua)
- With TSM as price source, CraftSim uses `VendorBuy` whenever TSM knows a vendor price, otherwise the configured expression, default `first(DBRecent, DBMinbuyout)`. With Auctionator it calls `Auctionator.API.v1.GetAuctionPriceByItemID`. — [tool source] [CraftSim DataSource/PriceAPIs.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceAPIs.lua), [Util/Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua)
- Quality reagents: cost is the sum of the allocated quality items; if nothing is allocated the cheapest quality is assumed. The code notes "Midnight reagents only have 2-tier quality" (earlier expansions: 3). — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/PriceData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/PriceData.lua)
- Quality: expected quality is derived from skill against recipe-difficulty thresholds; spending concentration raises the result exactly one tier (`expectedQualityConcentration = min(expectedQuality + 1, maxQuality)`). — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/ResultData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/ResultData.lua)
- Deposit: optional and off by default (`TSM_DEPOSIT_ENABLED = false`); when on it evaluates a user TSM expression (default `vendorsell`) per result item; always 0 for work orders. — [tool source] [CraftSim DataSource/PriceAPIs.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceAPIs.lua), [Util/Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua)
- Sale rate: CraftSim reads TSM `dbregionsalerate`; default thresholds are 0.1 for sending scan results to the craft queue and 0 (off) for the scan itself. — [tool source] [CraftSim DataSource/PriceAPIs.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceAPIs.lua), [Util/Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua), [Modules/RecipeScan/RecipeScan.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Modules/RecipeScan/RecipeScan.lua)
- Recipe scan can sort by profit, relative profit (profit / crafting cost), concentration cost, concentration value, or crafting cost. — [tool source] [CraftSim Modules/RecipeScan/RecipeScan.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Modules/RecipeScan/RecipeScan.lua)

**Auctionator ecosystem**
- CraftInfoAnywhere (a separate tooltip addon that calls the Auctionator price API; Auctionator's own crafting-info module could not be read, see Gaps) computes reagent value as sum(quantity x (vendor price if one exists, else auction price)); on retail it takes the cheapest option among a reagent slot's alternatives (quality tiers). It shows cost only, no profit, no AH cut. — [tool source] [CraftInfoAnywhere Main_Classic.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TheMouseNest/CraftInfoAnywhere/main/Main_Classic.lua), [Main_Mainline.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TheMouseNest/CraftInfoAnywhere/main/Main_Mainline.lua)

**Blizzard's stat definitions (retail)**
- Multicraft: "You have an x% chance to craft additional items. Only works on recipes for stackable items." Resourcefulness: "You have an x% chance to use fewer tradable reagents such as ore." Quality: final skill is compared against recipe difficulty; skill at or above difficulty gives maximum quality. — [official] [Blizzard: Dragonflight: Making It with Professions](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23876529/dragonflight-making-it-with-professions)
- Multicraft and Resourcefulness were added in Patch 10.0.0 (2022-10-25). — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Multicraft](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Multicraft), [Warcraft Wiki: Resourcefulness (attribute)](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Resourcefulness_(attribute))

**Historical practice**
- A 2011 goldmaking column describes using minimum buyout for the selling price (because the seller undercuts the lowest listing) and market value for material cost (because materials are bought days earlier), plus a universal 5% profit reduction for auction fees. *Unverified community claim (practice description).* — [community] [Engadget / WoW Insider, B. Berntsen, "Gold Capped: TradeSkillMaster advanced guide", 2011-03-21](https://www.engadget.com/2011-03-21-gold-capped-tradeskillmaster-advanced-guide.html)

### Inferences
- A defensible ranking formula for one craft, combining the tool logic above:
  - `unitCost(r) = min( vendorBuy(r) if vendor-sold, ahBuy(r), craftCostPerUnit(r) recursively and excluding cooldown-gated recipes, min over conversions of price(source)/rate )`
  - `matCost = sum(qty_r x unitCost(r))`
  - Classic/Forever (no procs): `profit = yield x sellPrice x 0.95 - matCost`
  - Retail: `profit = (baseYield + E[extra] x pMC) x sellPrice(quality) x 0.95 - matCost x (1 - pRes x 0.30 x (1 + bonus)) ` with pMC = rating/1100, pRes = rating/900 (resourcefulness applies to tradable required reagents only).
- Deposit is refunded on sale, so it is only a cost on failed postings. Expected deposit loss per sold unit is approximately `deposit x (1/saleRate - 1)` if each posting sells independently with probability saleRate; for commodity-like crafts with low vendor value this is negligible, for high-vendor-value gear at long durations it is not.
- Legacy TSM's default (profitPercent = 0) does not subtract the 5% cut, and its docs define profit as value minus cost; an addon should subtract the cut explicitly, as CraftSim does.
- Using current minimum buyout as the sale price (TSM legacy default, CraftSim default `first(DBRecent, DBMinbuyout)`) overstates profit when the lowest listing is thin; DBMarket is listing-based, not sale-based. A sale-based price (`DBRegionSaleAvg`) or own sale history is the conservative alternative.
- Time-to-sell is not computed by any of the three tools' source that I read; the closest proxies are TSM's region sale rate and sold-per-day. A simple estimate is `(units listed below your price + your units) / soldPerDay`.
- Classic and Forever clients have no multicraft/resourcefulness/concentration, so the Classic formula is deterministic except for specialization procs (see section 8).

### Gaps
- No game-data (DB2 / wago.tools) source was found for the exact multicraft extra-item distribution or the resourcefulness refund distribution; CraftSim itself labels both constants as approximations. Treat 0.30 and the 2.1/1.83/1.875/2.5 constants as tool estimates, not game constants.
- Current TSM 4.x source is not publicly browsable; the default material-cost string and craft-value method were confirmed only in the legacy 2.8.3 fork.
- Auctionator's own crafting-info module (the profit line in the profession window) could not be read: the main Auctionator repository was not found on GitHub under the organisation or maintainer accounts checked.
- Not opened (HTTP 403): Icy Veins "Professions Stats and Gear Guide for Midnight" (https://icy-veins.com/wow/professions-stats-and-gear). Not opened (TLS error): the r/woweconomy thread on crafting calculations. Community figures seen only in search snippets (e.g. "multicraft 1.25 x", "resourcefulness 0.30 x") are therefore not used as support here.
- Deposit durations and percentages for Classic-style clients (historically 2/8/24 h) were not confirmed from a primary source; the wiki page gives only the 12/24/48 h table.

## 2. Shuffles and destroy chains: how is expected value per input calculated?

### Takeaway
Destroy value is a plain expected value: sum over possible outputs of (expected quantity per input x output price), and a "shuffle" is a chain of such steps where each intermediate is routed to its best exit (auction, vendor, craft-then-disenchant, transmute). Legacy yield tables exist in tool source and on the wiki; no game-data-derived yield tables for current retail were found.

### Cited Findings

**Definitions and tool computation**
- TSM `Destroy` = the average value of the materials received by milling, prospecting or disenchanting an item, priced by default at DBMarket of the outputs (changeable). — [tool docs] [TSM: Which Price Sources can I use](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)
- Legacy TSM code: mill value = sum(price(pigment) x rate); prospect value = sum(price(gem) x rate); disenchant value = sum(price(material) x amountOfMats) for the row matching item class, quality and item level; default `destroyValueSource = "DBMarket"`. — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [TradeSkillMaster.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster/TradeSkillMaster.lua)
- In that data set, milling rates are per herb, while prospecting rates are per 5-ore prospect (the Shopping "destroying" search divides prospect rates by 5; ink targets divide by `pigmentPerInk`). — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [Shopping/modules/Destroying.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Shopping/modules/Destroying.lua)
- CraftSim (retail) models prospecting/milling as "salvage recipes": cost = price of the salvage input x required quantity, and the recipe then goes through the normal profit path including profession stats. — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/PriceData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/PriceData.lua)

**Mechanics**
- Prospecting consumes 5 ore of one type and since Patch 2.1.0 always yields at least one item; mass prospecting (Legion to Shadowlands ores) consumes 20 with no apparent change in yield per ore; since 10.0.0 each expansion has its own Prospecting spell. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Prospecting](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Prospecting)
- Midnight Prospecting (added 12.0.1, 2026-02-10): 5 Midnight ores of the same quality per cast; eligible ores Refulgent Copper Ore, Brilliant Silver Ore, Umbral Tin Ore. Outputs and rates are not listed. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Midnight Prospecting](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Midnight_Prospecting)
- Milling consumes 5 herbs and yields 1 to 4 pigments (one being most common), higher-level herbs occasionally give a rarer pigment; every expansion through Midnight has its own Milling skill. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Milling](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Milling)
- Disenchanting applies to uncommon, rare and epic armor and weapons; dust comes mostly from uncommon armor (about 75%), essences mostly from uncommon weapons (about 75%), shards from rares (about 100%), crystals from epics of item level 56+. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Disenchanting](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Disenchanting)

Thorium Ore: Blue Sapphire, Large Opal, Azerothian Diamond, Huge Emerald 0.3 each, Star Ruby 0.1. Fel Iron and Adamantite Ore: each of the six uncommon Outland gems 0.2. Titanium Ore: uncommon gems 0.25 each, rare gems 0.04 each, epic gems 0.03 each. — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [Conversions.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster/Data/Conversions.lua)
- The wiki confirms Saronite Ore prospects (Jewelcrafting 400) into those twelve gems but gives no rates. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Saronite Ore](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Saronite_Ore)
- Milling, expected pigments per herb, from the same table: common pigment 0.5 or 0.6 depending on the herb (e.g. Alabaster Pigment 0.5 from item 765 and 0.6 from items 2447 and 2449; Azure Pigment 0.5 or 0.6 from the Northrend herbs); rare pigment 0.05 or 0.1 depending on the herb (e.g. Icy Pigment from the same Northrend herbs). Inks: most common inks need 2 pigments (Ink of the Sea = 2 Azure Pigment, Snowfall Ink = 2 Icy Pigment), Ivory Ink needs 1. — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [Conversions.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster/Data/Conversions.lua)

Uncommon weapons invert the dust/essence weights (about 20-22% dust, 75% essence). Rare items: 100% one shard of the bracket (99.5% shard + 0.5% crystal for ilvl 56-115). Epic items: ilvl 56-60 one Nexus Crystal; ilvl 200-284 one Abyss Crystal. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Disenchanting tables](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Disenchanting_tables)

It differs from the wiki on one row: ilvl 66-80 Arcane Dust and Lesser Planar Essence at 1-3 each (wiki: 66-79 at 1-2). — [tool source] [BCC-Auctionator DisenchantingProbabilities.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Auctionator/BCC-Auctionator/main/Source/Constants/DisenchantingProbabilities.lua); differs from [Warcraft Wiki: Disenchanting tables](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Disenchanting_tables)
- Legacy TSM stores the collapsed expectation directly, e.g. Infinite Dust from uncommon armor ilvl 130-151 = 1.875 per item ("2-3, 75% chance = 2.5*0.75"). — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [Disenchanting.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster/Data/Disenchanting.lua)

**The Saronite shuffle (Historical: Wrath of the Lich King retail, 2009-2010)**
- Described in 2010 as: prospect Saronite Ore, then route each output by a decision diagram. Uncommon gems: use for meta-gem transmutes, craft into green jewelry to disenchant or vendor, or cut and vendor; the column states low-70s green jewelry such as Sun Rock Rings vendors for more than its disenchant value and that disenchanting only pays when Infinite Dust is above about 1.25g. Rare gems: transmute one per day per alchemist into an epic gem, cut and auction the rest; cut rare gems vendor for about 4.5g, which sets a price floor. The column says the introduction of the dungeon disenchant option floored Infinite Dust prices so the shuffle was "no longer profitable" for enchanting materials but still profitable overall. *Unverified community claim (all thresholds and prices are the author's, for 2010 retail realms).* — [community] [Engadget / WoW Insider, B. Berntsen, "Gold Capped: The saronite shuffle", 2010-04-14](https://www.engadget.com/2010-04-14-gold-capped-the-saronite-shuffle.html)

### Inferences
- Per-input EV for a destroy step: `EV(input) = sum_i rate_i x value(output_i)`, where `value(output) = max( ahPrice x 0.95 (weighted by sale probability), vendorSell, EV of the next transformation minus its extra reagent cost )`. Evaluate recursively from the leaves; the shuffle is profitable when `EV(input) > buyPrice(input)`.
- Worked Saronite form using the legacy table: `EV per ore = (0.2 x sum of six uncommon gem values + 0.04 x sum of six rare gem values) / 5`. With the wiki disenchant row for ilvl 130-151 uncommon armor, a green ring's disenchant EV = 0.75 x 2.5 x P(Infinite Dust) + 0.22 x 1.5 x P(Lesser Cosmic Essence) + 0.03 x P(Small Dream Shard), i.e. 1.875 dust + 0.33 essence + 0.03 shard per ring.
- The 2010 column shows why routing matters: the best exit for a green gem flipped from "craft and disenchant" to "cut/craft and vendor" when dust prices fell. An addon should recompute the best branch per node from live prices rather than hard-code the chain.
- Destroy steps have no AH cut on the input side but every output sold on the AH pays 5%, and large batches depress output prices; EV should be computed against the depth of the output market, not just the lowest listing.
- Yield variance is high per cast but shuffles are done in bulk, so ranking by expectation is reasonable; a minimum batch size (for example 100+ casts) is needed for realised results to approach the EV.

### Gaps
- No game-data-derived (DB2 / wago.tools) prospecting, milling or disenchant yield tables were located for Dragonflight, The War Within or Midnight; the wiki pages for those skills list inputs only. The legacy rates above come from a hand-maintained tool table, which is tool data rather than a client data extract.
- The wiki's disenchanting tables are explicitly outdated past Mists-era item levels; modern retail disenchant yields were not sourced.
- The archived Wowhead "JC shuffle" guide (https://www.wowhead.com/guide=805/making-gold-with-the-jc-shuffle-archive) returned only its page wrapper; its content could not be verified and is not used.
- No source was found for whether WoW Forever changes Classic disenchant or prospect tables.

## 3. Daily/weekly cooldown crafts and how to value a cooldown

### Takeaway
A cooldown is valued as profit per use divided by cooldown length (gold per day), with the output priced at market rather than at cost; tools deliberately exclude cooldown recipes from "craft it yourself" material pricing for the same reason. Cooldown lengths differ sharply by client, and sources conflict on some Classic values.

### Cited Findings
- Classic: Transmute: Arcanite (1 Thorium Bar + 1 Arcane Crystal, Philosopher's Stone) shows a 2-day cooldown in the Classic database. — [primary data] [Wowhead Classic: spell 17187 Transmute: Arcanite](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=17187/transmute-arcanite)
- Historical/retail: the Arcanite cooldown was removed in Patch 2.4.0 (2008-03-25); the recipe requires Alchemy 275 and is sold by Alchemist Pestlezugg in Gadgetzan; the page says it "does not invoke the 20-hour cooldown common to most transmutations", while the quoted patch note calls the old cooldown "23 hours" (internal inconsistency on the page). — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Transmute: Arcanite](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Transmute:_Arcanite)
- Transmutes in general: "The length of the cooldown varies by recipe, but prevents all transmutations until it has expired"; Burning Crusade primal transmutes have a daily cooldown. The page is inconsistent about Primal Might (listed both as no-cooldown and in the daily-cooldown table). — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Transmutation](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Transmutation)
- Mooncloth: 2 Felcloth, Tailoring 250, must be made at a Moonwell; pattern sold by Qia in Everlook in limited supply; before Patch 2.4.3 the cooldown was 92 hours (3 days 20 hours), removed in 2.4.3. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Mooncloth](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Mooncloth); a commercial guide instead gives 96 hours for Classic Era — [community] [Timesaver.gg, "Best Gold-Making Professions in WoW Classic Era (2026)", 2026-07-20](https://timesaver.gg/blog/best-gold-making-professions-wow-classic-era-2026)
- Forever: a commercial blog (data from Wowhead's Forever database and its own AH scans, 11-12 November 2026; it states nothing was tested in game) reports trimmed cooldowns: Arcanite 47 h, Mooncloth 95 h (pattern now Tailoring 230 and Bind on Pickup), Salt Shaker 71 h, eight essence transmutes 23 h, Iron to Gold and Mithril to Truesilver 1 h, plus a new Transmute: Legionite (Heavy Thorium Bar + Fel Crystal, about 47 h). *Unverified community claim.* — [community, commercial] [Timesaver.gg, "WoW Forever profession cooldowns", 2026-11-12](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-profession-cooldowns-gold)
- The same blog's valuation method: profit per use = median sale price x 0.95 - materials at median; gold per day = profit per use x 24 / cooldown hours. Its 12 November 2026 US figures: Arcanite +24.69g per use (+12.61g/day), Salt Shaker selling the salt +28.47g (+9.62g/day), Water to Air +8.62g/day, Mooncloth +16.05g (+4.05g/day); several essence transmutes were negative (Air to Fire about -11.50g). It notes only 40-70 units listed per region for these goods. *Unverified community claim; prices are asking prices, not sales.* — [community, commercial] [Timesaver.gg, 2026-11-12](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-profession-cooldowns-gold)
- Tool treatment: legacy TSM sets `ignoreCDCraftCost = true`, skipping cooldown recipes as the cost basis of a material when another source exists, and excludes cooldown crafts from intermediate crafting. — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [Cost.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/Modules/Cost.lua)
- CraftSim likewise excludes cooldown recipes from sub-recipe cost substitution unless `COST_OPTIMIZATION_SUB_RECIPE_INCLUDE_COOLDOWNS` is enabled, and tracks cooldowns with charges (`maxCharges`, `cooldownPerCharge`) per crafter. — [tool source] [CraftSim DataSource/PriceSource.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceSource.lua), [Classes/CooldownData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/CooldownData.lua)
- Midnight: the alchemy daily "Wondrous Synergist" has an 18-hour cooldown, reducible to about 9 hours with 20 more points in the Synthesis Synergy specialization; mote transmutes and this daily are called the main gold source of the transmute build. *Unverified community claim.* — [community] [WoW-Professions: Midnight Alchemy Specialization Guide](https://wow-professions.com/midnight/alchemy-specialization-guide-and-builds)
- Historical: epic gem / meta transmutes in Wrath were limited to one per 20 hours per alchemist and chosen "by opportunity cost" (example: Cardinal Ruby 200g vs Eternal 30g vs cut Scarlet Ruby 50g). *Unverified community claim.* — [community] [Engadget, Berntsen 2010](https://www.engadget.com/2010-04-14-gold-capped-the-saronite-shuffle.html)

### Inferences
- Rank cooldowns by `goldPerDay = (outputPrice x 0.95 x expectedYield - inputCost) x 24 / cooldownHours`; for charge-based retail cooldowns use `24 / cooldownPerCharge` and cap by charges if the player logs in rarely.
- When several recipes share one cooldown (transmutes), the value of the cooldown is the maximum profit among them; every other use has that as opportunity cost.
- Material produced by a cooldown should enter downstream recipes at its market price (what it could be sold for), not at its input cost. Both TSM and CraftSim default to this.
- Practical cooldown shorter than a login cycle (23 h vs 24 h) only matters if the player logs in at the same time daily; valuing per login is more realistic than per hour.
- Alts multiply cooldown income linearly but the reported market depth (tens of units listed) means a multi-alt operation moves the price; see section 10.

### Gaps
- Classic cooldown values conflict across sources (Mooncloth 92 h vs 96 h; Arcanite wiki text vs database). The client's own cooldown data is the only reliable arbiter; on a live client the addon can read it directly.
- No primary or official source was found for the Forever cooldown changes; they rest on one commercial blog that cites Wowhead's Forever database.
- Shared-cooldown rules in Forever (whether Arcanite and essence transmutes share a timer) are stated as unknown by the only source.
- Burning Crusade cloth cooldowns (Primal Mooncloth, Shadowcloth, Spellcloth) and Primal Might were not confirmed from a reliable page in this pass.

## 4. Material conversion arbitrage

### Takeaway
Free, instant conversions (essence splitting/combining, motes to primals, crystallized to eternals) are priced by the market to within roughly the 5% auction cut, as a peer-reviewed measurement on Planar Essences found; durable spreads appear where conversion is one-way, cooldown-gated, vendor-limited or requires a profession.

### Cited Findings
- Kosminsky (2009), *Journal of Virtual Worlds Research* 2(4), DOI 10.4101/jvwr.v2i4.869: used Lesser/Greater Planar Essence (3 Lesser convert to 1 Greater and back, by any character) to test for arbitrage with auction data from 13 September to 13 October 2008. At modal prices the ratio was exactly 1:3 (Lesser 5.00, Greater 15.00; 0% deviation). At mean prices there was "a roughly 5% profit to be made by breaking GPE's into their LPE's" (Lesser mean 6.02 vs predicted 5.71; Greater mean 17.12 vs predicted 18.06). The author attributes the gap to level-70 players obtaining Greater Essences and not bothering to split them. — [peer-reviewed] [Kosminsky 2009, JVWR 2(4)](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/view/869) ([full text PDF](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/view/869/634))
- Morrison and Fontenla (2013), "Price convergence in an online virtual world", *Empirical Economics* 44(3), 1053-1064, DOI 10.1007/s00181-012-0567-3: using data from eight structurally identical WoW servers, both the Phillips-Sul non-linear convergence test and the authors' own "band of inaction" test found price convergence in all eight worlds. (Abstract page only; full text is paywalled.) — [peer-reviewed] [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/empeco/v44y2013i3p1053-1064.html)
- Conversion ratios encoded in legacy TSM as `transform`: 3 Lesser = 1 Greater essence in both directions (Magic, Astral, Mystic, Nether, Eternal, Planar, Cosmic); 3 Small Dream Shard = 1 Dream Shard and 3 Small Heavenly Shard = 1 Heavenly Shard (combine only); 10 Motes = 1 Primal (Burning Crusade); 10 Crystallized = 1 Eternal and back (Wrath). `vendortrade`: Mists-era Ink Trader exchange of Ink of Dreams 1:1 for older inks. — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [Conversions.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster/Data/Conversions.lua)
- TSM's `convert(dbmarket)` is part of the default material price: a material is priced at the cheapest source item divided by its rate. — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [TradeSkillMaster_Crafting.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting.lua), [Conversions.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster/Data/Conversions.lua)
- Vendor reagents: TSM only knows `vendorBuy` for items the player has seen at a vendor; CraftSim and CraftInfoAnywhere both use the vendor price in preference to the auction price when one exists. — [tool docs] [TSM price sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean); [tool source] [CraftSim PriceAPIs.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceAPIs.lua), [CraftInfoAnywhere Main_Classic.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TheMouseNest/CraftInfoAnywhere/main/Main_Classic.lua)
- Cooldown-gated conversions show persistent, direction-dependent spreads: on 12 November 2026 (Forever, US medians) Water to Air was +8.62g while Air to Fire was about -11.50g; Essence of Air 14.07g, Earth 8.50g, Water 4.75g, Living Essence 1.69g. *Unverified community claim.* — [community, commercial] [Timesaver.gg, 2026-11-12](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-profession-cooldowns-gold)
- Retail quality tiers create a related choice: CraftSim's cost routine picks the cheapest quality tier when the player has not allocated one, and its reagent optimiser searches for the most profitable quality mix. — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/PriceData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/PriceData.lua); [tool docs] [CraftSim on CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/craftsim)

### Inferences
- Arbitrage test for a conversion of n low units into 1 high unit: combine if `P_high x 0.95 > n x P_low_buy`; split (where reversible) if `n x P_low x 0.95 > P_high_buy`. The no-arbitrage band is therefore about +/-5% around parity, which matches the roughly 5% mean deviation Kosminsky measured. Deviations beyond the band on free conversions should be short-lived.
- Addon implication: a material's effective buy price should be `min(direct price, converted price)` for every recipe (what TSM's `convert()` does), which also surfaces the arbitrage automatically as a "craft" with zero skill requirement.
- One-way conversions (small shard to large shard, motes to primals) bound the price from one side only: n x P_low cannot stay above P_high / 0.95 for long, but P_high can exceed n x P_low indefinitely if nobody combines.
- Cooldown-gated or profession-gated conversions (transmutes) are not arbitraged away because capacity is capped per character per day; they should be ranked as cooldowns (section 3).
- Vendor-sold reagents put a hard ceiling on a reagent's value; any AH listing above vendor price is never the right cost basis, and any AH listing of a limited-supply vendor item is priced by scarcity instead.

### Gaps
- No academic work was found on multi-step production chains or crafting margins in WoW specifically; the two papers above cover conversion parity and cross-server convergence only. Lehdonvirta and Castronova, *Virtual Economies: Design and Analysis* (MIT Press, 2014) appears relevant (a "Production and economic growth" section was visible in a library catalogue snippet) but was not opened and is not cited for any claim.
- Cross-realm or cross-faction arbitrage of crafted gear was not researched here (other researchers' scope).
- Vendor prices of reagents by client (and Forever-specific vendor changes) were not extracted from game data.

## 5. Crafting for vendor: crafts whose vendor sell price exceeds material cost

### Takeaway
Vendor sale is the zero-risk exit: no 5% cut, no deposit, unlimited demand, instant. Tools treat vendor value as a floor (and CraftSim values junk-quality outputs purely at vendor price), but no source provides a systematic list of vendor-profitable crafts; it has to be computed from recipe data and vendor sell prices.

### Cited Findings
- CraftSim values a grey (junk) result at `GetVendorSellPriceByItemID` and applies no auction cut ("grey items are sold to vendor; no AH cut"). — [tool source] [CraftSim Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua), [Classes/PriceData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/PriceData.lua)
- TSM exposes `vendorSell` as a price source and its default Vendoring operation sells to a vendor any item that has expired from the auction house at least 20 times (no value conditions, soulbound excluded). — [tool docs] [TSM price sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean), [TSM: How do the default operations work](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/addon/how-do-the-default-operations-work)
- Historical example (Wrath retail, 2010): crafted low-70s green jewelry "such as Sun Rock Rings" vendored for more than its disenchant value, and cut rare gems vendored for about 4.5g, so sellers were advised not to list them below about 4.5g net of the auction cut. *Unverified community claim.* — [community] [Engadget, Berntsen 2010](https://www.engadget.com/2010-04-14-gold-capped-the-saronite-shuffle.html)
- The deposit itself scales with vendor sell price (15/30/60% for 12/24/48 h), so high-vendor-value crafts are the most expensive to list and relist. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House)

### Inferences
- Vendor profit per craft = `yield x vendorSell(output) - matCost`. Any recipe where this is positive is profitable at unlimited volume, limited only by how many reagents can be bought before the reagent price rises above break-even. The addon should therefore walk the reagent order book and report "profitable up to N crafts", not just a per-craft number.
- For every craft the realisable value should be `max(vendorSell, ahPrice x 0.95 adjusted for sale probability)`; the vendor price is a floor that removes downside on slow sellers.
- Vendor-profitable crafts are the natural skill-up targets (section 9): the leveling cost becomes negative or small.
- Because vendor value comes from static item data, a vendor-profit opportunity only appears when reagent prices crash (new patch, botting, oversupply), so it is worth re-scanning after each price refresh rather than keeping a fixed list.

### Gaps
- No game-data extract of vendor sell prices against recipe material lists was produced; no reliable published list of currently vendor-profitable crafts for Midnight, Classic or Forever was found.
- Whether Forever changes vendor sell values of crafted items is unknown.

## 6. Demand-side selection: which crafted categories sell reliably, and when

### Takeaway
The measurable demand signals are region sale rate and sold-per-day; the qualitative guidance (raid consumables first, demand peaking at raid reset and at new tiers/season starts) comes only from community guides and is not backed by any data set found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Measurable proxies: TSM `DBRegionSaleRate` (share of postings that sell) and `DBRegionSoldPerDay` (units sold per auction house per day), plus the player's own `SaleRate` and `NumExpires`. — [tool docs] [TSM value sources](https://tradeskillmaster.helpjuice.com/custom-strings/which-value-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)
- CraftSim uses a default region sale-rate threshold of 0.1 before auto-queueing a scanned recipe. — [tool source] [CraftSim Util/Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua)
- Retail structure that shapes demand: multicraft works only on stackable items (consumables, reagents), so high-volume consumable crafting is where multicraft builds apply; gear goes through crafting orders. — [official] [Blizzard: Dragonflight: Making It with Professions](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23876529/dragonflight-making-it-with-professions)
- Midnight alchemy: flasks have the highest demand, potions similar, phials are a smaller market with less competition; cauldrons unlock at 30 points in the relevant specialization. *Unverified community claim.* — [community] [WoW-Professions: Midnight Alchemy Specialization Guide](https://wow-professions.com/midnight/alchemy-specialization-guide-and-builds)
- Classic Era: potions and flasks sell into raid-reset demand; a quoted goldmaker says herb and consumable prices "consistently drop the day before reset and go up on and after reset"; Runecloth and Mageweave bags sell steadily because every new character needs them; enchanting as a tipped service is discouraged (tips often 1-5g). *Unverified community claim; the site sells gold and boosting.* — [community, commercial] [Timesaver.gg, 2026-07-20](https://timesaver.gg/blog/best-gold-making-professions-wow-classic-era-2026)
- Historical (Wrath retail): among meta gems the two most popular raiding cuts (Chaotic Skyflare, Relentless Earthsiege) were prioritised and Scarlet Ruby had the highest margin, so the red cut was learned first. *Unverified community claim.* — [community] [Engadget, Berntsen 2010](https://www.engadget.com/2010-04-14-gold-capped-the-saronite-shuffle.html)
- Midnight 12.1 added new embellishments, a weapon enchant (Rite of the Hash'ey), new alchemy consumables and many housing decor recipes across professions, mostly from Renown or vendors. *Unverified community claim (recipe list from a guide site).* — [community] [Method.gg, "All profession changes and new recipes in WoW Midnight patch 12.1", 2026-08-09](https://www.method.gg/guides/all-profession-changes-and-new-recipes-in-wow-midnight-patch-12-1)
- Forever: the same commercial blog says Blizzard's raid overview puts the first Forever raids on 9 December (year not stated; the article is dated 12 November 2026). *Unverified community claim.* — [community, commercial] [Timesaver.gg, 2026-11-12](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-profession-cooldowns-gold)
- Academic context: in Kosminsky's 2008 data a higher share of level 58-70 characters on a server went with a predictably higher netherweave cloth price (fitted curve R2 = 0.993), "regardless of population size". — [peer-reviewed] [Kosminsky 2009](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/view/869/634)

### Inferences
- A category score an addon can compute without guides: `expectedDailyProfit = min(soldPerDay x achievableShare, capacity) x profitPerUnit`, filtered by saleRate above a threshold. This naturally surfaces consumables (high sold-per-day) over gear (low sale rate, high deposit).
- Raid-reset timing implies a weekly cycle: craft and stock before reset, post on and just after reset. An addon can detect this locally by storing its own price snapshots by weekday.
- New recipes and new embellishments at a patch are temporarily supply-constrained (few crafters have the recipe or the knowledge points); the opportunity decays as knowledge spreads.
- Bags, profession tools and leveling gear are demand that scales with new or returning characters, so they peak at expansion launch, season start and (for Forever/Classic) server launch.

### Gaps
- No data-backed study of weekly or seasonal demand cycles for WoW consumables was found; all timing claims are community assertions.
- Not opened (HTTP 403): Icy Veins "Best Professions to Make Gold in Midnight" (https://icy-veins.com/wow/professions-making-gold). Search snippets suggested its tier list changed between May and August 2026 (gathering professions ranked higher early; Inscription, Jewelcrafting and Tailoring later), but this could not be verified and is not relied on.
- Weapon oils, gems, enchants and profession tools in Midnight were not individually sourced.
- No sold-volume data was collected; the addon would need TSM region data or its own history.

## 7. Retail-specific systems: crafting orders, concentration, knowledge, patron orders

### Takeaway
Retail adds three scarce resources that must be valued separately from gold cost: concentration (1,000 cap, 1 point per 6 minutes per profession), knowledge points (one reset per Midnight profession), and order capacity. CraftSim's source shows the working formulas for commission profit and gold per concentration point.

### Cited Findings

**Crafting orders**
- Three fulfilment types: public, guild, personal. "For public orders, the player requesting the creation of an item will need to provide the tradeable reagents"; guild and personal orders can have reagents from either side; requesters offer a commission and "pay a small posting fee"; the crafter collects the commission "minus a small cut for the Artisan's Consortium"; soulbound items can be ordered, with soulbound reagents usually supplied by the requester. — [official] [Blizzard: Dragonflight: Making It with Professions](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23876529/dragonflight-making-it-with-professions)
- Crafting orders were added in Patch 10.0.2 (2022-11-15); order clerks are listed in Valdrakken, Dornogal and Silvermoon City. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Crafting Orders](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Crafting_Orders)
- CraftSim's order profit: `commissionProfit = tipAmount - consortiumCut + sum(value of reagents the customer supplied)`, and the order's profit is that figure minus full crafting cost (so net = tip - cut - cost of reagents the crafter adds). No AH cut and no deposit apply. For NPC (patron) orders the reward items are added at AH price x 0.95, a reagent-bag reward at a user-set value, and optionally the "Moxie" currency reward at a user-set gold value per unit, with an extra first-craft Moxie bonus on patron orders. — [tool source] [CraftSim Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua)
- CraftSim also keeps a per-customer history of orders and can optimise and queue patron orders. — [tool docs] [CraftSim on CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/craftsim)

**Concentration**
- Concentration is capped at 1,000 and regenerates one point per six minutes; added in Patch 11.0.0 (2024-07-23) replacing Inspiration; Ingenuity gives a chance to refund some spent concentration. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Concentration](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Concentration)
- CraftSim's gold per concentration point: `concentrationValue = averageProfit(with concentration) / (concentrationCost x (1 - ingenuityChance x (0.5 + ingenuityBonus)))`, i.e. an Ingenuity proc refunds a base 50% of the cost. — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/RecipeData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/RecipeData.lua)
- Concentration cost is recipe-specific and falls as skill approaches recipe difficulty: CraftSim interpolates it from per-recipe curve data keyed by `craftingDataID` (described in the source as mapped from "db2 data"), with reductions from specialization, optional reagents and tools, and falls back to `C_TradeSkillUI.GetCraftingOperationInfo` when a recipe is not mapped. — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/RecipeData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/RecipeData.lua)
- Concentration is read per profession as a currency (`C_CurrencyInfo.GetCurrencyInfo(currencyID)` with `maxQuantity` and `rechargingCycleDurationMS`), and CraftSim stores it per crafter character. — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/ConcentrationData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/ConcentrationData.lua)
- Spending concentration yields exactly one quality tier above the skill-determined quality, capped at the recipe's maximum. — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/ResultData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/ResultData.lua)

**Knowledge and specialization**
- Specializations let crafters spend knowledge to "improve different areas of your profession permanently", most significantly crafting skill. — [official] [Blizzard: Dragonflight: Making It with Professions](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23876529/dragonflight-making-it-with-professions)
- Midnight: knowledge points can be reset once per Midnight profession, refunding all points; recipes unlocked by spent points are unlearned on reset. *Unverified community claim.* — [community] [Method.gg, 2026-08-09](https://www.method.gg/guides/all-profession-changes-and-new-recipes-in-wow-midnight-patch-12-1)
- Midnight alchemy knowledge income: about 40-50 points on the first day (first crafts, one-time treasures) and about 18 per week afterwards (patron orders, weekly quests, treasure drops, treatises); recommended AH build order is Multicraft, then Resourcefulness, then Gold quality and cauldrons, on one product line before branching after about 130 points. *Unverified community claim.* — [community] [WoW-Professions: Midnight Alchemy Specialization Guide](https://wow-professions.com/midnight/alchemy-specialization-guide-and-builds)
- Midnight reagents have two quality tiers (CraftSim code comment); the alchemy guide refers to "Gold Quality" as the top tier. — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/PriceData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/PriceData.lua); [community] [WoW-Professions](https://wow-professions.com/midnight/alchemy-specialization-guide-and-builds)

### Inferences
- From the wiki numbers: 1 point per 6 minutes = 10 per hour = 240 per day; an empty bar refills in 100 hours (about 4.2 days). Concentration income is therefore `240 x goldPerPoint` per profession per character per day, and the bar must be spent at least every 4 days to avoid waste.
- CraftSim divides the whole concentrated-craft profit by the concentration cost. When the same recipe is already profitable without concentration, the incremental value is the better ranking key: `(profitWithConc - max(0, profitWithoutConc)) / effectiveConcCost`. Recipes where the lower tier is unprofitable or unsellable make the two measures coincide.
- Because concentration cost falls with skill, the same recipe has a different gold-per-point for every character; the addon needs the player's actual cost from the client API, not a table.
- Knowledge points are the binding long-run constraint (weekly-capped income, one reset), so specialization choice is effectively an irreversible investment: rank candidate nodes by the increase in daily profit of the product lines they affect.
- For orders, the ranking number is `tip - consortiumCut - crafterSuppliedReagentCost (after resourcefulness savings)` plus any concentration spent valued at its best alternative gold-per-point.

### Gaps
- The Artisan's Consortium cut percentage and the posting fee are not stated in the official post or on the wiki; forum figures seen in search snippets (for example 5%) were not verifiable. The client returns `consortiumCut` per order, so it can be read directly.
- Public-order claim limits (search snippets mention a charge system introduced around 11.0) could not be confirmed from an official or wiki source; the Wowhead economy article that discusses them returned no body text.
- No official Blizzard article on Midnight profession changes was opened; the official forum post on Concentration (us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/1833285) returned 404.
- Gear quality tier count in Midnight was reported inconsistently by community guides in search results (three vs five ranks) and was not resolved.
- The gold value of Moxie currency and typical patron-order reward values were not sourced; CraftSim leaves them user-defined.

## 8. Classic-specific: specializations, limited recipes, pre-raid crafts, cooldowns

### Takeaway
Classic-style profit comes from access rather than procs: profession specializations with exclusive recipes, limited-supply vendor recipes, and long cooldowns. The same structures exist in WoW Forever with reported adjustments (shorter cooldowns, some patterns made Bind on Pickup).

### Cited Findings
- Blacksmithing specializations: Armorsmithing or Weaponsmithing at character level 40 and skill 200, mutually exclusive; Weaponsmiths can further specialise in axes, hammers or swords at level 50 and skill 250; each grants recipes not otherwise available, and many of the strongest items "can only be wielded by other weaponsmiths/armorsmiths or by the smith who made the item". Removed from retail in Patch 4.0.1; the page states the specialization remains in World of Warcraft: Classic and World of Warcraft: Forever. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Blacksmithing specialization](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Blacksmithing_specialization)
- Alchemy specializations (Elixir, Potion, Transmutation Master; the page does not state the patch that introduced them): each gives a chance of 1 to 4 additional items (up to 5 total) on crafts within its focus; one specialization at a time, switchable. The page is flagged outdated and gives no proc percentage. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Alchemy proficiencies](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Alchemy_proficiencies)
- The Transmutation page relays an unverified report that Transmutation mastery averages about 20% more items. — [reference wiki, reporting an unverified figure] [Warcraft Wiki: Transmutation](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Transmutation)
- Limited-supply vendor recipes: vendors hold a small fixed stock (usually 1 to 4) and restock after anything from seconds to several hours (one documented timer: Herbalist's Gloves pattern, 5 to 22 minutes); the vendor window must be reopened to see restocks. Examples listed (recipe for: vendor, zone): Transmute: Mithril to Truesilver (Alchemist Pestlezugg, Gadgetzan), Free Action Potion (Soolie Berryfizz, Ironforge; Vendor-Tron 1000, Desolace), Major Health enchanting formula (Qia, Winterspring), Lava Belt (Lokhtos Darkbargainer, Blackrock Depths), Golden Scale Coif (Krinkle Goodsteel, Tanaris), Gyrofreeze Ice Reflector (Xizzer Fizzbolt, Winterspring), Icy Cloak (Ghok'kah, Dustwallow Marsh; Micha Yance, Hillsbrad Foothills), Superior Wizard Oil (Madame Ruby, Shattrath), Bolt of Soulcloth (Borto and Mathar G'ochar, Nagrand). — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Limited quantity recipes](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Limited_quantity_recipes), [Warcraft Wiki: Limited supply](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Limited_supply)
- Cooldowns: Arcanite 2 days in the Classic database; Mooncloth needs a Moonwell and its pattern is a limited-supply item from Qia (see section 3 for the conflicting cooldown lengths). — [primary data] [Wowhead Classic: spell 17187](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=17187/transmute-arcanite); [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Mooncloth](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Mooncloth)
- Mooncloth's uses include tailoring armor (Mooncloth Robe, Leggings, Vest), bags (Mooncloth Bag, Bottomless Bag) and the leatherworking Living Breastplate. — [reference wiki] [Warcraft Wiki: Mooncloth](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Mooncloth)
- Forever: reported changes include Pattern: Mooncloth and Recipe: Transmute Mithril to Truesilver becoming Bind on Pickup (so they cannot be bought for resale to alts), Mooncloth at Tailoring 230, a "Legacy" perk tree with a Professions branch (16 Legacy Points per character at launch) and a "Dedicated Study" perk on a 23-hour cooldown that, once all five tradeskills are 300, grants 2-4 random Elemental Essences. *Unverified community claim.* — [community, commercial] [Timesaver.gg, 2026-11-12](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-profession-cooldowns-gold)
- Classic Era ranking from a commercial guide: Alchemy (transmutes, potions and flasks around raid resets), disenchant flipping of underpriced greens, double gathering, and Tailoring (bags, Mooncloth). *Unverified community claim.* — [community, commercial] [Timesaver.gg, 2026-07-20](https://timesaver.gg/blog/best-gold-making-professions-wow-classic-era-2026)
- "Disenchant flipping" is directly computable from the disenchant tables in section 2 and is exactly what TSM's Destroy value and legacy "destroying" search implement (buy items whose price is below destroy value). — [tool docs] [TSM price sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean); [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [Shopping/modules/Destroying.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Shopping/modules/Destroying.lua)

### Inferences
- For a Classic/Forever addon, "who can craft this" is a first-class input: a recipe locked behind a specialization, reputation or limited-supply vendor has fewer competing crafters, so its margin persists; the addon can flag recipes whose learned-by population is structurally restricted.
- Specialization procs (alchemy masteries) act like retail multicraft: expected yield = 1 + pProc x E[extra]; without a verified proc rate the addon should let the user enter an observed rate or learn it from the player's own craft log.
- Limited-supply recipes are a pure buy-and-resell item when tradable; when a client makes them Bind on Pickup (as reported for Forever) the value moves from the recipe to the crafted output.
- Crafted gear with self-only or specialist-only use cannot be sold, so its value is the user's own upgrade, not gold; it should be excluded from profit rankings.

### Gaps
- Craftable pre-raid best-in-slot items were not researched from a reliable source in this pass; no list is provided.
- Tailoring (Mooncloth/Shadoweave/Spellfire), Leatherworking (Dragonscale/Elemental/Tribal) and Engineering (Gnomish/Goblin) specialization details were seen only in search snippets and are not cited.
- Blizzard's support article "Changing Profession Specializations in WoW Classic Versions" (us.support.blizzard.com/en/article/248671) requires login and could not be opened.
- No proc-rate data for alchemy masteries from game data or a controlled sample was found.
- All Forever-specific facts rest on one commercial blog; nothing official was found.

## 9. Leveling a profession profitably, profession pairings, alt armies

### Takeaway
No reliable, quantitative source was found for leveling-cost recovery or pairing choices; what exists is community advice. The tool sources do show the mechanics that make alts valuable: cooldowns, concentration and knowledge are per character and per profession.

### Cited Findings
- Per-character resources: CraftSim stores concentration and cooldown state per crafter character and profession, and can price a reagent at the expected crafting cost of a designated alt crafter. — [tool source] [CraftSim Classes/ConcentrationData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/ConcentrationData.lua), [Classes/CooldownData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/CooldownData.lua), [DataSource/PriceSource.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceSource.lua)
- TSM's `Crafting` cost is only available if one of the player's characters knows the recipe, which is why goldmakers keep the whole chain on their own account. — [tool docs] [TSM price sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)
- Pairings suggested for Classic Era: Alchemy + Herbalism (self-supplied herbs), Herbalism + Mining (double gatherer), or Alchemy on a bank alt fed with cheap auction-house herbs; the guide says leveling a profession for gold is only worth it if it will be worked consistently, and gives no leveling cost figures. *Unverified community claim.* — [community, commercial] [Timesaver.gg, 2026-07-20](https://timesaver.gg/blog/best-gold-making-professions-wow-classic-era-2026)
- Alt cooldown guidance for Forever: put each cooldown on the character that has the profession; sell in small batches because only 40-70 units per region are listed. *Unverified community claim.* — [community, commercial] [Timesaver.gg, 2026-11-12](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-profession-cooldowns-gold)
- Midnight: specialising one product line first and adding a second after about 130 knowledge points is the recommended path for auction-house alchemists. *Unverified community claim.* — [community] [WoW-Professions: Midnight Alchemy Specialization Guide](https://wow-professions.com/midnight/alchemy-specialization-guide-and-builds)
- Historical: a jewelcrafter plus an alchemist (daily transmute) plus an enchanter formed the Wrath shuffle chain on one account. *Unverified community claim.* — [community] [Engadget, Berntsen 2010](https://www.engadget.com/2010-04-14-gold-capped-the-saronite-shuffle.html)

### Inferences
- Leveling cost for an addon that knows recipes and prices: for each skill-up step choose the recipe minimising `(matCost - max(vendorSell, ahPrice x 0.95 x saleProbability) x yield) / expectedSkillUps`; the total is the net cost to recoup. Payback time = net cost / expected daily profit from the unlocked crafts and cooldowns.
- Pairings follow from the chains: a pairing is good when one profession's output is the other's input and the intermediate market has a wide spread or a 5% cut to avoid (Jewelcrafting + Enchanting for crafted-jewelry disenchanting; Alchemy with either for transmutes; Herbalism/Mining only when gathering time is valued below the material's market price).
- Alt value per day = sum of cooldown gold per day + 240 x gold per concentration point per profession (retail). The marginal alt is worth adding while that exceeds the player's time cost per login and while the extra volume does not move the price (section 10).
- Self-gathered materials still have an opportunity cost equal to their auction price net of the cut; pricing them at zero overstates crafting profit.

### Gaps
- No trustworthy numbers for profession leveling costs in Midnight, Classic or Forever.
- The r/woweconomy wiki and The Lazy Goldmaker were not successfully opened in this pass (Reddit mirror failed with a TLS error); nothing from them is cited.
- No academic or data-based treatment of multi-character production in WoW was found.

## 10. How goldmakers decide how many to craft and avoid crashing their own market

### Takeaway
The documented mechanism is a restock rule: craft up to a maximum stock level, but only if the shortfall is at least a minimum batch and the craft clears a minimum profit; both TSM and CraftSim implement it and allow the target to be an expression (for example tied to sale rate). Sizing the target relative to daily sold volume is practitioner advice, not something any source quantifies.

### Cited Findings
- TSM crafting operation: "Max restock quantity" is the maximum number wanted in stock; "Min restock quantity" suppresses the queue entirely if fewer would be added (example: minimum 10, shortfall 3, nothing is queued); "Set min profit" is enabled by default at 100g; all three accept custom strings, with restock values valid between 0 and 2000. Default operation: min 10, max 20, min profit 100g. — [tool docs] [TSM: Crafting Operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/1236068-tsm-addon-crafting-operations), [TSM: How do the default operations work](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/addon/how-do-the-default-operations-work)
- Legacy TSM queue code makes the rule explicit: `queue = max(maxRestock - totalInventory, 0)`; it is queued only if profit >= minProfit (when set) and reduced to 0 if below minRestock; inventory counts all tracked stock. Legacy defaults were min 1, max 3, min profit 100g. — [tool source, legacy TSM 2.8.3 fork] [Queue.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/Modules/Queue.lua), [TradeSkillMaster_Crafting.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting.lua)
- CraftSim "smart restock": `needed = max(0, target - owned)` where target is a TSM expression evaluated per item and owned is tradable inventory across the player, auction house and optionally alts and warband bank; scanned recipes are only queued if the region sale rate meets a threshold (default 0.1); for concentration crafts it exposes `GetQueueableAmount = floor(spendable concentration / cost per craft)`. — [tool source] [CraftSim DataSource/PriceAPIs.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceAPIs.lua), [Classes/ConcentrationData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/ConcentrationData.lua), [Util/Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua)
- Inputs available for sizing: `DBRegionSoldPerDay`, `DBRegionSaleRate`, own `SaleRate`, `NumExpires`, `NumInventory`. — [tool docs] [TSM value sources](https://tradeskillmaster.helpjuice.com/custom-strings/which-value-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)
- TSM's default Vendoring operation treats 20 expirations as the signal that an item is not selling. — [tool docs] [TSM: How do the default operations work](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/addon/how-do-the-default-operations-work)
- Practitioner example (2011): keep about three of each gem cut on the auction house and recraft "whenever I get below three"; judge profitability by gold, by percent of material cost, or both; one minimum profit is "not appropriate for all markets (glyphs come to mind)"; with large raw-material stockpiles one may "value volume over profitability". *Unverified community claim.* — [community] [Engadget, Berntsen 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011-03-21-gold-capped-tradeskillmaster-advanced-guide.html)
- Thin markets: with only 40-70 units listed per region for Forever cooldown goods, the advice is to sell in small batches because large dumps undercut the price. *Unverified community claim.* — [community, commercial] [Timesaver.gg, 2026-11-12](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-profession-cooldowns-gold)
- Academic context: Kosminsky concludes that, with caveats, WoW is "a highly competitive market approaching perfect competition", reporting all three tested conditions as present: "price stability, prices of goods increasing where higher concentrations of players require that good, and a lack of arbitrage". The paper's premise is that larger seller populations make price coordination impossible and supply more stable. — [peer-reviewed] [Kosminsky 2009](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/view/869/634)
- Listing prices are not sale prices: DBMarket is built from listings, so a market-value-based profit can exist on paper for an item that rarely sells. — [tool docs] [TSM price sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)

### Inferences
- A restock target that scales with demand: `maxRestock = clamp(round(soldPerDay x daysOfCover x targetShare), minBatch, cap)`, with `queue = max(0, maxRestock - owned)` and a zero queue when below `minRestock` or below `minProfit`. `daysOfCover` of 1 to 2 for fast commodities, longer for slow gear; `targetShare` well below 1 unless the player is the only crafter.
- Self-crash check: if the player's planned posting exceeds roughly one day of sold volume, the marginal units will not sell at the current price; the addon should value the batch against the depth of the order book (or assume a declining price) rather than multiply unit profit by quantity.
- The same walk-the-book logic applies on the input side: buying reagents for N crafts raises the average reagent price; break-even N is where marginal reagent cost meets output price x 0.95.
- In competitive, undercut-driven markets (consistent with the academic finding), margins on unrestricted recipes trend toward the 5% cut plus effort; durable margins need a constraint (cooldown, concentration, knowledge, recipe access) or timing.
- Minimum profit should be relative as well as absolute (for example `max(flat, x% of crafting cost)`) so the rule works for both cheap consumables and expensive gear; TSM's min-profit field accepts a custom string, and the 2011 column describes judging by gold, by percent of material cost, or both.
- For retail commodities (region-wide since 9.2.7) the relevant volume is regional, so individual players are small relative to the market for mainstream consumables but not for niche crafts; for Classic/Forever single-realm markets the player's own share is much larger.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the relationship between a seller's posted quantity and price impact in WoW; "do not exceed about a day of sold volume" is this note's inference, not a cited rule.
- Whether current TSM documents a recommended restock expression using `DBRegionSoldPerDay` was not confirmed; only that the fields accept custom strings.
- TSM's region sale statistics are only available through its desktop app data; no equivalent primary data source for Classic/Forever realms was identified.
- The Wowhead economy articles that reportedly describe sale-rate-based minimum-profit rules (for example a higher markup when region sale rate is below 0.2) appeared only in search snippets and were not opened; they are not cited.
