# Robust prices and scarce capacity earn gold

The research supports a plain answer: in a game like World of Warcraft the most gold comes from converting items where capacity is capped (cooldowns, restricted recipes, concentration) and from trading only what demonstrably sells, with every decision priced after the auction cut and the deposits lost on failed listings. The studies that exist describe the auction house as a competitive market in which conversion parities hold and price gaps between linked markets close, so the edge on liquid goods is small and bounded by fees (peer-reviewed study, small sample; [Kosminsky 2010](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/view/869/634)) (peer-reviewed study, verified at abstract level only; [Morrison & Fontenla 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/empeco/v44y2013i3p1053-1064.html)). Price levels are set mainly by the operator: expansions, patches, sinks and recipe changes move whole categories by double digits, while the popular "buy on day X, sell on day Y" weekly clock has no data behind it at any evidence tier. Every numeric trading threshold in circulation (buy at 80% of market value, 60% for gear, 15 to 30% margin) is a community convention from a handful of authors, and every gold-per-hour figure found is self-reported and unverified. What is solid is narrower and more useful: the retail fee mechanics, the algorithms the established tools document for themselves, and textbook statistics for outlier-prone data. An addon that sees only its own scans, the player's recipes, inventory and ledger can therefore compute a robust usual price, a deal score, estimated sales per day, days of supply, cost to clear, fee-aware flip and craft profit, cheapest material source, restock quantity, cooldown value, and it can learn weekly patterns and farm values from the player's own history instead of assuming them. For WoW Forever (released 4 November 2026, interface 16001) no fee rule has an official or wiki source, so cut, deposit, durations and ordering rules belong in the client's own answers, not in constants. Two independent audits re-checked the sources behind this report and found no fabricated reference, but they relabelled several tiers and softened a number of claims; those corrections are applied throughout.

## How to read the evidence labels

Each important claim carries its evidence tier in plain words. "Peer-reviewed study" means a journal or conference paper with external review; "abstract only" means the audits could confirm the paper's existence and its abstract but not its full text, so magnitudes not stated in the abstract are not used. "Working paper or preprint" covers arXiv papers, institutional working papers and theses, which are named as such. "Official Blizzard source" means a Blizzard news page, legal text or a post by an account carrying the staff flag. "Tool's own documentation" and "source code" mean a tool describing or implementing itself; code from third-party ports is named as third-party. "Reference wiki" is warcraft.wiki.gg. "Government reference handbook" is the NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, and "textbook" is an open textbook read in full. "Community convention" marks rules of thumb from guides and blogs; where the publisher sells gold or boosting, that conflict of interest is stated next to the citation. Formulas that the researchers derived themselves, as opposed to formulas a source states, are marked "researchers' derivation".

## Prices follow game design, drift after launches, and show no proven weekly clock

### Operator decisions move price levels more than players do

The best-supported driver of in-game prices is the operator. Around the Legion launch (30 August 2016) the gold price of the WoW Token rose from 37,971 to 101,042 gold in North America between 16 August 2016 and 25 April 2017, **a factor of 2.66**, and by 2.72 in Europe; the rise began about a month after launch, and real-world exchange-rate moves explained only between -0.06 and +0.11 of those multipliers (journal article with low-assurance peer review from a publisher that appears on archived copies of Beall's list, descriptive only, figures verified against the paper's tables; [Holm & Mäkinen 2018](https://ibimapublishing.com/articles/JISNVC/2018/672253/672253.pdf)). The authors read this as an expansion-driven increase in money supply, but that is their assumption and was not tested. The same series shows an announced rule change pricing in at once: when tokens became convertible to Battle.net balance, Western token prices shifted upward on 7 February 2017 ([Holm & Mäkinen 2018](https://ibimapublishing.com/articles/JISNVC/2018/672253/672253.pdf)). A master's thesis reaches a compatible conclusion from 2009 data, tying long-run gold inflation to operator patches and to the EUR/CNY exchange rate through real-money trade (master's thesis, abstract only; [Skuhrovec 2009](https://invenio.nusl.cz/record/277053?ln=en)).

Evidence from other games shows the mechanism causally. In Old School RuneScape a developer item sink raised the prices of the targeted goods by about **7% (95% interval 5.0 to 9.6) in the first round and 14.4% (11.9 to 16.9) in the second**, with no distinguishable change in trade volume, while a transaction tax had no detectable volume effect at its lower threshold (preprint with no publication venue, full text verified, other game; [Hogan-Hennessy, Xenopoulos & Silva 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07970)). Supply-side changes cut the other way: in EVE Online's first quarter of 2010 the primary producer price index fell 14.2% and Tech II construction components fell 22% after an expansion made production more efficient (official developer report, full text verified; [CCP Quarterly Economic Newsletter Q1 2010](https://cdn1.eveonline.com/community/QEN/QEN_Q1-2010.pdf)). Inflation is also uneven across goods: over six months of Old School RuneScape data the price indices by quartile moved -0.18%, +4.91%, +11.97% and -5.30%, and the top 20 items made up 69.7% of the top-100 traded volume (peer-reviewed conference paper, IEEE Conference on Games 2019, full text verified; its unit-root statements are internally inconsistent and are not used here; [Scholten et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06721)). The practical reading is item-specific: a good whose production gets easier loses value even while the currency inflates, and a short basket of the most traded items is enough to track the price level.

Operators also change prices directly through recipes. Midnight patch 12.1.5 cut the material costs of more than 200 housing-decor recipes by up to 80% (news report based on Blizzard and test-realm data; no price series was found for the affected materials; [PC Games, 13 October 2026](https://www.pcgames.de/World-of-Warcraft-Spiel-42971/News/Dekorationen-guenstiger-Housing-1556580/)). On the enforcement side, Blizzard reported closing or suspending more than 74,000 accounts in one month in 2020, mostly for automation, and in November 2023 restricted gold purchases of tokens to accounts with at least 30 days of paid game time (official Blizzard sources; [Kaivax, 17 June 2020](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/actions-taken-to-address-exploitative-gameplay/558339); [Kaivax, 17 November 2023](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/1714609)). No source at any tier measures what such bans do to item prices.

### The market behaves competitively, so edges on liquid goods are thin

The one peer-reviewed study of single-item WoW prices with a readable full text covers netherweave cloth on six servers from 13 September to 13 October 2008. Median prices ranged from 11.77 to 17.50 gold across servers, mean and median were nearly identical on each server (for example 12.00 against 12.29), and in each of three server pairs the server with the larger share of level 58 to 70 characters had the higher median, with a quadratic fit of R² = .993 through only six points (peer-reviewed study; the author was an undergraduate and the project came from a high-school research programme; [Kosminsky 2010](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/view/869/634)). The same paper tested conversion arbitrage on game-wide prices from a different data site with no stated dates: three Lesser Planar Essences convert to one Greater and back, modal prices sat at exactly 5.00 and 15.00 gold, and mean prices (6.02 and 17.12 against parity-implied 5.71 and 18.06) left roughly 5% to be made by splitting Greater into Lesser, which the author attributes to supply of Greater essences from level-70 players ([Kosminsky 2010](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/view/869/634)). The paper does not test moving goods between servers, and its stability result is not clean: medium-population servers were the least stable (standard deviation 2.35, against 1.62 on low-population and 0.895 on high-population servers), so it does not support a simple "bigger market, steadier price" rule.

Two further papers find that price levels across eight structurally identical WoW worlds converge and stay inside a band of inaction created by trade barriers, with divergences reverting quickly (peer-reviewed studies, abstract only; [Morrison & Fontenla 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/empeco/v44y2013i3p1053-1064.html); [Morrison & Fontenla 2017](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ecolet:v:158:y:2017:i:c:p:62-66)). The abstracts do not say how goods moved between worlds, how wide the band was or which goods were sampled, so these papers support the statement that linked markets do not drift apart; they are not evidence that a player can trade across realms. Related work points the same way: a new EverQuest II server quickly reproduced the macroeconomic aggregates of existing servers (peer-reviewed study, abstract only; [Castronova et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444809105346)), and a controlled experiment that doubled a potion's price cut purchases by 43.1%, an implied elasticity of -0.431 from 43 players (working paper, abstract only; [Castronova 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ces/ceswps/_2355.html)). Textbook elasticity logic explains why this matters for pricing: for necessities without close substitutes a price rise increases revenue, while luxuries with substitutes sell only at competitive prices (textbook; [OpenStax, Principles of Economics 3e, section 5.3](https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/5-3-elasticity-and-pricing)). No elasticity estimate from real WoW market data exists.

Taken together, the defensible position is that a WoW auction house is a competitive, cheap-to-enter market. Kosminsky's argument that most goods come from abundant monsters, so new sellers enter quickly, is qualitative, and the speed of that supply response has never been measured. Durable margins should therefore be sought where entry is restricted, not where a price merely looks low.

### Launches and patches produce short spikes; weekly cycles are unproven

Lifecycle effects have the support described above (token drift after an expansion, deflation after production changes). The finer-grained claims do not. Community columns from 2011 describe patch-day gem spikes that faded and a case in which tailors who stockpiled for a new bag pattern oversupplied it and sold below material cost (community columns, Cataclysm-era, single author; [Engadget, 26 September 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011/09/26/gold-capped-preparing-for-and-making-money-off-of-patch-4-3/); [Engadget, 12 May 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011/05/12/gold-capped-how-to-profit-from-every-single-new-patch/)). These illustrate that crowded speculation erases its own profit, but no source quantifies typical pre-patch run-ups or post-patch decay.

For weekday and hour-of-day price cycles in WoW **no peer-reviewed study, working paper or primary-data analysis was found**. The only semi-quantified evidence is a 2012 column using charts from one realm with no stated data source or date range: supply peaked at the Tuesday reset and thinned over the following days, listings rose on weekends, and prices were much less predictable than quantities (one consumable rose nearly 50% in a low-supply week while a gem stayed in a 210 to 230 range), with the author concluding that no perfect pattern exists (community column; [Engadget, 7 February 2012](https://www.engadget.com/2012/02/07/what-days-should-you-buy-or-sell-on-the-auction-house/)). The Old School RuneScape index study reports no obvious seasonal effects, judged visually and in a game without weekly raid lockouts ([Scholten et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06721)), and a study of a Chinese online game finds a one-day periodicity in the number of avatars online, which concerns activity and not prices (peer-reviewed study, abstract only; [Jiang et al. 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.4827)). A weekly pattern is plausible, and its direction is consistent across community sources, but its size is unknown, so it should be measured per item on the player's own data and never hard-coded.

### Retail fee and market rules are documented; some only by wiki or tools

For retail the frictions are as follows. The house keeps 5% of the winning bid, and the deposit is 15%, 30% or 60% of the item's vendor sell price for 12, 24 or 48 hours with a 1 silver minimum, returned on sale and lost on expiry or cancellation (reference wiki; the page carries an out-of-date notice and describes retail only; [Warcraft Wiki, Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House)). The 5% figure is corroborated by a current retail-only addon that hard-codes a 0.95 multiplier (source code; [CraftSim Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua)); Blizzard's own 2018 post confirms only that deposits are based on vendor price, and it announced a planned per-stack deposit increase for some profession materials, to be tested first, with 20% of the asking price appearing only as a worked example and no evidence in the post that it shipped (official Blizzard source; [Ythisens, 22 August 2018](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/auction-house-update/10385)). Since patch 8.3 stackable commodities are bought by quantity from the cheapest listings and priced in silver and gold only, and since patch 9.2.7 commodity listings are shared by all realms in a region while armor and weapons stay realm-specific (official Blizzard sources; [8.3 auction house preview](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23236723); [9.2.7 update notes](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23833174)). The claim that the newest listing sells first among equal commodity prices is not in Blizzard's text; it rests on TradeSkillMaster's documentation, which sets its retail undercut default to zero for that reason (tool's own documentation, not official; [TSM Auctioning operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/1072146-tsm-addon-auctioning-operations)).

Two data facts shape everything an addon can know. Blizzard's web API publishes auction snapshots hourly, per connected realm and as one regional commodities document (official Blizzard sources, posts by accounts titled Blizzard Developer; [Araspir, 12 February 2020](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/blizzard/t/world-of-warcraft-api-update-visions-of-nzoth/3461); [Korakk, 18 August 2022](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/blizzard/t/immediate-change-to-auction-apis-for-commodities-with-927/31522)). Those snapshots contain listings, quantities and prices only, never sales (tool's own documentation; [Undermine Exchange API](https://undermine.exchange/api.html)). Every "sale rate" or "sold per day" figure is therefore either inferred from listings or taken from players' pooled transaction logs, which is how TradeSkillMaster builds its regional sale statistics (tool's own documentation; [TSM price sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)).

## Auction-house trading: the fee arithmetic is verified, the buy thresholds are convention

### Flipping rests on a reference price that is not a sale price

Flipping means buying a listing below a reference value and reposting near that value. The reference most players use is TradeSkillMaster's market value: per scan it considers at most the cheapest 30% of auctions, stops at a price jump of 20% or more once 15% of auctions are included, drops prices more than 1.5 standard deviations from the mean, averages the rest, and blends daily values over 14 days with a half-life of a little over two days (tool's own documentation, last updated 2021; this article is the only first-party evidence, since the public Lua implementation was written from it and does not independently confirm it; [TSM, AuctionDB market value](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/auctiondb-market-value/how-is-auctiondb-market-value-calculated)). TradeSkillMaster itself states that this value reflects listings, not sales ([TSM price sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)), which is the root of the most common loss: an item nobody buys can still show a high market value.

The buy rules built on that reference are conventions, not measured optima. One author recommends buying trade materials at 80% of market value or lower, selling at about market value, and skipping materials worth less than five times their vendor price because deposits eat the margin (community convention; single author whose site has a Patreon and a TradeSkillMaster affiliate link; [The Lazy Goldmaker, material flipping](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/material-flipping-is-still-the-king-of-entry-level-goldmaking-in-classic)). The same author suggests 60% of market value for non-crafted bind-on-equip gear ([The Lazy Goldmaker, BoE flipping](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/low-effort-gold-flipping-boes-in-wrath-classic)), and a second site suggests a 15 to 30% target margin (community convention; the publisher sells gold and boosting; [TimeSaver, Classic Era flipping](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-classic-era-auction-house-flipping-gold)). The arithmetic on the first rule is instructive (researchers' derivation, valid for a 5% cut): buying at 80% and selling at 100% of market value nets 15 points of market value, an 18.75% return on cost before deposit losses, so the rule leaves a 15-point cushion for any error in the reference price. No dataset of realised flipping margins exists, and no source at any tier gives a recommended minimum sale rate. Third-party tools rank candidates on sale-based data: Saddlebag Exchange multiplies sales per day by price to size a market and classifies the current minimum against the average with bands at plus or minus 15% and 60% (tool's own documentation; [Saddlebag Exchange market overview](https://saddlebagexchange.com/blog/wow/how-to-trade-using-our-wow-market-overview)). The evidence-backed version of flipping is therefore: use a robust reference, require evidence of turnover, and rank by profit after cut and expected deposit loss.

### Sniping, bids and vendor floors are mechanical; their payoff is unmeasured

Sniper tools re-run a filtered search and alert the player. In retail TradeSkillMaster repeats a shopping search with a 30-second delay; in Classic it rescans the last page, where new listings appear (tool's own documentation; [TSM Sniper operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/tsm-addon-sniper-operations)). The maximum-price rules players attach to it are conventions, for example a tiered share of a deliberately conservative reference (the minimum of several averages), from 80% for the most valuable items down to 10% for cheap ones, with thresholds stated in gold at 2018 retail price levels (community convention, single author; [The Lazy Goldmaker, sniper guide](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/tsm4-guide-part-10-finding-the-best-deals-with-sniper)). The same guide warns anecdotally of bait listings that are cheaper than an inflated history but still expensive. Taking the minimum of several references is the one design idea here worth copying, because a single manipulated average then cannot trigger a buy. No data on snipe frequency or realised profit was found.

Bid-only auctions are a structural blind spot in Classic-style houses: listings without a buyout sort last by unit price, bids are held in escrow and cannot be withdrawn, and late bids extend the remaining time (reference wiki, describing the classic bid system; [Warcraft Wiki, Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House)). Research on eBay and Amazon shows that late bidding is driven by a hard closing time and largely disappears when auctions extend after late bids (peer-reviewed study, abstract only; [Roth & Ockenfels 2002](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7729)), so in WoW the edge comes from other buyers not looking at such auctions, not from last-second timing. Two rules need no market judgment at all: a listing priced below its vendor sell value is a risk-free purchase as long as the stored vendor price is current, and items a vendor sells without limit cannot sustainably trade above the vendor's price. The reference wiki documents 99 limited-quantity vendor recipes across eight professions, which are the classic resale candidates (reference wiki, Classic-era content, not re-checked by the audits; [Warcraft Wiki, Limited quantity recipes](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Limited_quantity_recipes)); the usual markup rule of 30% over vendor cost is a single-author convention (community convention; [The Lazy Goldmaker, vendor recipes](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/classic-goldmaking-make-easy-gold-flipping-vendor-recipes)).

### Resetting a market is the least supported strategy

A reset buys every listing below a target price and relists higher. Everything practitioners say about choosing targets (limited supply, not farmable, few listings, a visible gap in the price ladder, at least a million gold of bankroll) comes from one author (community convention; [The Lazy Goldmaker, resetting markets](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/advanced-gold-making-tactics-resetting-markets)). The best documented case is a failure: about 1.3 million gold spent on roughly 1,600 bolts to lift the price to 1,200 gold, only 200 to 300 sold in three hours of active reposting, 500 mis-posted at 5 gold, and a net loss of about 300,000 gold on a stake that was about 2% of the author's wealth (community case study; [The Lazy Goldmaker, Chronocloth reset](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/i-spent-1-3-million-gold-resetting-the-chronocloth-market)). The binding constraint was volume at the target price, not the cost to clear.

Finance research explains why. Trade-based manipulation is profitable in theory when other traders think the manipulator is informed (peer-reviewed study, abstract only; [Allen & Gale 1992](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v5y1992i3p503-29.html)); historical corners raised volatility and hurt other assets (peer-reviewed study, abstract only; [Allen, Litov & Mei 2006](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/revfin/v10y2006i4p645-693.html)); and arbitrage is carried out by few capital-constrained traders and fails when prices move far from fundamentals (peer-reviewed study, abstract only; [Shleifer & Vishny 1997](https://www.nber.org/papers/w5167)). Those markets have fixed supply. In an MMO supply is produced on demand, which is why removing supply raised prices in the RuneScape sink study only because the developer kept removing it. No study of resets inside a game auction house exists, so the position here is that a reset is a bet on rivals' and farmers' response time and should be refused whenever the item can be crafted or farmed for less than the target price.

### Location arbitrage is narrow on retail and fee-bound elsewhere

On retail, factions share one house and commodities are region-wide, so the only location gap left is realm-to-realm for non-commodity items, which the Warband bank can move because it spans every realm and faction in a region and accepts any non-soulbound item (official Blizzard source; [Warbands overview](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24115313/)). An in-game addon sees only the house it is standing in, so this strategy needs scans stored per character or outside data. For Classic-style clients the faction markets connect through neutral houses; the only opened source for those fees is a 2007 guide giving a 5% faction and 15% neutral cut, with deposits of 5%, 20% and 60% of vendor value for 2, 8 and 24 hours and exactly five times that at neutral houses (community guide; no official or wiki confirmation exists; [Ten Ton Hammer, Auctioneer guide](https://www.tentonhammer.com/guides/auctioneer-guide-page-2)). Whatever the actual numbers, the rule is the band of inaction from the convergence papers: a gap is actionable only when it exceeds the full round-trip cost.

### Posting behaviour: undercut cycles, sale probability and stale stock

In an auction house a seller sells only when priced at or below every competing listing, which Kosminsky states as the basic rule of the market ([Kosminsky 2010](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/view/869/634)). Price-setting theory predicts the resulting sawtooth: in alternating-move competition, cycles of small undercuts followed by a jump back up are an equilibrium that keeps profits above the competitive level (peer-reviewed study, abstract only; [Maskin & Tirole 1988](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecm/emetrp/v56y1988i3p571-99.html)), and gasoline data show exactly that shape, least often in the most and least concentrated markets (peer-reviewed study, abstract only; [Doyle, Muehlegger & Samphantharak 2010](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/edgeworth-cycles-revisited-0)). TradeSkillMaster's posting model reflects practice: a minimum, normal and maximum price with explicit actions when the market is below the minimum or above the maximum, an undercut default of zero on retail and one copper on Classic, and cancel-scanning that costs one forfeited deposit per cancel ([TSM Auctioning operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/1072146-tsm-addon-auctioning-operations)).

Two findings from market microstructure transfer directly. Execution time for limit orders is very sensitive to the limit price and not to order size, and hypothetical fills constructed from price paths are very poor proxies for real ones (peer-reviewed study, abstract only; [Lo, MacKinlay & Zhang 2002](https://web.mit.edu/~alo/www/Papers/limit10.html)); sale probability must therefore be calibrated from the player's own sales and expiries, not from "the price touched my level". And a dealer holding inventory should accept a lower price the more units are held, the more volatile the price and the longer the horizon: the reservation price is `r = s - q·γ·σ²·(T - t)`, with fill rates decaying exponentially in the distance of the quote from the market, and the inventory-aware strategy produced lower profit variance in simulation (peer-reviewed article, full text of the preprint verified; [Avellaneda & Stoikov 2008](https://people.orie.cornell.edu/sfs33/LimitOrderBook.pdf)). That is the theoretical basis for marking down stale stock instead of reposting it at the original price.

### Position sizing has theory but no game-specific rule

No source at any tier states a maximum share of gold per market. Practice is expressed as diversification and minimum bankrolls. The theory is Kelly's: betting everything maximises expected wealth but ruins the bettor with probability one, and for an even-money bet won with probability q the growth-optimal fraction is q minus (1 - q) (journal article in the Bell System Technical Journal, a corporate technical journal, full text verified; [Kelly 1956](https://www.princeton.edu/~wbialek/rome/refs/kelly_56.pdf)). The general payoff form and the practice of betting half or a quarter of that fraction when probabilities are estimated come from a reference site only ([Wikipedia, Kelly criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion)). Market impact in financial markets grows with the square root of order size relative to daily volume, validated for orders up to a few percent of daily volume in futures (peer-reviewed study, full text verified; [Tóth et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1694)); this is not evidence about auction houses, where the exact answer comes from walking the listing ladder, as TradeSkillMaster's own example shows: the five cheapest units cost 12.2 each and the fifteen cheapest 16.3 each ([TSM, AuctionDB market value](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/auctiondb-market-value/how-is-auctiondb-market-value-calculated)).

## Crafting margins persist where capacity is capped

### The profit formula is settled; the sale price is the weak input

The tools agree on the skeleton: profit equals expected output times sale price after the cut, minus the cheapest cost of each reagent. TradeSkillMaster defines crafting cost as the sum of material prices and profit as crafted value minus that cost (tool's own documentation; [TSM Crafting operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/1236068-tsm-addon-crafting-operations)). CraftSim, a current retail-only addon, multiplies sale value by 0.95, converts profession ratings to chances by dividing by 1100 (multicraft), 900 (resourcefulness) and 1000 (ingenuity), estimates extra items on a multicraft proc from constants keyed to base yield (2.1, 1.83, 1.875, otherwise 2.5), and assumes a resourcefulness proc saves 30% of required-reagent cost; the source labels both proc models as approximations, and no game-data source for the true distributions was found (source code; [CraftSim Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua); [CraftSim ProfitCalculation.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua)). One implementation detail should not be copied: in the code path for recipes with stats the expected deposit is subtracted twice. Blizzard's own description confirms what the stats do: multicraft gives a chance of extra items on stackable recipes only, and resourcefulness a chance to use fewer tradable reagents (official Blizzard source, describing the 10.0 launch system; [Dragonflight: Making It with Professions](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23876529/dragonflight-making-it-with-professions)).

For reagent cost, a third-party private-server port of legacy TradeSkillMaster (TSM Revived for Ascension, based on TSM 2.8.3) prices each material at `min(dbmarket, crafting, vendorbuy, convert(dbmarket))`, that is the cheapest of buying, crafting, a vendor, or converting from another item; it values the craft at the minimum buyout, deducts no auction cut unless the user sets one, and skips cooldown recipes as a cost basis when another recipe exists (source code, third-party legacy port that may carry porter edits, not TradeSkillMaster's current defaults; [TradeSkillMaster_Crafting.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting.lua); [Cost.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/Modules/Cost.lua)). CraftSim prefers a vendor price whenever one is known and otherwise defaults to `first(DBRecent, DBMinbuyout)` (source code; [CraftSim PriceAPIs.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceAPIs.lua)). The weak input in all of them is the sale price: a current minimum buyout overstates profit when the cheapest listing is thin, and a listing-based average can show profit on an item that rarely sells. CraftSim's own guard is a regional sale-rate threshold of 0.1 before a scanned recipe is queued ([CraftSim Const.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Util/Const.lua)). An addon without pooled sale data needs its own turnover estimate to play that role.

### Destroy chains and conversions are expected values with contested yield tables

Milling, prospecting and disenchanting are valued as an expectation: the sum over outputs of expected quantity times output price, which is TradeSkillMaster's Destroy source ([TSM price sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)). The yield tables behind such values are legacy and inconsistent. The third-party TSM port stores prospecting rates per five ore (Copper Ore: Malachite 0.5, Tigerseye 0.5, Shadowgem 0.1; Saronite Ore: 0.2 per uncommon gem, 0.04 per rare gem) (source code, third-party legacy port; [Conversions.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster/Data/Conversions.lua)). For disenchanting, an archived first-party Auctionator table gives uncommon armor of item level 56 to 60 as 37.5% one dust, 37.5% two dust, 10% one essence, 10% two essences and 5% a shard, but its reference row for Infinite Dust (item level 121 to 151, one to three dust, an expectation of 1.5 at a 75% chance) disagrees with the TSM port and the wiki (130 to 151, two to three dust, 1.875) (source code, archived 2021; [BCC-Auctionator DisenchantingProbabilities.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Auctionator/BCC-Auctionator/main/Source/Constants/DisenchantingProbabilities.lua); [Disenchanting.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster/Data/Disenchanting.lua)). Any worked example using 1.875 dust per item is therefore one of two published values. No game-data-derived yield tables were found for current retail expansions, and nothing documents whether WoW Forever changes the Classic tables.

Free, reversible conversions are priced close to parity. The essence result above (modal prices at exact 1:3 parity, mean prices about 5% apart, one pair, 2008) is the only measurement, and the paper explains the gap by supply, not by fees ([Kosminsky 2010](https://jvwr-ojs-utexas.tdl.org/jvwr/article/view/869/634)). That a gap of about the size of the auction cut is the natural no-trade band is the researchers' inference, not a finding of the paper. Structurally, crafting trees are directed acyclic graphs solvable in linear time by relaxing vertices in topological order, and reversible conversions are the currency-arbitrage problem, solved by finding a negative cycle on negated log rates (textbook; [Sedgewick & Wayne, Algorithms, section 4.4](https://algs4.cs.princeton.edu/44sp/)). Because routing changes with prices, as a 2010 column on the Wrath-era ore shuffle described when falling dust prices flipped the best exit for green gems from disenchanting to vendoring (community column, historical; [Engadget, 14 April 2010](https://www.engadget.com/2010-04-14-gold-capped-the-saronite-shuffle.html)), the best branch must be recomputed from live prices at each node.

### Cooldowns, access and concentration are where margins survive

The synthesis that best fits the evidence is that unrestricted crafts are competed down toward fees and effort, while margins survive on capped capacity. Three observations support it; none is a direct measurement. First, both crafting tools deliberately refuse to price a material at the input cost of a cooldown recipe, treating cooldown output as worth its market price ([Cost.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/Modules/Cost.lua); [CraftSim PriceSource.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceSource.lua)). Second, the game restricts who can craft: Classic's Arcanite transmute shows a two-day cooldown in the Classic database (database site built from client data and crowd uploads; [Wowhead Classic, Transmute: Arcanite](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=17187/transmute-arcanite)), and limited-quantity vendor recipes gate entry further ([Warcraft Wiki, Limited quantity recipes](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Limited_quantity_recipes)). Third, retail adds concentration, capped at 1,000 and regenerating one point every six minutes, so 240 points per profession per day (reference wiki; [Warcraft Wiki, Concentration](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Concentration)); CraftSim values it as average profit with concentration divided by the effective concentration cost, where an ingenuity proc refunds a base 50%, and spending it raises the result by exactly one quality tier (source code; [CraftSim RecipeData.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Classes/RecipeData.lua)). Retail crafting orders are a separate income line with no auction cut: for public orders the requester supplies the tradable reagents and the crafter collects the commission minus a small Artisan's Consortium cut whose percentage Blizzard's post does not state ([Dragonflight: Making It with Professions](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23876529/dragonflight-making-it-with-professions)). A cooldown's value is its best profit per use spread over its length, and cooldown, concentration and knowledge are per character, which is why alts multiply this kind of income until the extra volume moves a thin market.

### How much to craft: a restock rule, sized by turnover

The documented mechanism for quantity is a restock rule. TradeSkillMaster queues crafts up to a maximum stock level, suppresses the queue when fewer than a minimum batch would be added, and requires a minimum profit, with shipped defaults of 10, 20 and 100 gold (tool's own documentation; [TSM default operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/addon/how-do-the-default-operations-work)). The legacy third-party port makes the arithmetic explicit as `queue = max(maxRestock - inventory, 0)`, zero below the minimum batch or minimum profit, with legacy defaults of 1, 3 and 100 gold (source code, third-party legacy port; [Queue.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/Modules/Queue.lua)). A 2011 practitioner column describes the same habit as keeping about three of each gem cut listed and judging profit by gold, by percentage of material cost, or both (community column; [Engadget, 21 March 2011](https://www.engadget.com/2011-03-21-gold-capped-tradeskillmaster-advanced-guide.html)). What no source quantifies is the relation between posted quantity and price; "do not post more than about a day of sales" is the researchers' rule, grounded only in the ladder arithmetic above.

## Farming rates are unverified; a player's own log is the only evidence

### Every published gold-per-hour number is a self-report

Academic work on "gold farming" concerns real-money trade, which Blizzard prohibits; its role taxonomy (hunter, gatherer, producer, dealer and others) is nonetheless the closest thing to a citable classification of what farmers do (working paper, full text verified; the author warns it relies on secondary, mainly unrefereed sources; [Heeks 2008](https://hummedia.manchester.ac.uk/institutes/gdi/publications/workingpapers/di/di_wp32.pdf)). For in-game farming by ordinary play, **no dated, methodology-backed gold-per-hour measurement exists for any farm in any client**. One undated Classic Era guide lists self-reported, unverified estimates that cluster between 25 and 120 gold per hour, its Zul'Farrak figure is a general estimate, its Dire Maul figures differ between its own table and text, and it gives no method, sample or date (community guide; [NoobToBoss](https://noobtoboss.com/wow-classic-mage-gold-farming-guide/)). The more careful guides now decline to quote a rate at all and tell readers to check their own auction house (community guide; the publisher sells gold and boosts; [Gamingcy](https://gamingcy.com/blog/midnight-dual-gathering)). The much-cited USD 3.42 hourly wage is an EverQuest figure from a 2001 abstract valued in dollars and says nothing about WoW gold per hour (working paper, abstract only; [Castronova 2001](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ces/ceswps/_618.html)). Guide figures are useful at most for ordering farms within one guide.

### Session trackers measure asset value at listing prices

The trackers players use compute looted value divided by active time. LootAppraiser Reloaded divides total looted item value by session seconds net of pauses and multiplies by 3,600; its default price source is a listing-based regional average, it falls back to vendor price, and nowhere in the valuation path does it subtract the auction cut, lost deposits or a sale-rate discount (source code; [UI.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/berickus/LootAppraiserReloaded/HEAD/Core/UI/UI.lua); [LootProcessing.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/berickus/LootAppraiserReloaded/HEAD/Core/LootProcessing.lua)). MoneyLooter adds a per-quality minimum price below which gear is valued at disenchant value, and smooths the rate with a moving average (tool's own page, not re-checked by the audits; [MoneyLooter](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/moneylooter)). These numbers are estimates of asset value, not income. Three different quantities hide behind "gold per hour": raw coin (exact), vendor value (exact, because the client returns each item's vendor price in copper; reference wiki; [C_Item.GetItemInfo](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_Item.GetItemInfo)), and auction value (an estimate that depends on the price source and on whether the item sells).

### Finding farms means starting from prices and the player's own sessions

The game client does not expose drop rates, and the gathering database addon records only node positions on gathering casts, with no loot quantities or timestamps (source code; [GatherMate2 Collector.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Nevcairiel/GatherMate2/master/Collector.lua)). A farm finder therefore has two honest options: ship a curated item-to-source table, or work backwards from the auction house (which high-value items turn over fast enough to absorb a farmer's output) and then score candidate farms from the player's own logged sessions. Entry caps bound instance farms, and the sources disagree on them: Blizzard's 2020 Classic post set 30 instances per day alongside 5 per hour, citing exploitative and automated gameplay (official Blizzard source; [Kaivax, 16 June 2020](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/556616)); the reference wiki states 10 per hour per account for classic and modern modes as of the Midnight era ([Warcraft Wiki, Instance](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Instance)); a tracker's settings text refers to 10 resets per hour and a guide still plans around 5. Blizzard's current support article could not be opened, so the cap must be a setting. Blizzard also throttles repeated layer changes on Classic, a measure its post describes as restricting frequent layer-hopping (official Blizzard source; [Kaivax, September 2019](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/290703)).

The rules that bound farming are clear in the official texts: the licence agreement prohibits bots and exploits, gathering for sale outside the game, and in-game services for payment (official Blizzard source; [Blizzard EULA](https://www.blizzard.com/en-us/legal/fba4d00f-c7e4-4883-b8b9-1b4500a402ea/blizzard-end-user-license-agreement)), and input broadcasting and any automation of multi-boxing are actionable (official Blizzard source; [policy update, May 2021](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/policy-update-for-input-broadcasting-may-2021/956610)). Hyperspawn spots are a grey area discussed only by players and press, so an addon should neither rank nor recommend them.

## What an addon can compute from its own data

### What the client lets an addon do

The design envelope is set by API restrictions documented on the reference wiki for retail behaviour; none of it has been checked in a Forever client. A full auction dump through `C_AuctionHouse.ReplicateItems` is throttled to one successful call per 15 minutes, account-wide ([ReplicateItems](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.ReplicateItems)); the legacy `QueryAuctionItems` path is throttled at about 0.3 seconds per page query and 15 minutes for a full dump ([QueryAuctionItems](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_QueryAuctionItems)). The functions tagged as requiring a hardware event and barred from scripts are `PostItem`, `PostCommodity`, `PlaceBid`, `StartCommoditiesPurchase` and `CancelAuction`, plus the legacy [`PostAuction`](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_PostAuction) and [`PlaceAuctionBid`](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_PlaceAuctionBid); `ConfirmCommoditiesPurchase` and `CraftRecipe` carry no hardware-event tag, and `SendMail` and `SendBrowseQuery` are script-restricted only ([World of Warcraft API index](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/World_of_Warcraft_API); [PostItem](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.PostItem); [PlaceBid](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.PlaceBid)). `PostItem` requires whole-silver prices, while the `PlaceBid` page says gold only, which is probably a wiki slip and should be tested in the client. Blizzard's add-on policy requires add-ons to be free with visible code and not to harm realms or other players; its text does not mention automation (official Blizzard source; [UI Add-On Development Policy](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/ui-add-on-development-policy/24534)). The compliant pattern is therefore analysis done automatically from saved scans, with each post, purchase, bid and cancel bound to one user click.

One storage decision precedes all signals. Depth, cost to clear and a filtered price cannot be reconstructed afterwards from a stored minimum price and total quantity; they have to be derived from the full listing ladder at scan time and saved as summaries. Existing tools show how little is enough: a private-server port of Auctionator 9.1.3 (about five years old; the current upstream source is not public) stores per item the last seen minimum plus, per day, the low and high of the minimums and the maximum quantity ([Auctionator Database Mixin.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sirus-addons/Auctionator/HEAD/Auctionator/Source/Database/Mixin.lua)), and Undermine Exchange's backend keeps 14 days of per-snapshot history per item ([shatari constants.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/erorus/shatari/HEAD/src/constants.js)).

### Robust usual price (median and MAD)

Formula: per scan compute one ladder-based value, per day reduce the scans to one value, then take `usual = median(dailyValue over W days)` and `scale = 1.4826 × MAD`, where `MAD = median(|x - median(x)|)`. The scale definition and the reasons to prefer it are from the government reference handbook: the standard deviation is distorted by tail values and MAD is not ([NIST e-Handbook, Measures of Scale](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda356.htm)). For the per-scan value three documented models exist: the cost per unit of the cheapest 15% of available quantity (the site author's own definition; [Erorus, method notes](https://gist.github.com/erorus/09e2d19f1dd114c3588ff4141063a760)), TradeSkillMaster's filtered mean of the cheap end described earlier ([TSM, AuctionDB market value](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/auctiondb-market-value/how-is-auctiondb-market-value-calculated)), and the plain daily minimum buyout. For the across-days step the precedents are a weighted median of at most 11 daily minimums with weight 0.99 per day of age (source code, official aux repository; [aux history.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shirsig/aux-addon/master/core/history.lua)) and a 4-day median per house (source code, official; [OribosExchange.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/erorus/shatari/HEAD/addon/OribosExchange.lua)). Combining a cheapest-fraction scan value with a median over days is the researchers' derivation. Data needed: the listing ladder at each scan and one stored value per item per day. Windows grounded in the tools are 4 days (Oribos Exchange), 14 days (TradeSkillMaster) and 60 days as a slow anchor ([TSM price sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)). If decayed weights are wanted, the only published daily weights come from one third-party backport and are consistent with the documented half-life; they are not TradeSkillMaster's published weights ([backport Core.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Keoo88/TradeSkillMaster-3.3.5-backport/HEAD/TradeSkillMaster_AuctionDB/Core.lua)). Failure modes: MAD is zero for items whose price barely moves (fall back to a percentage band); a level shift after a patch makes the window stale; scans happen only when the player visits the house, so days are unevenly covered; a stored minimum alone is one seller's opinion.

### Deal score

Formula: `M = 0.6745 × (priceNow - usual) / MAD`, with a deal flagged when M is below -3.5 and the item has enough observations. The statistic, the 0.6745 constant and the 3.5 cutoff for potential outliers are from the handbook, which also warns that ordinary z-scores mislead in small samples because their maximum is `(n - 1)/√n`, about 2.85 with ten observations ([NIST e-Handbook, Detection of Outliers](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda35h.htm)). Using the outlier cutoff as a buy trigger is the researchers' derivation. Two simpler companions are a ratio band, `priceNow / usual`, with Saddlebag's classes at plus or minus 15% and 60% ([Saddlebag wiki, market overview](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ff14-advanced-market-search/saddlebag-with-pockets/master/app/content/blog/wiki/how-to-trade-using-our-wow-market-overview.md)), and the percentile rank of the current price in the item's own history. The 80% and 60% buy levels remain available as user settings, labelled as conventions. Data needed: the usual price and scale above plus the current ladder. Failure modes: a single cheap unit triggers the score but cannot be bought in size (pair it with cost to clear); a falling market looks like a string of deals (pair it with the regime flag below); bait listings against an inflated history; the originator of Bollinger bands makes the same point for mean-based bands, stating in his own rules that touching a band is not a signal (reference, originator's own site; [Bollinger Band Rules](https://www.bollingerbands.com/bollinger-band-rules)).

### Estimated sales per day from quantity changes between scans

Formula (researchers' derivation): `soldEst = Σ max(0, qty(t-1) - qty(t))` over the scans of a day, refined when the ladder is kept by counting only units that vanished from the cheap end, or listings that disappeared while their time-left band was longer than the gap between scans. The idea has precedent but no validation. A conference short paper inferred sellers' successful transactions by comparing successive hourly WoW auction lists across eight markets, describing the method without testing its accuracy (peer-reviewed conference short paper, preliminary results; [Hsu, Togelius & Sun 2014](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~sushil/pubs/newestPapers/aux/paper_104.pdf)); Auctioneer's Purchased statistic infers purchases from items that disappear before they could have expired and needs frequent scans (tool's own description; [Auctioneer](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/auctioneer)). By analogy only, a study of vending machines observed every four hours shows that ignoring availability between observations biases demand estimates (working paper, published version peer-reviewed, abstract only, vending machines not auctions; [Conlon & Mortimer](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14315)). Data needed: consecutive scans with quantity per price level and time-left bands. Failure modes: cancel-and-repost and expiries count as sales, so the figure is an upper bound; restocking between scans hides sales; gaps longer than the shortest duration lose information. The ledger gives a calibration: the player's own sales per day divided by the player's share of listed quantity is an independent turnover estimate.

### Days of supply and quantity remaining

Formula: `daysOfSupply = quantityListedNow / soldEstPerDay`, the accounting ratio "days' sales in inventory" expressed in units, where a rising figure signals slow sales (textbook; [OpenStax, Principles of Accounting, section 10.5](https://openstax.org/books/principles-financial-accounting/pages/10-5-examine-the-efficiency-of-inventory-management-using-financial-ratios)). A companion that needs no sales estimate is `quantityNow / averageQuantity`, which Saddlebag uses to flag commodities that may rise when stock runs out ([Saddlebag wiki, market overview](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ff14-advanced-market-search/saddlebag-with-pockets/master/app/content/blog/wiki/how-to-trade-using-our-wow-market-overview.md)). Data needed: current quantity, its history, and the sales estimate. Failure modes: it inherits every bias of the sales estimate; total quantity includes overpriced stock that will never sell, so supply should be counted only within a band above the minimum; expiry waves and one large seller logging off look like shortages.

### Cost to clear the cheapest listings

Formula: `costToClear(P) = Σ price_i × qty_i for price_i ≤ P`; `avgFill(N)` = cost of the cheapest N units divided by N; `slippage(N) = avgFill(N) / minPrice - 1`. A partial-reset target is the largest relative gap between consecutive price levels, with the quantity below the gap as the cost. These are direct sums over the ladder; TradeSkillMaster's 20% jump rule is the precedent for gap detection and its 12.2 against 16.3 example shows why a unit price depends on the quantity wanted ([TSM, AuctionDB market value](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/auctiondb-market-value/how-is-auctiondb-market-value-calculated)). The decision rule for resets, `profit = units × P × (1 - cut) - costToClear - expected deposit loss`, rejected when `units / soldPerDay` is long relative to how fast supply can return, is the researchers' derivation, motivated by the Chronocloth case ([The Lazy Goldmaker](https://thelazygoldmaker.com/i-spent-1-3-million-gold-resetting-the-chronocloth-market)). Data needed: the full ladder at scan time, or stored summaries such as quantity within 5% and 20% of the minimum. Failure modes: the ladder is stale within minutes on busy items; fresh supply arrives; volume at the target price, not cost, is the binding constraint. Without a ladder the square-root impact law gives only an order of magnitude and was validated in futures markets, not auction houses ([Tóth et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1694)).

### Expected flip profit after cut and deposit

Formula (researchers' derivation): with buy price B, intended sale price S, cut c, deposit d per posting and probability p that one posting sells, `E[profit] = S × (1 - c) - B - d × (1 - p) / p`, and break-even `S_min = (B + d × (1 - p) / p) / (1 - c)`. The fee structure behind it (cut on sale, deposit returned on sale and lost otherwise) is from the reference wiki for retail ([Warcraft Wiki, Auction House](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Auction_House)). The cut is 0.05 on retail only; on any other client it is a parameter to be read or measured. The deposit should come from the client, which exposes `C_AuctionHouse.CalculateItemDeposit` (listed for standard since 8.3.0, forever 1.60.1 and mists 4.4.2; [CalculateItemDeposit](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.CalculateItemDeposit)). The sale probability comes from the ledger as `p = sales / (sales + expiries + cancels)` per item, the same quantity TradeSkillMaster exposes as the player's own sale rate ([TSM value sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/en_US/custom-strings/which-value-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)); ranking then uses profit per day held, `E[profit] / (duration / p)`. Data needed: ladder, fee parameters, and ledger entries for sales, expiries and cancels. Failure modes: p is not constant when competitors camp an item; the ledger is small for most items, so pooling by item class is needed; analysing only sold lots is survivorship bias; a snapshot-based stand-in for p is exactly the kind of hypothetical fill the limit-order literature finds unreliable ([Lo, MacKinlay & Zhang 2002](https://web.mit.edu/~alo/www/Papers/limit10.html)).

### Cheapest material source across buy, craft, vendor and convert

Formula: `unitCost(r) = min(vendorBuy(r), ahBuy(r), craftCostPerUnit(r), min over conversions of price(source) / rate)`, evaluated recursively with cooldown-gated recipes excluded from the craft branch and a guard against cycles. This is the documented behaviour of the tools: the legacy third-party TSM port's default material string and conversion routine ([TradeSkillMaster_Crafting.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting.lua); [Conversions.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster/Data/Conversions.lua)), CraftSim's vendor-first lookup and optional sub-recipe substitution ([CraftSim PriceSource.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/DataSource/PriceSource.lua)), and a tooltip addon in the Auctionator ecosystem that uses vendor price when present and auction price otherwise ([CraftInfoAnywhere](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TheMouseNest/CraftInfoAnywhere/main/Main_Classic.lua)). The linear-time evaluation order is the textbook shortest-path method for acyclic graphs ([Sedgewick & Wayne](https://algs4.cs.princeton.edu/44sp/)). Data needed: recipes and reagent lists, scan prices, vendor prices seen at vendors, a conversion table. Failure modes: a vendor's selling price is known only after the player has visited that vendor; the cheapest unit's price understates the cost of buying reagents for many crafts, so the ladder must be walked; self-gathered materials still cost their market price net of the cut; conversion tables are legacy and client-specific.

### Craft profit

Formula: `profit = yield × sellPrice × (1 - c) - Σ qty_r × unitCost(r) - expected deposit loss`, and on retail `yield = baseYield + E[extra] × pMulticraft` with material cost reduced by `pResourcefulness × 0.30 × (1 + bonus)` on tradable required reagents, as in CraftSim ([CraftSim ProfitCalculation.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/derfloh205/CraftSim/main/Pricing/ProfitCalculation.lua)). The retail proc constants are tool approximations, and CraftSim's double subtraction of the deposit should not be reproduced. The realisable value of any output has a floor: `max(vendorSell, sellPrice × (1 - c))`, which is how CraftSim treats junk-quality results (vendor price, no cut). Data needed: recipes, the cheapest-source costs above, the usual price of the output, turnover. Failure modes: minimum buyout as sale price overstates profit on thin ladders; without a turnover filter paper profits appear on items that never sell; on clients other than retail the cut is unverified.

### Restock quantity

Formula: `queue = max(maxRestock - owned, 0)`, set to zero when the queue is below a minimum batch or the craft is below a minimum profit; this is TradeSkillMaster's documented rule ([TSM Crafting operations](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/1236068-tsm-addon-crafting-operations)) and the explicit code of the legacy third-party port ([Queue.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/Modules/Queue.lua)). Scaling the target to demand, `maxRestock = clamp(round(soldEstPerDay × daysOfCover × targetShare), minBatch, cap)`, and making the minimum profit relative as well as absolute, are the researchers' derivations. Data needed: inventory across characters, sales estimate, craft profit. Failure modes: the sales estimate is an upper bound, so targets run high; the player's achievable share of sales is unknown until the ledger has history; no source quantifies how posted quantity moves price.

### Cooldown value

Formula: `goldPerDay = (outputPrice × (1 - c) × expectedYield - inputCost) × 24 / cooldownHours`; when several recipes share one cooldown its value is the maximum among them, and every other use carries that as opportunity cost. The per-use and per-day arithmetic is the method a commercial blog applies to cooldown goods (community source; the publisher sells gold, power leveling and profession kits; [TimeSaver, Forever cooldowns](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-profession-cooldowns-gold)); the shared-cooldown rule is the researchers' derivation, and the tools' exclusion of cooldown recipes from cost bases is the supporting design precedent ([Cost.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BanditTech/TradeSkillMaster/main/TradeSkillMaster_Crafting/Modules/Cost.lua)). Data needed: the cooldown length read from the client (published lengths conflict), recipe inputs, usual prices of inputs and output. Failure modes: cooldown goods trade in thin markets, so the output price moves when several alts sell; valuing per hour overstates income for a player who logs in once a day; asking prices are not sale prices.

### Per-item hour-of-week pattern from the player's own history

Formula (researchers' derivation on a textbook method): `index(bucket) = median over observations in the bucket of price_t / trend_t`, where `trend_t` is a centred 7-day rolling median of the daily value and buckets count hours since the region's weekly reset; indices are normalised to average 1 and computed for quantity as well as price. This is classical multiplicative decomposition with medians substituted for means; the textbook states its limits, which are no estimate at the ends of the series, over-smoothing of sudden moves, an assumed unchanging seasonal pattern, and no robustness to unusual values ([Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, Forecasting: Principles and Practice, section 3.4](https://otexts.com/fpp3/classical-decomposition.html)). An index should be shown only when its bucket has several observations across at least three to four weeks and when the spread between bucket medians exceeds the typical spread within a bucket. Data needed: timestamped scans over several weeks. Failure modes: the addon scans only when the player is at the auction house, so most buckets are empty or biased toward play hours; a patch inside the window breaks the pattern; pooling items to gain sample size assumes they share a cycle. Since the only published evidence found clearer cycles in quantity than in price ([Engadget, 7 February 2012](https://www.engadget.com/2012/02/07/what-days-should-you-buy-or-sell-on-the-auction-house/)), the quantity index is the one more likely to prove useful.

### Farm value from the player's own loot log, repriced at current prices

Formula: per session, `value = rawCoin + Σ qty_i × value_i`, and `perHour = value / activeSeconds × 3600` with paused time removed, which is LootAppraiser Reloaded's computation ([UI.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/berickus/LootAppraiserReloaded/HEAD/Core/UI/UI.lua)). The stricter per-item value is the researchers' derivation: `value_i = max(vendorSell_i, usual_i × (1 - c))` for tradable items with evidence of turnover, and vendor value (or disenchant value) otherwise. Storing item counts per session instead of gold lets every past session be repriced at today's usual prices, so farms can be compared at current conditions. The three components should be shown separately as raw coin, vendor floor and auction estimate, and the result labelled as the player's own measured rate, never as an expectation for others. Data needed: a loot log with item, quantity, location and session time; vendor prices from the client; the usual price and turnover signals. Failure modes: listing-based prices overstate what sells; the farmer's own output depresses thin markets; rare drops dominate short samples (one guide's own arithmetic gives a 36.77% chance of no drop in 1,000 kills at a 1-in-1,000 rate; community guide, not re-checked by the audits; [Warcraft Tavern](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/guides/wow-classic-gold-guide/)); travel, vendoring and mailing time belong inside the timed session; instance caps limit runs per hour and differ by client.

### Regime-break and shortage flags

Formula: on the standardised daily log price `z_t`, run a two-sided CUSUM, `S_hi = max(0, S_hi + z_t - k)` and `S_lo = max(0, S_lo - z_t - k)`, with k = 0.5 and an alarm at h of about 4 to 5; the recursions, the choice of k as half the shift to detect, and the advantage over single-point limits for shifts of two standard deviations or less are from the handbook ([NIST e-Handbook, CUSUM](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc323.htm)), as is the exponentially weighted moving average with a smoothing constant usually between 0.2 and 0.3 ([NIST e-Handbook, EWMA](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc324.htm)). Applying them to auction prices, standardising with the median and MAD, restarting the usual-price window at an alarm and suppressing deal flags until the new window fills are the researchers' derivations. A shortage flag combines quantity far below its own history with a rising price; a glut is the reverse, which is also the footprint of crowded speculation after a patch. Data needed: daily values and quantities. Failure modes: an outlier-inflated scale hides real shifts; gaps in scanning turn gradual moves into apparent jumps; the flags detect events without identifying causes.

### Destroy, conversion and vendor-floor values

Formula: `destroyValue(input) = Σ rate_i × value(output_i)`, with each output valued at the best of its auction price after the cut, its vendor price, or the value of a further transformation; a conversion of n low units into one high unit is worth acting on when `P_high × (1 - c) > n × P_low`, or in reverse where the conversion is reversible. The expectation is TradeSkillMaster's Destroy definition ([TSM price sources](https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/custom-strings/which-price-sources-can-i-use-and-what-do-they-mean)); the recursive routing and the fee band are the researchers' derivations. The vendor floor is the simplest signal of all: flag any listing whose unit buyout is below the vendor sell price. Data needed: yield and conversion tables per client, scan prices, vendor prices, the player's professions. Failure modes: published yield tables conflict and are legacy; outputs sold in bulk depress their own price, so the liquidity check belongs on the outputs; stored vendor prices go stale when a hotfix changes them.

### Position cap

Formula (researchers' derivation): `maxUnits = min(liquidityCap, kellyCap)`, with `liquidityCap = share × soldEstPerDay × targetHoldDays` and `kellyCap` a quarter to a half of `f = p / l - (1 - p) / g`, where g is the fractional gain if the item sells at target and l the fractional loss if it is dumped at its fallback value. Kelly's result supplies the logic and the ruin warning ([Kelly 1956](https://www.princeton.edu/~wbialek/rome/refs/kelly_56.pdf)); the general form and the fractional practice rest on a reference site ([Wikipedia, Kelly criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion)). Data needed: the sale probability and prices from the ledger, the sales estimate, liquid gold. Failure modes: p estimated from a few lucky sales overstates the cap sharply; payoffs are not binary; items in one category share demand shocks, so a per-category cap is needed as well. The liquidity cap is the more binding and the better supported of the two, and the figure should be displayed as a suggested maximum, not an instruction.

## WoW Forever: a handful of solid facts, and fee rules that belong to the client

### What rests on solid sources

A small set of Forever facts is well supported. The reference wiki documents World of Warcraft: Forever as a Blizzard client released on 4 November 2026, internal name Camelot, level cap 60, with one mega-realm per ruleset per region (reference wiki; [World of Warcraft: Forever](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/World_of_Warcraft:_Forever)). The TOC format page lists interface 16001, the suffixes `_Forever` and `_Camelot`, and the game types forever and camelot, and it warns that its values are contributor-maintained (reference wiki; [TOC format](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/TOC_format)). Two Blizzard staff posts are the only official economic facts: the 10 November 2026 hotfixes added gold rewards to most quests of level 48 and above, cut the vendor value of Iron Grenades from 5 silver to 1 silver 25 copper and of Minor Arcane Elixir from 25 to 5 copper, and made two ammunition items tradable; the following post deferred the auction house changes to the weekly maintenance of 17 November (official Blizzard source; [Forever hotfixes, post 6](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-hotfixes-november-11-2026/2403040/6); [post 7](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-hotfixes-november-11-2026/2403040/7)). The major tools list Forever support: TradeSkillMaster on CurseForge ([TradeSkillMaster](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/tradeskill-master)), TradeSkillMaster's public data files ([TSM public data](https://tradeskillmaster.com/public-data)), and wowauctions.net with four Forever realms each for the US and EU ([wowauctions.net](https://www.wowauctions.net/)).

A project note follows from the wiki's suffix table. It treats `_Mainline.toc` as a lower-priority family suffix that also matches Forever, lists `_Standard` as the Midnight-only suffix, and gives Midnight's interface as 120105, whereas this project's AGENTS.md describes `_Mainline` as retail with `120000, 120001`. The game-specific `_Forever` and `_Camelot` files take priority on Forever, so nothing breaks there, but the description and the retail interface numbers are worth checking against a live client ([TOC format](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/TOC_format)).

### What rests on unofficial sources only

Everything else about the Forever economy is unofficial, usually from a single source with a commercial interest. Blizzard has published neither a deposit formula nor a commission for the Forever auction house (community site; [Kami Labs](https://kami-labs.fr/en/wow-forever/hotel-des-ventes-wow-forever-depot-1-cuivre/)). The figures in circulation, a 5% faction and 15% neutral cut with deposits of 5%, 20% and 60% of vendor price for 2, 8 and 24 hours and five times that at neutral houses, come from a site that sells Forever gold and boosting and that itself calls the deposit table the Classic rule, not a confirmed Forever rule (community guide, conflict of interest, single source, unverified; [ConquestCapped](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/)). A one-star third-party addon fork also assumes a 5% cut in its crafting line (third-party README, single source, unverified; [OribosExchange-Forever](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZiiMs/OribosExchange-Forever/HEAD/README.md)). The cut is disputed: a player in a bug thread computed proceeds with a 0.9 factor, but that calculation also subtracts a deposit that is refunded on sale, so it is weak evidence for 10%; the same thread shows a 1 silver minimum deposit still charged on client 1.60.1.70938 between 11 and 13 November (player posts, no staff reply; [Blizzard forums bug thread](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/bug-auction-house-deposit-price/2411025)). A reduction of the minimum deposit to 1 copper was scheduled for the 17 November maintenance, and no opened source confirms it is live (community site, written before the maintenance; [Bind on Forever](https://bindonforever.com/articles/wow-forever-auction-house-changes-november-17)).

The structure of the market has the same single source: one auction house per region, ruleset and faction, with the factions connected only through neutral houses and stackable goods sold per unit from the cheapest listings (community guide, gold-selling site, unverified; [ConquestCapped](https://conquestcapped.com/guides/wow-forever/wow-forever-auction-house/)). Because that is unconfirmed, no posting rule for Forever (matching the lowest price against undercutting by a copper) should be designed in; the ordering among equal prices has to be observed. Even the listing durations conflict: the gold-selling guide says 2, 8 and 24 hours, while the wiki's listing of `PostItem` for the forever game type would imply 12, 24 and 48, and that listing is a template entry, not a client test ([PostItem](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.PostItem)). The same caution applies to the API surface: the wiki lists the modern `C_AuctionHouse` functions for the forever game type and does not list `QueryAuctionItems`, yet this project's own Forever notes document a paged `QueryAuctionItems` path, so both APIs need runtime detection, and the 15-minute dump throttle is unverified on Forever.

Price data access is unresolved. A gold-selling site claims Blizzard publishes no price API for Forever and that third-party data come from addon scans; the third-party fork claims Blizzard does publish Forever auction data for four regions; Undermine Exchange's API documentation says retail only while its home page links a Forever section; and TradeSkillMaster lists Forever data without saying where it comes from ([TimeSaver, week 1 prices](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-auction-house-prices-week-1); [OribosExchange-Forever](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZiiMs/OribosExchange-Forever/HEAD/README.md); [Undermine Exchange API](https://undermine.exchange/api.html); [Undermine Exchange](https://undermine.exchange/)). The launch price data share that weakness: between 7 and 12 November, 34 of 46 tracked materials reportedly got cheaper, the median fell 41% and listed units rose 63%, while the lowest-tier materials rose (community source, the publisher sells Forever gold and boosting, data from addon scans of 16 markets, unverified; [TimeSaver, week 1 prices](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-auction-house-prices-week-1)). The pattern matches the supported lifecycle picture (prices fall fast as supply ramps on a fresh economy), but the numbers should not be relied on.

Profession facts follow the same rule. Reported Forever cooldowns (Arcanite about 47 hours, Mooncloth about 95 hours, Salt Shaker about 71 hours) and the per-day profits computed from them are self-reported and unverified, from a publisher that sells gold; the article states it tested nothing in game, uses asking-price medians, and found only 39 to 66 units listed across the five US markets it sampled (community source, conflict of interest, single source; [TimeSaver, Forever cooldowns](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-profession-cooldowns-gold)). Whether Forever supports bids, whether it has a token, what its instance cap is, and whether its disenchant and prospecting tables differ from Classic are not stated by any opened source.

### What to read from the game client instead of assuming

The consequence for the addon is a list of values that must come from the client or from the player's own records. The deposit should be requested per posting from `C_AuctionHouse.CalculateItemDeposit` or `CalculateCommodityDeposit`, both listed for the forever game type ([CalculateItemDeposit](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.CalculateItemDeposit); [CalculateCommodityDeposit](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_AuctionHouse.CalculateCommodityDeposit)). The cut should be a per-house setting (faction and neutral houses differ in every community account) and can be measured from the player's own sale mail by comparing proceeds with the sale price, a method that is the researchers' suggestion. Listing durations, the minimum deposit, whether bids exist and how equal prices are ordered should be taken from what the client's own auction interface offers and shows. Vendor prices should be re-read from `C_Item.GetItemInfo` instead of cached indefinitely, since a hotfix has already changed some ([C_Item.GetItemInfo](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/API_C_Item.GetItemInfo)). Cooldown lengths should be read from the client's recipe data, the scan API and its throttle detected at runtime, and the instance cap left as a setting.

## Source quality

### How the sources were audited

Two independent audits, both dated 20 November 2026, re-checked the sources behind the six research notes without doing new research. The first covered market theory, price dynamics and trading strategies: it checked bibliographic records against Crossref, arXiv, RePEc, NBER and publisher pages, read full texts where they were open, and spot-checked lower-tier sources that carry fee rules, automation rules, Forever claims and numeric thresholds. The second covered crafting, tools and farming: it re-opened every academic and official source, read Blizzard forum threads in a form that shows the poster's staff flag, downloaded every cited source file again and read the constants from it without executing anything, and spot-checked wiki, tool and community pages on which an addon design fact depends. Neither audit found a fabricated or unresolvable reference; all 22 DOIs tested in the first audit resolve to the stated authors, title and journal, and every algorithm constant an addon would copy matched the cited file exactly in the second.

### Main corrections applied

The corrections fall into four groups. Tier labels were changed: Holm & Mäkinen is treated as a low-assurance journal article whose causal reading is untested; Kelly 1956 as a corporate technical journal article; Bouchaud 2009 as an encyclopedia entry and so not used as a finding; Marks and Skuhrovec as an undergraduate and a master's thesis; Scholten et al. as a peer-reviewed conference paper; the NIST handbook as a government reference, not an official game source; and the code citations as what they are: the legacy TSM code is a third-party private-server port of TSM 2.8.3, the public market-value implementation is a third-party backport written from the documentation, the Auctionator storage code is a private-server port of version 9.1.3, and the LootAppraiser Reloaded and GatherMate2 files are first-party source code. Claims were softened: Kosminsky is dated 2010 with data from 2008, is not cited for cross-server arbitrage, is not used to link the 5% gap to the auction cut, and is noted as non-monotonic on stability and fitted through six points; Morrison & Fontenla are not cited as evidence that realm gaps opened at an expansion launch or that players can trade across realms; the 2018 Blizzard deposit post is described as a plan, not a shipped 20% deposit; the newest-first rule at equal prices is attributed to tool documentation, not Blizzard; the hardware-event requirement is restricted to the functions the wiki actually tags; and the instance-cap and layer posts are quoted for the reasons they actually give. Numbers were fixed: the Forever cooldown goods are 39 to 66 units across five sampled US markets, the alleged conflict over neutral deposits is dropped because the opened 2007 guide gives exactly five times the faction rates, the Infinite Dust yield is presented as one of two published values, and CraftSim's double deposit subtraction is flagged. Finally, the 5% cut is restricted to retail, every Forever fee and structure claim is marked as unofficial with its conflict of interest named, and gold-per-hour and gold-per-day figures are labelled self-reported and unverified wherever they appear.

### Important questions with no solid evidence at any tier

The gaps matter as much as the findings. No source at any tier measures weekday or hour-of-day price cycles in WoW, the size of pre-patch run-ups or post-patch decay, the distribution of realised flipping margins, optimal buy thresholds, the probability or time of sale for an auction listing, price impact or slippage in a game auction house, the accuracy of sales inferred from listing snapshots, how posted quantity moves price, the outcome of market resets, a sensible maximum share of wealth per market, the effect of bot bans on item prices, inflation in WoW beyond the token series, or demand elasticity from real game market data. No methodology-backed gold-per-hour measurement exists for any farm. On mechanics, no official source states the Classic-era fee table, the retail commodity deposit formula, the tie-break among equal commodity prices, the Artisan's Consortium cut, or the true multicraft and resourcefulness distributions, and no game-data-derived yield tables exist for current retail destroy skills. For WoW Forever the cut, the deposit formula, the durations, the ordering rule, bid support, the existence of an official price feed, token availability, instance caps and cooldown lengths all lack an official or wiki source.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the problem more than it supplies trading rules. A competitive, cheap-to-enter market with a fee on every sale and a penalty on every failed listing leaves three places where gold accumulates: capacity that others cannot replicate at will, turnover that is known instead of assumed, and patience priced correctly through stale-stock markdowns and position caps. None of those requires a prediction about which weekday is cheap. They require measurement, and the measurements that matter (what sells, how fast, at what net price, at what cost in the player's own time) are exactly the ones no public source provides and an addon with a scan history and a ledger can.

That makes the addon's own data the strongest evidence available to its user, stronger than any guide or threshold cited here, provided the addon is honest about it: sales inferred from listings shown as upper bounds, patterns shown only when the history supports them, farm rates shown as the player's own and split into certain and estimated parts. The same discipline applies to WoW Forever, where the rules are still moving and largely undocumented; an addon that asks the client for the deposit, measures the cut from its own sales and observes how equal prices are filled will be correct on the day the rules change, which is more than any published table can promise.
